### The importance and tedium of note-taking

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:700/1*35RjB8D0XSM6Y4SoZV3nUQ.jpeg)

**I love reading, especially non-fiction, and I often taking notes as I read.** In the internet age, I often read e-books, which means I usually have a split-screen setup: the book (either on a PDF reader or Kindle app) on the left, and my note-taking app (previously Evernote, now Notion) on the right.

**Taking notes is very important to me** — I note down witty quotes, meaningful statistics, and the key points made by the author. While critics would say that I won’t review these notes ever in the future — I actually do! These notes often come in handy at random times, like when I’m trying to convince my friend of a certain position based on what I had read but forgot the exact person or statistic. More importantly, taking notes, as proven by multiple scientific studies, solidifies the my, understanding of the text, as it’s a form of active engagement with the text.

**I know I’m in the minority when it comes to note-taking. It is definitely tedious, so many people avoid it entirely.** Taking notes slows down reading, which often contradicts our desire to read quickly and grasp the main point — especially when there’s a lot of material to cover under tight deadlines. I remember an Urban Studies class in college where the professor assigned 200 pages of reading per week. Taking notes faithfully meant I could only manage about 40 pages per hour, much slower than those who read without taking notes.

However, people aren’t averse to the **purpose of note-taking — which is to record and save memorable content for future use**. How do I know this? We all love taking photos at memorable places and occasions (at a concert, when on a mountain-top, etc.).

And so, I’m willing to bet that **if there were a frictionless way to record down what is memorable from a text**, many would jump at it.

**That is the inspiration for aiRead — making reading and note-taking frictionless.**

**Real learning comes with engagement with the text**. Understanding the style and tone of the author, processing the author’s arguments, and making connections to your existing knowledge and interests. Taking notes is an example of very active engagement with text.

Many people are tempted to use AI summarizers (there are tons of ‘Chat with your PDF’ tools out there).

**But these AI summarizers actually harm your learning, by eliminating your engagement with the text**. When you upload a PDF to an AI summarizer, you often skip reading the text in its raw form. Instead, the chatbot provides a generic summary, filtered through the perspective of a large language model rather than your unique lens, resulting in a monotonous, impersonal summary. You might miss an analogy, quote, or statistic that could resonate deeply with you. Asking follow-up questions to the chatbot returns informative yet dry answers, revealing only small segments of the text. This deprives you of experiencing the full richness of the content, along with any non-linear connections or valuable insights you might have discovered.

**As a result, traditional summarizers leave you disconnected from the context, and your learning experience heavily short-changed.**

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:700/1*S9KXofiviaa5VdOMMTn-lg.jpeg)

**A less tedious, yet effective way of engaging with texts is highlighting**. While reading, I highlight the statistics, key arguments, and quotes that I find important. This maintains engagement, as I decide which excerpts are personally significant, out of the pages of raw text. Sure enough, people recognise the value of highlighting enough to create products to help people to store highlights of different types, from e-book highlights (Readwise), to webpage highlights (Hypothes.is, a Chrome extension I use).

and so, what if we could make note-taking frictionless yet meaningful for your learning?

**Enter aiRead — an integrated PDF reader and notepad interface for you to learn better.**

Upload and read your documents, highlighting as you go. Our notepad has helpful AI features, such as summarizing your highlights, voice dictation and question-answering. **This makes note-taking frictionless, so you can focus on what matters most — reading, engaging and learning.**

**Highlights are the key to how aiRead makes note-taking frictionless while retaining your personal engagement with your reading.** As you read and highlight excerpts in your reading, aiRead learns what you find significant in the reading, and **creates a personalised summary emphasising your highlights**.

Through the notepad, we hope to deliver a seamless note-taking process where the AI collaborates with you. You are in control of your notepad, while the AI synthesizes, summarizes and listens unobtrusively and helpfully as a helpful co-pilot. We believe that a canvas-like interface with AI co-pilot is a superior user interface as compared to chatbots.

**Additionally, aiRead transforms your text into an audio podcast, bullet-form summary, and Q&A flashcards, helping you to augment your learning.**

Text might not always be the preferred way of engaging with content — sometimes you might prefer to listen to a **talk-show** between two people discussing the content, as a lighter way to digest the issues.

Also, while reading deep into a paper, it’s important to have a holistic view of the text, to see how the specific point being made connects to the larger argument the author is advancing. Hence, the **bullet-form summary**, with each bullet jumping to the specific section, will be valuable, enhancing your understanding of your reading.

Other times, consuming the content in a question-and-answer format could be more effective, as it challenges you to think deeper and uncovers hidden assumptions that one could easily gloss over or take for granted.

Which is why we have the **Q&A Flashcard** **mode**. Q&A thinking, which has its roots in Socratic dialectical conversations, helps you engage with the content more actively and critically, and explore multiple perspectives to an issue.

Furthermore, we’re working on a **Quiz mode**! The AI asks you questions, and you answer through voice! Then the AI essentially acts as a helpful tutor, grading your answers, highlighting any gaps or misconceptions you might have with the content, and pointing you to parts of the text to review to gain a fuller grasp of the content.
