# Meet new encoder language models (LLM2Encoder)

The evolution of language models has followed two major paths: generative models (such as GPT, Llama and T5) and discriminative models (like BERT and DeBERTA). While generative, auto-regressive models have garnered significant attention and investments, leading to remarkable advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs), discriminative models seemed to stagnate — until the emergence of works such as GLiNER and UTC.

Generative models excel at producing human-like text and performing a wide range of language tasks. On the other hand, discriminative models, particularly encoder-based architectures, have traditionally been more adept at tasks requiring structured, deterministic outputs and a deep understanding of input text, such as named entity recognition (NER) and text classification.

GLiNER demonstrated that small encoder models could achieve impressive zero-shot performance in NER and, as we’ve shown with our multi-task model, excel in various other tasks including relation extraction, classification, and open information extraction. However, GLiNER’s limitations became apparent: it relies on outdated encoder architectures as its backbone, lacking support for *modern optimizations* like flash-attention. These older models are also constrained by *maximum window sizes* and were trained on relatively *small text corpora*, limiting their overall generalisation capabilities.

Recognizing the strengths and weaknesses of both paradigms, we identified a clear need for convergence. To achieve this synthesis, we leveraged the phenomenal LLM2Vec work to adapt modern decoder architectures into bi-directional encoders. This approach allows us to harness the power of advanced LLMs while retaining the benefits of encoder-based models for specific tasks.

Because our final aims are to adapt decoders for tasks like NER, and relation extraction Q&A, we only trained models on the Maked Next Token prediction task on Wikipedia text corpora. For this research project, we take the LLama line of models, including Sheared LLama and Tiny LLama and the Qwen Line of models including 0.5B and 1.5B models. You can check the collection of such models including raw models and models that use converted decoders to encoders as a backbone.

Then we took converted decoders and used them as backbones of GLiNER and GLiClass architectures. Because of the nature of positional encoding and the attention mechanism of modern decoders, we are required to change the pooling strategy for entity representations. Instead of aggregating information to a special token (<<ENT>>) we used the first token pooling of entity representations. Fine-tuned Qwen 1.5 B-based GLiNER demonstrated promising results, demonstrating the best results on 24 datasets for zero-shot NER benchmarking. At the same time, the Tiny-LLama version of GLiNER demonstrated the best performance in zero-shot setting on biological datasets among all publicly available models.

While it’s hard to compare previous GLiNER models because of different datasets, training parameters and other factors, we can’t say obviously about the scaling properties of GLiNER models. But our recent bi-encoder line of GLiNER models, which provides more efficient and interpretable inference is a good candidate for researching scaling properties. Those models were trained on the same datasets and similar training procedures. We trained 3 DeBERTa-based models and Llama-1.3 B-based GLiNER model. Below, you can see a clear indication of improved performance with a large network. However, it’s also important to say that bi-encoder architecture is more friendly for initial decoder models because it’s not required to have good bi-directional attention to get good entity representations.

### How to use new GLiNER models:

First of all, you need to install the GLiNER and LLM2Vec packages:

`pip install gliner -U`

pip install llm2vec

For the Qwen-based models, you need to be sure that you use the latest version of Transformers:

`pip install transformers -U `

Here is an example of inference using a Llama-based model:

`from gliner import GLiNER`


model = GLiNER.from_pretrained("knowledgator/gliner-llama-1B-v1.0")


text = """

Cristiano Ronaldo dos Santos Aveiro (Portuguese pronunciation: [kɾiʃˈtjɐnu ʁɔˈnaldu]; born 5 February 1985) is a Portuguese professional footballer who plays as a forward for and captains both Saudi Pro League club Al Nassr and the Portugal national team. Widely regarded as one of the greatest players of all time, Ronaldo has won five Ballon d'Or awards,[note 3] a record three UEFA Men's Player of the Year Awards, and four European Golden Shoes, the most by a European player. He has won 33 trophies in his career, including seven league titles, five UEFA Champions Leagues, the UEFA European Championship and the UEFA Nations League. Ronaldo holds the records for most appearances (183), goals (140) and assists (42) in the Champions League, goals in the European Championship (14), international goals (128) and international appearances (205). He is one of the few players to have made over 1,200 professional career appearances, the most by an outfield player, and has scored over 850 official senior career goals for club and country, making him the top goalscorer of all time.

"""


labels = ["person", "award", "date", "competitions", "teams"]


entities = model.predict_entities(text, labels, threshold=0.5)


for entity in entities:

print(entity["text"], "=>", entity["label"])

### GLiClass: efficient zero-shot text classifiers

Additionally, we explore new encoder models as backbones for our zero-shot classification approach — GLiClass. This is an efficient zero-shot classifier inspired by GLiNER work. It demonstrates the same performance as a cross-encoder while being more compute-efficient because classification is done at a single forward path.

We trained both Qwen-based and LLama-based classifiers and compared them with previously developed DeBERTa models. While zero-shot performance was similar in few-shot learning, the new models demonstrated superior performance.

To use new GLiClass models, you need to install the package first:

`pip install gliclass`

Below you can see the inference script as an example:

`from gliclass import GLiClassModel, ZeroShotClassificationPipeline`

from transformers import AutoTokenizer


model = GLiClassModel.from_pretrained("knowledgator/gliclass-qwen-0.5B-v1.0")

tokenizer = AutoTokenizer.from_pretrained("knowledgator/gliclass-qwen-0.5B-v1.0")


pipeline = ZeroShotClassificationPipeline(model, tokenizer, classification_type='multi-label', device='cuda:0')


text = "One day I will see the world!"

labels = ["travel", "dreams", "sport", "science", "politics"]

results = pipeline(text, labels, threshold=0.5)[0] #because we have one text


for result in results:

print(result["label"], "=>", result["score"])

### Conclusion

Modern promising encoder-based architectures relied on old-fashioned models, such as DeBERTa that limited their knowledge, ability to process long texts and lack of support of Flash Attention. By adapting modern decoder architectures into bi-directional encoders, we have successfully combined the strengths of modern LLMs with the task-specific benefits of encoder-based models. The development of GLiNER and GLiClass models, utilizing converted decoders as backbones, has shown promising results in zero-shot and few-shot learning scenarios for tasks such as named entity recognition and classification. These new models, particularly those based on Llama and Qwen architectures, have demonstrated superior performance on various benchmarks while offering improved efficiency through techniques like Flash Attention. Our work shows that with limited fine-tuning (Wikipedia corpus) decoders can be quickly transformed into high-quality encoders, which opens many important use cases such as NER, Q&A, text classification, search results reranking, entity-linking, etc. Encoders provide more interpretable and reliable ways to solve previously mentioned tasks and having the benefits of modern decoders, like FA support and large pre-trained corpus the total benefits and opportunities became obvious. We plan to continue our research and bring much better GLi* models with a longer context window, better multi-task and multi-lingual abilities and even support of multiple modalities.
