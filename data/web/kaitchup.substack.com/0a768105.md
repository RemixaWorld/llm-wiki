In previous articles, we saw how to quantize and fine-tune Qwen3 models.

Qwen3 is available in a range of sizes, from 0.6B to 235B parameters, including two Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) variants. Among these, Qwen3-32B and Qwen3-30B-A3B stand out as models of similar size. The latter is an MoE model that activates only 3B parameters during inference, making it significantly faster than the dense Qwen3-32B, though with a slight trade-off in accuracy on most tasks.

But how significant is this accuracy gap? Should Qwen3-30B-A3B be your default choice for faster inference? And what happens to this performance difference after quantization? While dense models like Qwen3-32B tend to quantize well, MoE models, especially those with very small expert networks like Qwen3-30B-A3B, may be significantly more challenging to quantize.

In this article, we’ll answer these questions. We’ll start by explaining how Qwen3-30B-A3B works. Then, we’ll compare its performance with Qwen3-32B before and after 2-bit and 4-bit quantization. Then, we will look into the actual speedup delivered by the MoE architecture, and how it holds after quantization.

## Qwen3 MoE Explained

The neural architecture of Qwen3 MoE models closely resembles that of the dense Qwen3 models. Both share the same self-attention mechanism, but the MoE variants use significantly fewer attention heads for queries and key/values (Q/KV):

  * **Qwen3-32B:** 64 / 32

  * **Qwen3-30B-A3B:** 32 / 4

This reduction in attention heads makes the MoE model more efficient at computing self-attention. It also results in a smaller KV cache, meaning that long-context inference consumes less memory.

Regarding the expert layers, Qwen3-30B-A3B features 128 experts, with only 8 activated per token per layer during inference. These are relatively small, fine-grained experts, similar to the approach introduced by the DeepSeek models. However, unlike DeepSeek, the Qwen3 MoE models do **not** share experts.

Interestingly, the Qwen team’s technical report does not directly compare Qwen3-30B-A3B with the larger Qwen3-32B dense model. Instead, the MoE results are placed alongside those of the smaller Qwen3-14B, making it harder to assess their relative strengths at a glance.

However, when we compare Qwen3-30B-A3B directly with the Qwen3-32B base model, the dense version consistently outperforms the MoE across all benchmarks.

Next, let’s examine how the models compare after 2-bit and 4-bit quantization.

## Qwen3 MoE Quantized to 2-bit and 4-bit

I've added the quantized Qwen3 MoE model to my collection of quantized Qwen3 variants:

  * [Quantized Qwen3](<https://huggingface.co/collections/kaitchup/quantized-qwen3-68132ed4ca82afb00ba988ea>)

I applied the same quantization approach used for the dense models, but to say it performed poorly would be an understatement.

![image](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!XPlR!,w_2400,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F2f4f9000-f7cb-4fb2-b58a-204839d67d4f_2047x575.png) 

Quantizing the MoE models leads to a significant drop in performance, even at 4-bit precision, whereas the 4-bit quantized version of Qwen3-32B performs nearly identically to its original model.

This discrepancy likely stems from the fact that models with tiny experts are inherently more challenging to quantize. With fewer parameters per module (e.g., per expert), there’s less redundancy to absorb quantization errors.

The quantized versions released by the Qwen team may not suffer from this issue. If I understand correctly, they apply a more fine-grained quantization strategy specifically optimized to the expert layers.

  * [Qwen/Qwen3-30B-A3B-GPTQ-Int4](<https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-30B-A3B-GPTQ-Int4>)

Unfortunately, I was unable to run the Qwen team's quantized MoE model with the Transformers library, so I can only assume it performs better than my own quantized versions. Notably, a 2-bit variant has not been released by the Qwen team.

In summary, I can’t currently recommend using the quantized version of Qwen3-30B-A3B, as the performance degradation is too severe. The larger Qwen3-235B-A11B may be less affected by quantization, thanks to its larger expert layers, which provide more capacity to absorb quantization noise.

## So, When to Use Qwen3 MoE?

Quantized or not, the Qwen3 MoE model significantly underperforms compared to its dense counterpart in terms of overall accuracy. But maybe the model is fast enough to justify this trade-off?

The Qwen team has published [their own benchmarks comparing inference throughput using both SGLang and Transformers](<https://qwen.readthedocs.io/en/latest/getting_started/speed_benchmark.html>).

Below is a summary of the SGLang results:

![image](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!L9hN!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F31eed1f5-6c75-4acc-85b1-271e0683591e_778x308.png) 

The MoE model is significantly faster than the dense model, especially for short input sequences. At an input length of 1, the MoE version is nearly 7× faster. However, this speed advantage diminishes as the sequence length increases, dropping to just 1.9× at the maximum supported length.

Interestingly, the quantized MoE models are actually slower than their non-quantized counterparts, except at maximum sequence length. This further reinforces the conclusion that quantized versions of the Qwen3 MoE models are not a practical choice.

## Conclusion

If you're looking for a high-performing model with faster inference, Qwen3-30B-A3B is a strong option. For moderate sequence lengths, it can be 6× faster than a similarly sized dense model, with only a modest drop in accuracy.

However, if you're constrained by memory, it's better to use quantized versions of the dense models. The quantized Qwen3-30B-A3B model not only delivers significantly lower accuracy, but it's also slower, negating the primary advantage of the MoE architecture.

Keep in mind that while MoE models have fewer _active_ parameters during inference, all parameters still need to be loaded into memory. As a result, they are not inherently more memory-efficient. Offloading experts to CPU RAM is technically possible, but it significantly slows down inference, undermining the key benefit of using an MoE model. It's a trade-off worth considering.
