We have numerous options to compress large language models (LLMs). 4-bit quantization is one of the most popular as it can significantly reduce the size of LLMs while preserving most of their accuracy.

4-bit quantization can be achieved through various methods. In the Kaitchup, I reviewed the most used and best-performing methods to run quantized models on a GPU: AQLM, AWQ, GPTQ, and AutoRound. 

However, it’s always unclear when a new model is released whether a particular quantization method will work better than the others. Moreover, depending on your applications, you might be more interested in a method that produces faster models, more accurate models, or smaller models. 

In this article, I compare AQLM, bitsandbytes, AWQ, GPTQ, and AutoRound for the 4-bit quantization of Llama 3.1. We will see how fast, memory-efficient, and accurate are the models produced by these methods.

## A Brief Overview of AQLM, bitsandbytes, AWQ, GPTQ, and AutoRound

I wrote about all these quantization methods. In this section, I’ll just briefly summarize how they work and point to the article presenting the method.

#### AQLM: Costly but Accurate Quantization Through Additive Quantization

AQLM exploits additive quantization (AQ). Unlike traditional approaches like Post-Training Quantization (PTQ), which compresses weight rows based on known calibration data, AQ focuses on compressing input vectors by grouping weight rows and representing each group with a sum of vectors selected from multiple “codebooks” where a codebook is a learnable set of candidate vectors used to encode data. This process must be trained and involves an iterative optimization method.

Despite the high computational cost associated with AQ, especially in large LLMs, the performance gains achieved through this method justify the expense, especially for low-bit quantization. However, for 4-bit quantization, the accuracy for AQLM models is not expected to be higher than with other popular methods.

#### Bitsandbytes: Accurate and Ideal for QLoRA with NormalFloat4

Bitsandbytes quantizes to 4-bit using the NormalFloat4 (NF4) data type which has been shown superior to the INT4 data type that is used by most other quantization methods. To further reduce the model size, this method also quantizes the quantization constants (double or “nested” quantization).

This method is the most popular choice for QLoRA fine-tuning, i.e., fine-tuning a LoRA adapter on top of a quantized model.

A significant advantage of Bitsandbytes quantization is that it doesn’t perform a calibration step. A model can be quickly quantized at loading time. 

Among the disadvantages, a model quantized with Bitsandbytes can be between 4 and 3 times slower for inference than the original, not quantized, model.

#### GPTQ: Fast for inference but Slightly Less Accurate

GPTQ is the oldest quantization method I’ll use in this article. 

It’s probably the most used quantization method for inference on GPU and benefits from CUDA kernels highly optimized to speed up inference. It also supports the Marlin format which further accelerates inference.

However, as we saw in a previous article, GPTQ is not always accurate. For Llama 3, I observed a significant accuracy drop on downstream tasks once quantized. We will see whether we observe the same for Llama 3.1.

GPTQ needs a calibration step. It’s not as costly as AQLM but still takes 30 minutes on a high-end GPU for an 8B model.

#### AWQ: Accurate and Fast Quantized Models Thanks to an Activation-aware Quantization

Preserving a small percentage (from 0.1% to 1%) of the parameters from quantization, based on their activation levels, can significantly reduce performance loss induced by quantization.

AWQ doesn’t quantize the most “active” parameters. Some will remain 16-bit parameters, while the remainder will be INT4. However, mixing different quantization precisions is hardware-inefficient. To address this, the authors developed a per-channel scaling method that adjusts scaling factors to minimize quantization errors for important parameters. This approach allows for uniform quantization while still preserving the most critical parameters.

AWQ also exploits optimized CUDA kernels for faster inference and supports the Marlin format.

## Quantize Llama 3.1 with AQLM, bitsandbytes, AWQ, GPTQ, and AutoRound

I quantized Llama 3.1 and Llama 3.1 Instruct with bitsandbytes, AWQ, GPTQ, and AutoRound. The models are available in this Hugging Face collection:

  * [Quantized Llama 3.1](<https://huggingface.co/collections/kaitchup/quantized-llama-31-66b9ee6fc689a131a017425c>) (CC-BY 4.0)

In this article, I only evaluate the Instruct version of Llama 3.1 8B.

#### Quantize Llama 3.1 with bitsandbytes

We need to install transformers and bitsandbytes:
    
    
    pip install --upgrade transformers bitsandbytes 

To quantize the model, we must define a BitsandBytesConfig with a compute dtype (i.e., the data type to which the parameters are dequantized for the forward pass). If your GPU is recent, use bfloat16.
    
    
    import torch
    from transformers import AutoTokenizer, AutoModelForCausalLM, BitsAndBytesConfig
    
    if torch.cuda.is_bf16_supported():
      compute_dtype = torch.bfloat16
    else:
      compute_dtype = torch.float16
    
    model_name = "meta-llama/Meta-Llama-3.1-8B"
    quant_path = 'Meta-Llama-3.1-8B-bnb-4bit'
    tokenizer = AutoTokenizer.from_pretrained(model_name)
    bnb_config = BitsAndBytesConfig(
            load_in_4bit=True,
            bnb_4bit_quant_type="nf4",
            bnb_4bit_compute_dtype=compute_dtype,
            bnb_4bit_use_double_quant=True,
    )
    model = AutoModelForCausalLM.from_pretrained(
              model_name, quantization_config=bnb_config
    )
    
    model.save_pretrained("./"+quant_path, safetensors=True)
    tokenizer.save_pretrained("./"+quant_path)

#### Quantize Llama 3.1 with GPTQ

For GPTQ, we will use [AutoGPTQ](<https://github.com/AutoGPTQ/AutoGPTQ>). Datasets and Optimum are also necessary to quantize within Hugging Face Transformers.

_Note: When I wrote this article, AutoGPTQ was still installing an older version of Transformers that doesn’t support Llama 3.1. I recommend upgrading Transformers once AutoGPTQ is installed (the same for AutoAWQ as we will see below)._
    
    
    pip install --upgrade auto-gptq accelerate datasets optimum
    pip install --upgrade transformers 
    
    
    from transformers import AutoModelForCausalLM, AutoTokenizer
    from optimum.gptq import GPTQQuantizer
    import torch
    model_path = 'meta-llama/Meta-Llama-3.1-8B'
    w = 4 #quantization to 4-bit. Change to 2, 3, or 8 to quantize with another precision
    
    quant_path = 'Meta-Llama-3.1-8B-gptq-'+str(w)+'bit'
    
    # Load model and tokenizer
    tokenizer = AutoTokenizer.from_pretrained(model_path, use_fast=True)
    model = AutoModelForCausalLM.from_pretrained(model_path, torch_dtype=torch.float16, device_map="auto")
    quantizer = GPTQQuantizer(bits=w, dataset="c4", model_seqlen = 2048)
    quantized_model = quantizer.quantize_model(model, tokenizer)
    
    quantized_model.save_pretrained("./"+quant_path, safetensors=True)
    tokenizer.save_pretrained("./"+quant_path)

#### Quantize Llama 3.1 with AWQ

For AWQ, we will use [AutoAWQ](<https://github.com/casper-hansen/AutoAWQ>).
    
    
    pip install --upgrade autoawq optimum accelerate
    pip install --upgrade transformers
    
    
    from awq import AutoAWQForCausalLM
    from transformers import AutoTokenizer
    
    model_path = 'meta-llama/Meta-Llama-3.1-8B'
    quant_path = 'Meta-Llama-3.1-8B-awq-4bit'
    quant_config = { "zero_point": True, "q_group_size": 128, "w_bit": 4, "version": "GEMM" }
    
    # Load model and tokenizer
    model = AutoAWQForCausalLM.from_pretrained(model_path, safetensors=True, device_map='cuda')
    tokenizer = AutoTokenizer.from_pretrained(model_path, use_fast=True)
    
    # Quantize
    model.quantize(tokenizer, quant_config=quant_config)
    
    # Save quantized model with safetensors
    model.save_quantized("./"+quant_path, safetensors=True)
    tokenizer.save_pretrained("./"+quant_path)

#### Quantize Llama 3.1 with AutoRound

We will use Intel’s [AutoRound](<https://github.com/intel/auto-round/tree/main>):
    
    
    pip install --upgrade transformers auto-round

I quantized with asymmetric and symmetric quantizations. Marlin only supports symmetric quantization.
    
    
    from transformers import AutoModelForCausalLM, AutoTokenizer
    import torch
    model_name = "meta-llama/Meta-Llama-3.1-8B"
    model = AutoModelForCausalLM.from_pretrained(model_name, torch_dtype=torch.float16)
    tokenizer = AutoTokenizer.from_pretrained(model_name)
    
    from auto_round import AutoRound
    
    bits, group_size, sym = 4, 128, True
    autoround = AutoRound(model, tokenizer, bits=bits, group_size=group_size, batch_size=2, seqlen=512, sym=sym, gradient_accumulate_steps=4, device='cuda')
    autoround.quantize()
    output_dir = "./AutoRound/GPTQ-sym/"
    autoround.save_quantized(output_dir)

_Note: The 4-bit models are not 4 times smaller than the 16-bit (mainly) because these methods don’t quantize the token embeddings and the language modeling head._

#### Accuracy 

I benchmarked the model on Arc Challenge and MMLU to observe whether the models were significantly degraded after quantization.

![image](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!P8hu!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F3ec2b3f5-a8e0-4672-89a8-3b34d5dcf65c_1200x1366.png) 

In a previous article, we saw that Llama 3 8B doesn’t support well GPTQ quantization, with a drop of 10 points of accuracy on Arc Challenge:

This doesn’t seem to be the case for Llama 3.1. Even though GPTQ performs at slightly lower accuracy than AutoRound, AWQ, and Bitsandbytes, the difference is negligible.

Overall, Llama 3.1 seems very robust to quantization. Moreover, despite a quantization to a lower precision, AQLM produces a model that only slightly underperforms the 4-bit models.

## Conclusion

Llama 3.1 8B is very robust to 4-bit quantization. 

_Which quantization methods should you use?_

It depends on what you want to do. It seems that there are no good reasons to choose AutoRound (asym) and GPTQ. We can forget them for Llama 3.1 8B.

AutoRound (sym) and AWQ perform the same: similar accuracy, same size, and same inference speed. Both are good choices.

Bitsandbytes yields significantly slower models. Outside of the context of QLoRA fine-tuning, I can’t see a good reason to use it.

AQLM is impressive. Even though it has been quantized to a lower precision, it could maintain a reasonably good accuracy. However, it lacks a good inference throughput. If you have enough memory for the 4-bit models, this AQLM model won’t be a good option.

_What about lower precision quantization?_

We could also consider quantization to 3-bit and 2-bit precision. AutoRound and GPTQ support it, but not bitsandbytes and AWQ. However, for 8B parameter models, rather than quantizing the model to lower precision, I recommend trying smaller models first. For instance, Gemma 2 9B quantized to 2-bit might underperform Gemma 2 2B in your applications.

For larger models, e.g. 70B+ parameters, 2-bit quantization is more accurate. We will confirm it in another article.
