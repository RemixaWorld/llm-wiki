In December 2024, Microsoft released Phi-4, a powerful 14B parameter model. While impressive, models of this size are difficult to run or fine-tune on consumer hardware.

To address this, Microsoft introduced Phi-4 Mini, a smaller 3.8B parameter version. It can be fine-tuned on a 24GB GPU and runs smoothly on a 12GB GPU. Alongside Phi-4 Mini, Microsoft also released Phi-4 Multimodal, which is capable of processing audio, visual, and text inputs, a rare feature among open models.

Microsoft achieved this by integrating and fine-tuning multimodal LoRA adapters on top of Phi-4 Mini, adding only 1.73B additional parameters.

In this article, we'll explore how Microsoft developed this multimodal LoRA model. We'll also evaluate Phi-4 Mini's performance and examine its efficiency after 8-bit, 4-bit, 3-bit, and 2-bit quantization. The model can be accurately quantized to 4-bit, which makes it a 3 GB model!

### Phi-4 Mini Architecture

The Phi-4-Mini series consists of two models: Phi-4-Mini, a language model, and Phi-4-Multimodal, which supports language, vision, and audio modalities. 

Both models use the o200k tokenizer, based on [tiktoken](<https://github.com/openai/tiktoken>), with a vocabulary size of 200,064 and are built on a decoder-only Transformer architecture with a 128K context window using LongRoPE. _Note: This is very short given the original promises of LongRoPE, which is to enable context with millions of tokens._

The language model features 32 Transformer layers, each with Group Query Attention (GQA), which optimizes key-value memory usage with 24 query heads and 8 key-value heads. 

![source](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!W8Rd!,w_2400,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F783eed0f-23a8-4269-b801-ac26ef711faf_1681x853.png) 

Multimodal fine-tuning often degrades language model performance, but Phi-4-Multimodal avoids this by using a mixture of Low-Rank Adapters (LoRAs), where different LoRAs handle different modality interactions. This allows the model to support a broad range of tasks, including vision-language and speech-related applications, without altering the base language model. The trade-off could be that the model is not learning as well how to use these new modalities. In a way, this is similar to what Meta did with Llama 3.2 Vision: They had frozen the LLM and added a vision module on top of it. The result was not great compared to other vision LLMs released then, e.g., MOLMo and Qwen2-VL.

For vision tasks, Phi-4-Multimodal integrates an image encoder based on SigLIP-400M, fine-tuned with LLM2CLIP on high-resolution image-text pairs. A 2-layer MLP projector aligns vision features with text embeddings. LoRA is added only during supervised fine-tuning (SFT) to maintain efficiency. The vision system introduces approximately 810M additional parameters. To handle various image resolutions, a dynamic multi-crop strategy determines the optimal image slicing based on aspect ratios, reducing the need for excessive resizing.

Speech and audio processing in Phi-4-Multimodal relies on an 80-dimensional log-Mel spectrogram input with a 10ms frame rate. The model incorporates a pre-trained audio encoder connected to Phi-4-Mini via an audio adapter. The encoder consists of 3 convolutional layers and 24 conformer blocks, providing an attention dimension of 1024 and a feed-forward dimension of 1536. A 2-layer MLP audio projector maps 1024-dimensional speech features to the language model’s 3072-dimensional embedding space. LoRA is also applied to the language decoder for improved speech/audio performance without degrading text capabilities. The audio processing components introduce 920M additional parameters, with a speech token rate of 80ms, equating to roughly 750 tokens per minute of audio.

### Training

The training pipeline for Phi-4-Multimodal consists of separate stages for vision, speech/audio, and vision-speech joint training. 

_Note: The training process involves many components. Refer to the figure in the previous section to check where a particular component is in the architecture of the model._

Vision training follows a structured four-stage approach: 

  1. The projector is aligned with text embeddings while keeping the vision encoder frozen. 

  2. Both the projector and vision encoder are jointly trained on a broad dataset to improve OCR and dense understanding capabilities. 

  3. Introduction of LoRA to the language decoder, allowing generative vision-language training on single-frame SFT data. 

  4. Multi-frame training extends the model’s temporal understanding and supports longer context lengths, up to 64K tokens. 

Speech/audio training is a two-stage process: 

  1. Pre-training on large-scale ASR data to align the audio encoder with the language model while keeping the decoder frozen. 

  2. Post-training on 100M weighted SFT samples, enabling broader speech and audio capabilities. The model supports long-form audio processing, theoretically handling up to 2.8 hours of speech, though practical fine-tuning is needed for such extended use cases.

Vision-speech joint training occurs after both vision and speech post-training, during which the language model, audio encoder, and audio projector are frozen, while the vision adapter, vision encoder, and vision projector are fine-tuned. 

Training relies on a mixture of vision-speech SFT data alongside language and vision post-training datasets to maintain performance across modalities. 

For reasoning capabilities, the Phi-4-Mini model undergoes a three-phase training approach: 

  1. Pre-training on 60B reasoning tokens derived from advanced LLMs, using rejection sampling to refine outputs. 

  2. Fine-tuning on a curated dataset of 200K high-quality chain-of-thought (CoT) samples covering various domains and complexities. 

  3. DPO training with 300K labeled preference samples, improving reasoning accuracy by learning from corrected outputs.

**No GRPO! No reinforcement learning!**

### Datasets

The training data for Phi-4-Mini has been improved over its predecessor, [Phi-3.5-Mini](<https://kaitchup.substack.com/p/fine-tuning-phi-35-moe-and-mini-on>), through better filtering, enhanced math and coding datasets, and optimized data mixtures. 

The pre-training corpus consists of 5 trillion tokens, with an emphasis on high-quality, diverse content. Post-training data includes expanded function-calling and summarization examples, along with instruction-following datasets to improve model usability. Coding data is enriched with code completion tasks that require understanding and inserting code into existing snippets. The reasoning training dataset is supplemented with synthetic CoT data, where incorrect generations are filtered through both rule-based and model-based approaches to enhance logical consistency.

Vision-language training data incorporates interleaved image-text documents, OCR-processed PDFs, and datasets for chart comprehension, totaling 0.5T tokens during pre-training. Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) expands on this with multimodal instruction-tuning datasets covering diverse domains such as diagram reasoning and PowerPoint analysis. Vision-speech training data is generated by converting selected text queries into speech using in-house text-to-speech (TTS) systems. Speech/audio training includes both pre-training and post-training datasets, covering ASR, speech translation, question answering, summarization, and audio understanding. Post-training data combines real and synthetic speech, formatted for structured model input. Overall, the training pipeline ensures robust multimodal capabilities while preserving the language model’s core strengths.

## How Well Do They Really Perform?

Once again, Microsoft's reported results on public benchmarks seem almost too good to be true, especially for the multimodal model. In practice, Phi models often fall short of their benchmark scores, yet they remain excellent models overall.

Don't trust overfitted benchmarks blindly. Run the models yourself and see how they perform in your target applications.

## Conclusion

Phi-4 Mini is a nice addition to the Phi family. It has some reasoning capabilities but for a 3.8B model, don’t expect much. Its multimodal version was certainly not expected. We already had a Phi vision model before, but the addition of audio makes this iteration stand out.
