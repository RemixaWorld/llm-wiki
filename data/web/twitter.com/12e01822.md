# 简要的MOE综述-预训练MOE、再升级MOE、混搭MOE

Apropos of nothing, here is a mini-tutorial on three types of Mixture of Experts (MoE): Pre-trained MoE, upcycled MoEs, and FrankenMoEs.

#### MoE refresher
MoEs replace the feed-forward layers with sparse MoE layers. These layers contain a certain number of experts (e.g. 8), each one being a neural network (usually a FFN). Then, a router/gate network is in charge of picking which experts to use.

 MoEs are
- Much faster to pretrain,
- Have faster inference in terms of # of activated parameters,
- But still require high VRAM if you want all experts to be loaded in memory

In any case, in terms of **activate** parameters, they tend to outperform dense models with same # of params.

Examples of MoEs: Switch Transformers, Mixtral, DBRX, Jamba DeepSeekMoE and many others.

#### Pre-trained MoEs
Given a fixed compute budget, MoEs theoretically achieve the same quality as their dense counterpart much faster. This has led companies to explore MoE pre-training.

MoE experts **do not** specialize in a topic. They specialize in shallow concepts or groups of tokens. This is a frequent misconception from the community.

Good intro to MoEs: https://huggingface.co/blog/moe

#### FrankenMoE
FrankenMoE is inspired by the big wave of model merges and takes that to MoEs. The idea is to pick several fine-tuned models with the same architecture, and build a MoE out of it. 

How does it work? let's say you have a very good code fine-tune and a very good chat fine-tune. Then, thanks to a bit of further training (which makes the router learn to which expert to send each token), the tokens are re-routed to the corresponding expert. Note that here we have topic/task experts, which is VERY different than the goal of pretrained MoEs.

I'm quite skeptical of this technique as you lose many of the advantages of MoEs (no more load balancing), but results such as https://huggingface.co/mlabonne/Beyonder-4x7B-v2 seem to be quite good so far.

#### Upcycled Fine-grained MoEs
The idea of upcycled MOEs is to pick up an (already trained) base model, such as a small Qwen model, and replicate the FFN multiple times to create multiple experts. 

You can even use **fine-grained experts**, an idea introduced by DeepSeek-MoE, which means cutting the FFNs into smaller ones. 

This can result in a very high number of small experts (even hundreds of experts), and then you can control how many experts you want to be activated. Once this model is initialized, you can then keep pre-training, which should require much less compute to achieve high quality.

I recommend Upstage SOLAR, DeepSeek Moe, and an incoming report to learn more about this!
