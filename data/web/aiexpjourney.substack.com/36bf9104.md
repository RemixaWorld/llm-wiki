If you’ve ever worked on RAG systems dealing with different modalities—or even just thought about the idea, today’s article might spark some useful insights.

## Why Traditional RAG Setups Are Hitting Their Limits

#### The problem

Many so-called “knowledge engines” still rely heavily on vector similarity and keyword matching. That works fine for short, generic queries, but once you introduce long documents, multimodal content, or domain-specific knowledge, accuracy starts to suffer. Most of these systems are also built for individuals or small-scale document uploads, which makes them hard to scale or adapt across different modalities. If we want to solve high-precision tasks, we need something deeper than just plugging a vector DB into a model.

#### What we actually need

To push RAG to the next level, we need to unify vector search, knowledge graphs, full-text indexing, and structured databases into a single retrieval surface. 

This setup should be capable of dynamically routing queries and fusing evidence from multiple sources, so we’re not just improving recall and accuracy, but also maintaining contextual consistency throughout the response.

## HetaRAG: A Blueprint for Hybrid and Deep Retrieval

HetaRAG is a hybrid, deep-retrieval RAG system designed to go beyond simple vector search. It brings together multiple modalities and storage types, allowing documents, tables, formulas, and more to be processed and queried through a unified retrieval surface. When a question comes in, HetaRAG routes it through multiple retrieval channels in parallel, then re-ranks and summarizes the results into a coherent answer or a full research-style report.

#### How it processes data

The system starts with Docling ([AI Innovations and Trends 03: LightRAG, Docling, DRIFT, and More](<https://aiexpjourney.substack.com/p/ai-innovations-and-trends-03-lightrag>)) and MinerU ([From Big Picture to Details: MinerU 2.5 Redefines Document Parsing — AI Innovations and Insights 77](<https://aiexpjourney.substack.com/p/from-big-picture-to-details-mineru>)) two batch parsers that convert messy, unstructured files into structured or semi-structured data. Once parsed, the content is standardized and embedded using models like BGE-m3 for text and QwenVL for multimodal inputs.

#### How it stores and searches

HetaRAG integrates several backends: Elasticsearch for full-text search, Milvus for vector retrieval, MySQL for structured queries, and Neo4j for relational reasoning. Each plays a specialized role, but together they form a cohesive backend for knowledge-intensive tasks.

#### How it builds knowledge

To enhance reasoning, HetaRAG explicitly supports knowledge graph construction using two distinct methods, HiRAG and LeanRAG. 

These approaches extract entity-relation triples from text and build hierarchical, multi-level knowledge graphs, enabling more effective reasoning and retrieval across different levels of semantic granularity.

#### How it retrieves

The hybrid retrieval module blends keyword and vector search using a tunable alpha parameter. On top of that, the system supports flexible re-ranking strategies and multiple deployment options, depending on the use case.

#### How it thinks deeper

The DeepSearch module builds on ideas from WebWalker ([AI Innovations and Insights 28: CUE-M and WebWalker](<https://aiexpjourney.substack.com/p/ai-innovations-and-insights-28-cue>)), enabling multi-hop retrieval and iterative reasoning. It’s built for navigating complex question chains where single-pass lookup just isn’t enough.

#### How it writes

DeepWriter is HetaRAG’s report generation component, which leverages data processed in an offline stage to produce long-form, evidence-grounded answers online. It integrates multimodal content, organizes it through a multi-phase pipeline (from query processing to citation generation), and ensures every claim is traceable to its source.

## A Closer Look: From Parsing to Storage to Retrieval and Generation

#### Parsing and Embeddings: Preparing the Ground for Unified Search

Everything starts with clean, structured data. HetaRAG uses two parsing pipelines: **Docling** , which is ideal for documents with regular layout and structure, and **MinerU** , which keeps the original formatting intact while extracting tables, images, and metadata from text blocks.

Once the content is parsed, it goes through an embedding and standardization step. Text is embedded using **BGE-m3** , while multimodal inputs go through **QwenVL**. All outputs are normalized into a shared schema of unified fields, laying the foundation for high-quality cross-modal retrieval.

#### Cross-Store Modeling: Giving Each Type of Evidence Its Place

HetaRAG connects multiple types of storage engines, each tuned for a specific kind of retrieval:

  * **Elasticsearch** handles full-text indexing and keyword search with speed and scale.

  * **Milvus** powers high-dimensional vector search and efficient collection management.

  * **MySQL** covers structured queries, joins, and complex aggregations.

  * **Neo4j** adds the ability to reason over entities and relationships, enabling graph-based queries.

Together, they form a flexible retrieval backbone that adapts to different data types and query goals.

#### Knowledge Graphs: Two Paths, Two Philosophies

HetaRAG supports two complementary approaches to graph-based reasoning:

  * **HiRAG** focuses on hierarchical aggregation, enabling inference across different semantic levels. ([How HiRAG Turns Data Chaos into Structured Knowledge Magic — AI Innovations and Insights 35](<https://aiexpjourney.substack.com/p/how-hirag-turns-data-chaos-into-structured>))

  * **LeanRAG** organizes rich entity–relation triples into multi-level clusters for lightweight graph querying.

The graph pipeline follows a three-stage abstraction: extract triples, build the graph, and query it. During extraction, users can choose between CommonKG for broader coverage or GraphRAG for more targeted use cases.

#### Advanced Retrieval: Two Tools for Smarter Search

To balance semantic breadth and precision, HetaRAG uses a hybrid retrieval method. An alpha parameter lets you fine-tune the blend between vector and keyword channels, adjusting how much weight each side gets in the final ranking.

On top of that, it supports flexible reranking with models that can run locally via vLLM, load from Hugging Face, or connect via API. The reranking mechanism is further enhanced by its support for parent document retrieval, which provides broader context to the retrieved chunks, thereby improving the accuracy and relevance of the final results.

It works by looping through a sequence of rewrite, retrieve, extract, and assess steps, repeating until the evidence feels solid enough to support an answer.

This agent can tap into both pre-built knowledge bases and live web pages, making it great for multi-source, cross-domain reasoning.

#### DeepWriter: Long-Form Writing, Grounded in Real Evidence

Once everything has been retrieved and organized, DeepWriter takes over. It is HetaRAG’s report generation component, which operates in an online stage using data prepared during an offline processing phase. The process moves in stages, coordinating retrieval, paragraph-level generation, source attribution, and multimodal figure placement.

The pipeline relies on several core tools: MinerU for parsing, Milvus for storage, GME for embedding generation, and Qwen2-7B as the main language model behind the writing.

## Evaluation

#### RAG Challenge: Reranking Matters More Than You Think — GPT-4o Hits 117.0

![Figure 4: RAG-Challenge Result. [Source].](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!ookE!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F8952f6fa-9b2b-4298-812d-1f37a9cefb0b_1504x574.png) 

To evaluate performance, the Enterprise RAG Challenge Round 2 dataset and official scoring protocol are used. The metrics include Retrieval (R), Generation (G), and a combined score calculated as `R / 3 + G`, with a maximum of 133 points.

The results indicate that rerankers contribute more substantially to performance gains than query rewriting does.

For GPT-4o, all reranking strategies improved the score. For Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct, some rerankers, such as bge-reranker-v2-gemma, improved performance, while others led to a slight decrease.

GPT-4o paired with `bge-reranker-large` reached the top score of 117.0. Meanwhile, Qwen2.5-72B combined with bge-reranker-v2-gemma achieved its best score of 109.8, an improvement of 1.2 points over its baseline.

#### DeepSearch: Iterate Until the Answer Holds

![Figure 5: The Example of DeepSearch. [Source].](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!rzlR!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Ff0e0216c-8811-45bd-85c9-f7a8b7d6e90a_1498x866.png) 

DeepSearch takes a different approach. Instead of answering the query in one shot, it breaks it down into smaller sub-questions, then gradually builds up the answer by pulling in supporting evidence from multiple documents.

Each step updates its internal memory, so the system doesn’t forget what it has already found. It keeps going until the answer feels complete enough — not just plausible, but well-supported.

## Thoughts

At its core, HetaRAG’s real contribution is an ambitious architectural vision — a **federated retrieval framework for heterogeneous data**. 

Instead of treating vector stores, knowledge graphs, full-text indexes, and relational databases as disconnected silos, HetaRAG brings them together into one unified search layer. 

Think of it like this: HetaRAG sets up a courtroom, invites witnesses from all walks of data — vectors, graphs, SQL tables, and text indices — and lets them all testify.

But the judge, the one who weighs all the evidence and decides what matters, is still fairly simple for now. That role is handled by reranking plus summarization, but the reranking framework is pluggable rather than fixed.

In my view, the real challenge, and also the system’s greatest potential, lies in designing a **Intelligent Evidence Arbitrator** — one that knows how to balance soft semantic signals from the vector space with hard factual hits from structured sources. How well we design that decision-maker sets the ceiling for how intelligent the entire system can become.
