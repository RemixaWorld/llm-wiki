Around a year ago, in November 2023, I created [my GPT](<https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpts/>) called “[What would Hubi say?](<https://chatgpt.com/g/g-UBVvjY4BT-what-would-hubi-say>)”.

By “Hubi” nickname, I was referring to Professor [Andrew Huberman](<https://g.co/kgs/ntixHxy>), because I have been a big fan of [his podcast](<https://www.hubermanlab.com/podcast>) for over two years now.

Unfortunately, as you can see from the photo below (focus on the “5 conversations” part), my GPT was not very popular. Truth be told, even I didn’t use it. If I recall correctly, back then GPT didn’t have up-to-date memory or could search the web and summarize the most recent podcasts.

No big deal for me.

I was still religiously listening to “Hubi’s” podcast every week. Okay, with a speed factor of 1.25x (or more) on YouTube, because I rarely find 2+ hours of spare time.

However, the idea of being able to summarize [Andrew Huberman’s](<https://www.hubermanlab.com/>) science-based tools quickly, which I quote, “[ _help listeners live a healthier, more fulfilling life_ ,](<https://www.hubermanlab.com/#:~:text=help%20listeners%20live%20a%20healthier%2C%20more%20fulfilling%C2%A0life>)” stuck with me the whole time.

Then recently my LinkedIn network exploded, and people were resharing their posts on [Google’s NotebookLM](<https://notebooklm.google/>). I watched my feed and thought, “ _How did I miss this?_ ”

If you, like me, missed following up on this not-so-new Google tool anymore ([introduced in July 2023](<https://blog.google/technology/ai/notebooklm-google-ai/>)), here’s a quick summary [1, 2, 3]:

* NotebookLM is an experimental AI research assistant, powered by [Gemini 1.5 Pro](<https://blog.google/technology/ai/google-gemini-next-generation-model-february-2024/>), designed to help you interact with and learn from provided information.
* By uploading your notes and sources (documents, articles, YouTube videos), you can “[ground](<https://cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/grounding/overview>)” the language model behind it.
* Once the NootebookLM has the provided sources, it enables you to ask questions, summarize the sources, brainstorm on concepts from the provided material, generate FAQs or study guides, and create an audio overview.

This last feature, an [audio overview](<https://blog.google/technology/ai/notebooklm-audio-overviews/>), is the main reason why my LinkedIn network, and consequently I, got engaged with the product recently.

So, I spent some time testing the tool on — you guessed it — my favourite “Hubi” podcast episodes.

## How I extracted knowledge from Andrew Huberman’s podcast using Google’s NotebookLM

Before I start, I need to share with you the following disclaimers:

* Google placed the following note within the tool to create awareness of the generated content [2]: “ _NotebookLM may still sometimes give inaccurate responses, so you may want to confirm any facts independently._ ”
* For the use case of this post, I didn’t evaluate the full correctness of the generated outcomes (insights) and re-listen to the podcasts. I focused on a high-level outcome analysis and the key tool’s possibilities that resulted in quick knowledge extraction.
* If you have expertise in the neuroscience or psychology field and notice inaccurate responses generated by NotebookLM in the text below, please comment on them and share your feedback.

Now, let’s present the methodological approach to how I tested the tool’s features on my favourite Huberman episodes.

> **What I did to extract the knowledge from the podcasts using NotebookLM:**

**#1 | 👉🏼 First** ,**** I created a new notebook called _“What Would Hubi Say?”_ in **my**[**NotebookLM**](<https://notebooklm.google/>)**environment.**

**#3 | 👉🏼 After providing inputs,** I used NotebookLM to create**my notes** with its predefined **“Key Topics” prompts** and **summarize key insights per auto-detected topic** of the selected YouTube video.

This was a **loop step** where I selected **two to three auto-detected topics** per video and saved the auto-generated output in a **notes format**. In total, I created **20 individual notes from 7 videos**.

**#4 | 👉🏼 Lastly,** I followed a**structured sub-step approach** to get my final outputs.

**_Substep 1:_ **I merged the individual notes into **one combined note**.

**_Substep 2:_ **I selected the text from the **combined note** and saved it to my local **text editor** to create a **PDF file**.

After that, I uploaded this **PDF file back to** my NotebookLM as **an additional data source**.

* The PDF file that contains **a summary of all 20 individual auto-generated notes** from the videos is available in my GitHub repo 👉🏼 [**_here_**](<https://github.com/CassandraOfTroy/what-would-hubi-say/blob/6570adf58b103d807a4683d4a2162df768417703/1_Combine_notebook_of_podcast_summary_What_would_Huby_say.pdf>)** _._**

**_Substep 3_ : **I produced **two outputs** using what I named the **“one click, one prompt, zero-code”** approach in NotebookLM:

* **✨ __ Output 1 [created with one prompt]:__ The protocols** on different topics such as goal setting, motivation, time management, learning and studying, procrastination, mental well-being, and emotional intelligence.
* **✨ Output 2 [created with one click]: An audio overview** (a completely new podcast) covering key topics derived from the original Huberman episodes.

**Now I can share what I liked and didn't like about the auto-generated “protocols”:**

* **👍🏼 Structured outputs:** I liked how the generated output is well-organized with numbered sections, making it easy to read, understand, and quickly search through.
* **👍🏼 Actionable outputs:** The output was full of instructions that were nicely explained and focused on concrete actions.
* **👍🏼 Explanation of the key concepts:** The explanation of the biological and scientific terms added credibility to the content.
* **👎🏼 Oversimplification and dense information:** Although practical steps are provided, key terms are oversimplified. If someone only takes the summary of the key terms without listening to the full podcast or having prior knowledge, it makes them challenging to understand.
* **👎🏼 Lack of references (links) to original data sources (videos):** The NotebookLM doesn’t yet provide links or references to the uploaded sources once a note (e.g., a summary of the key topic) is created.
* **👎🏼 No chat (prompt) history:** If you chat with your combined note source and create the new outputs, the chat history is not saved. Hence, you can’t trace retroactively which output was created by which prompt.

However, as a former researcher, I need to share my **overall feedback** and impression of the tool:

* I think that NotebookLM-like tools will create a real difference in **academia** and **research**(if this didn’t happen already). I personally couldn’t believe how **easy it is to use** and **extract knowledge** from the different types of sources (video or text). Regardless of the current beta issues (and the persistent LLM ones, such as hallucinations), the speed of knowledge extraction **will boost the productivity** of many people and **change how we learn, acknowledge, and create new information**. For better or worse.

Except for the quick textual knowledge extraction, what amazed me was an **audio overview feature, i.e.,** a feature that enables you to create **audio summaries** from your sources.

### Output 2: An audio overview of the protocols [one click, zero code]

**Imagine this:** a completely **new podcast** derived from my favourite Huberman episodes — without me needing to write a single line of code.

**One click** in NotebookLM was needed for me **to create** a short **audio summary** of Huberman’s protocols in the native English language:

An audio overview named “What would Hubi say?” [Generated by Google’s NotebookLM]

And, from a personal view, the outcome was again above my expectations.

Does it still lack depth and smooth transition? Does it mix key takeaways from different key topics? Is it overly enthusiastic and full of expressions like “ _actually, oh wow, yeah, awesome, right, …_ ”?

**— _Yes on all_ ,** but for someone like me, who is **not a native English speaker** and wants to (possibly) **create video content** from her writing, this feature is a pretty sweet deal. On top of this, it enables me to generate **audio summaries of long podcasts** in a condensed manner, which I can consume when I am “on the go.”

That said, to end this post, I am genuinely asking myself: “ _What would Hubi say about this?_ ”
