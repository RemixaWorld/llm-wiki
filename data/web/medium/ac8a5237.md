# ‘Transfusion’ Is Supercharging Training Multi-Modal LLMs Like Never Before

## A deep dive into how a novel approach called ‘Transfusion’ enables training a single Transformer model using both text and images, resulting in superior performance compared to traditional multi-modal LLMs.

Multimodal LLMs are gaining popularity.

Give them text, images, audio or video, and they will work with it all.

Conventional multimodal LLMs consist of separate, modality-specific architectures trained differently for different data types.

The [Next token prediction objective](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08774>) is typically used for discrete data such as text and code, while [Diffusion](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.11239>) is used for continuous data such as images, audio, and video.

_How about combining them both to train a single model?_

Researchers at Meta, Waymo, and the University of Southern California, in their [recent preprint in ArXiv](<https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2408.11039>), have made this possible.

They introduce a technique called **Transfusion** that combines the next token prediction objective with diffusion to train a single Transformer over mixed-modality data.

They show that scaling this method to 7 billion parameters results in a model that can generate text and images at par with similar-scale diffusion and language models.

## But First, What Is Next Token Prediction?

Next token prediction is an approach taken by an LLM in which it tries to guess which word (or part of the word) or token will come next in a sequence of words/ tokens.

To achieve this, it considers the words in its training data and learns to predict the next word.

More technically, given a sequence of words/tokens from a fixed vocabulary, the LLM predicts the probability of the next token based on the preceding tokens in the sequence.

It is trained using an [Autoregressive classification approach](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autoregressive_model>) where it learns to predict the next token using a probability distribution that is optimized by minimizing the [Cross-entropy](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-entropy>) between the predicted and actual data distributions.

This loss (conventionally called LM or Language Model loss) is represented as —

After training, the model [samples its learned probability distribution to generate the next token](<https://prvnsmpth.github.io/animated-transformer/>) based on the [Temperature and Top-p truncation](<https://renepersau.substack.com/p/understanding-temperature-top-p-and>).

## Next, What Is Diffusion?

The [Diffusion process in Machine Learning](</stable-signature-metas-solution-to-end-misinformation-spread-with-ai-generated-images-abe4ecfd5aec#b203>) is based on the physical process of [**Diffusion**](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffusion>), in which particles present in areas of their higher concentration move to areas of their lower concentration, eventually dispersing evenly in an equilibrium state.

Press enter or click to view image in full size

Diffusion of purple dye in water (Source: Image by [BruceBlaus](<https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/User:BruceBlaus>) on [Wikimedia Commons](<https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Blausen_0315_Diffusion.png>))

[**Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs)**](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.11239>)**** use this concept for training.

Unlike language models that work with discrete data, such as text tokens, DDPMs operate over continuous data vectors, like images.

In Diffusion, non-discrete continuous training data (e.g. images) is gradually corrupted by adding Gaussian noise step by step (_Forward process_).

A pre-defined noise schedule, such as the [Cosine scheduler](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09672>), parameterizes (`β`**)** this process and determines how much noise is added at each step.

The model is then trained to reverse this process by learning how to remove this noise and revert to the original data (_Reverse process_).

The overall model’s training objective is to minimize the difference between the predicted and the actual noise added at each time step using a loss function such as the [Mean Squared error](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean_squared_error>).

Once trained, generating new data involves beginning with random noise and iteratively denoising it to create high-quality, meaningful samples.

A technique called [Classifier-Free Diffusion Guidance](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.12598>) conditions the model's image generation based on a given caption.

## And Then There Are VAEs & LDMs

Early Diffusion models work directly with image pixels, and hence, their approach is computationally intensive.

Modern Diffusion techniques use [Variational Autoencoders (VAEs)](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.6114>) to create a latent representation of original image data, where smaller image patches are converted into vectors.

Then, models called [Latent Diffusion models](</stable-signature-metas-solution-to-end-misinformation-spread-with-ai-generated-images-abe4ecfd5aec#519b>) efficiently work on this latent representation, making tasks like image generation less computationally expensive.

This is taken further by Autoregressive models (like [DALL-E](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.12092>)) that treat image generation as a language generation task.

They first convert images into discrete tokens using Discrete autoencoders like [Vector-quantized VAEs (VQ-VAEs)](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.00937>).

This is done by a **Quantization layer** in their architecture, which converts the continuous latent representation of an image into discrete tokens that can then be processed like text.

## Here Comes ‘Transfusion’

The Transfusion approach is inspired by the above-discussed and aims to combine training with both discrete and continuous data for a single model.

### Representing Data

Text data consisting of strings is tokenized into discrete tokens from a fixed vocabulary.

These tokens are integer values that represent a word or parts of words.

Each token is converted into Embeddings before feeding it to the Tranfusion model.

Images are encoded into continuous vectors or patches using a pre-trained Variational Autoencoder (VAE).

Each image’s patches are sequenced from left to right and top to bottom to create a sequence of patch vectors.

These patch vectors have special tokens that mark the image data's beginning (BOI) and end (EOI).

A pre-trained VAE converts images to and from latent representations and then into patch representations using a simple linear layer or a [U-Net down block](<https://medium.com/gitconnected/skeleton-recall-loss-is-the-new-breakthrough-in-segmentation-b1ce43c093f4#11fd>). (Image from [the original research paper](<https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2408.11039>))

These are then inserted between the text tokens to create a single sequence of mixed modality data.

### Model Architecture

The core of the Transfusion model is a [Transformer](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformer_\(deep_learning_architecture\)>) that can process both text and image data using its Attention and Feed-forward layers.

Traditional text processing Transformers use**Causal Attention,** a **unidirectional A** ttention approach where each token in a sequence has access to only the tokens before it.

This ensures that the model can process tokens in each sequence, like humans do, without “looking ahead” to future words.

This Attention mechanism is implemented by masking future tokens (**Causal masking**).

This is unlike images that do not have a natural sequence**.**

**Bi-directional Attention** is used in image processing so that each patch can access all other patches in the same image to capture spatial information.

Transfusion uses both of these mechanisms to ensure that text is processed with Causal Attention and image elements with bidirectional Attention while sharing the same Attention space.

Tranfusion expands the causal mask allowing each patch to access all other patches in the same image. (Image from [the original research paper](<https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2408.11039>))

### Loss Function

Transfusion model’s training objective combines both:

* **Language modelling loss (LM)** for text (calculated per token) and
* **Diffusion loss (LDDPM)** for images (calculated per image)

These are combined and balanced using a coefficient `λ`.

Transfusion loss (Image from [the original research paper](<https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2408.11039>))

### Model Inference

For generating multi-modal data, the Transfusion model uses a decoding algorithm that switches between **Language modelling (LM) mode** for text generation and **Diffusion mode** for image generation.

The LM mode starts by generating tokens one by one using a learned prediction distribution over a vocabulary.

When it encounters a BOI (beginning of image) token, the Diffusion mode is activated.

Starting with pure noise, it gradually denoises it and generates an image.

An EOI (end of image) token is then added, and the model returns to the LM mode once again.

## Performance Of ‘Transfusion’

### Comparison With ‘Chameleon’

Transfusion is primarily compared with another multi-modal model called [**Chameleon**](<https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09818v1>).

Chameleon converts both text and image data into discrete tokens before processing them (called the **Early-fusion approach**).

It uses Language modelling loss across both modalities during its training.

Contrary to this, Transfusion keeps images in continuous space, creating their latent representations using a pre-trained VAE, which preserves more image detail and improves efficiency.

It also uses different Attention mechanisms and losses for text and images.

The performance of both is measured on various benchmarks using various metrics as follows:

* Language Modeling: [C4](<https://paperswithcode.com/dataset/c4>) [Perplexity](<https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/perplexity>) and [Wikipedia](<https://huggingface.co/datasets/legacy-datasets/wikipedia>) Perplexity
* Text Classification/Language Understanding: LLaMA 2 Eval Suite Accuracy
* Image Captioning: [MS-COCO](<https://paperswithcode.com/dataset/coco>) 5k [CIDEr](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.5726>)
* Image Generation: MS-COCO 30k [FID](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fr%C3%A9chet_inception_distance>)
* Text-to-Image Alignment: MS-COCO 30k [CLIP](<https://openai.com/index/clip/>)

It is seen that Transfusion has superior results and computational efficiency (uses fewer [FLOPs](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FLOPS>)) when compared to Chameleon across a range of multi-modal tasks (especially image generation and text prediction).

### Performance Comparison With Popular LLMs

A 7-billion-parameter version of Transfusion trained on 2 trillion tokens (1 trillion text corpus tokens and 3.5 billion images and captions) outperforms popular image generation models like [DeepFloyd](<https://stability.ai/news/deepfloyd-if-text-to-image-model>) and [SDXL](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01952>).

Transfusion also matches the performance of the [Llama models](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288>) in text generation tasks.
