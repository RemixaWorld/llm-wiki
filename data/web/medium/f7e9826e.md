This article starts with a common scenario: participating in an open-book exam. Typically, we have three strategies:

* Method 1: Answer quickly for familiar topics. For unfamiliar ones, refer to the reference book. Quickly locate the relevant sections, organize and summarize them mentally, then write your answer on the exam paper.
* Method 2: For each topic, refer to the book. Identify the relevant sections, summarize them in your mind, and then write your response on the exam paper.
* Method 3: For each topic, consult the book and identify relevant sections. Before forming an opinion, categorize the gathered information into three groups: `**Correct**`, `**Incorrect**`, and `**Ambiguous**`. Process each type of information separately. Then, based on this processed information, compile and summarize it mentally. Write your response on the exam paper.

Method 1 involves [self-RAG](</advanced-rag-08-self-rag-c0c5b5952e0e>), whereas method 2 is the [classic RAG](<https://medium.com/ai-in-plain-english/a-brief-introduction-to-retrieval-augmented-generation-rag-b7eb70982891>) process.

Lastly, method 3, referred to as [Corrective Retrieval Augmented Generation (CRAG)](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.15884.pdf>), is introduced in this article.

## Motivation of CRAG

Figure 1: The examples show that a low-quality retriever is prone to introducing a substantial amount of irrelevant information, impeding the generators from acquiring accurate knowledge and potentially misleading them. Source: [Corrective Retrieval Augmented Generation](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.15884.pdf>).

Figure 1 illustrates that most traditional RAG methods do not consider the relevance of the document to the question and simply merge the retrieved documents. This can introduce irrelevant information, which can hinder the model from obtaining accurate knowledge and possibly mislead it, leading to hallucination issues.

Additionally, most traditional RAG methods take the entire retrieved documents as input. However, a significant portion of text in these retrieved documents is often not necessary for generation and shouldn’t be equally involved in RAG.

## Key Idea of CRAG

CRAG has designed a lightweight retrieval evaluator to assess the overall quality of documents retrieved for specific queries. It also uses web search as a supplementary tool to improve retrieval results.

CRAG is plug-and-play, allowing for seamless integration with various methods based on RAG. The overall architecture is depicted in Figure 2.

Press enter or click to view image in full size

Figure 2: The position of CRAG(red dashed box) in RAG. A retrieval evaluator is designed to evaluate the relevance of retrieved documents to the input. It also estimates a confidence level, which can trigger different knowledge retrieval actions, namely `**{Correct, Incorrect, Ambiguous}**`. Here, "`**x**`" represents the query. Image by author, the CRAG section(red dashed box) is sourced from [Corrective Retrieval Augmented Generation](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.15884.pdf>).

As illustrated in Figure 2, CRAG enhances the traditional RAG by introducing a retrieval evaluator to assess the relationship between the retrieved documents and the query.

There are 3 possible judgment results.

* If it is `**correct**`, this implies that the retrieved documents contains the necessary content as required by the query, then employ a knowledge refinement algorithm to rewrite the retrieved documents.
* If the retrieved documents is `**incorrect**`, this means that the query and the retrieved documents are irrelevant. Consequently, we can’t send the document to LLM. In CRAG, a web search engine is used to retrieve external knowledge.
* For `**ambiguous**` cases, this implies that the retrieved documents might be close but insufficient to provide an answer. In such cases, additional information would need to be sourced through web search. So both the knowledge refinement algorithm and the search engine are employed.

Lastly, the processed information is forwarded to the LLM for response generation. Figure 3 provides a formal depiction of this process.

Figure 3: Evaluation and processing. Source: [Corrective Retrieval Augmented Generation](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.15884.pdf>).

Note that the web search does not use the user’s input query directly for searching. Instead, it constructs a prompt and presents it to GPT-3.5 Turbo in a few-shot manner to obtain the query for searching.

After gaining a general understanding of the method, let’s individually discuss the two key components of CRAG: the retrieval retrieval evaluator and the knowledge refinement algorithm.

### Retrieval Evaluator

As shown in Figure 4, retrieval evaluator significantly influences the outcomes of subsequent procedures and is crucial in determining the overall system performance.

Press enter or click to view image in full size

Figure 4: Knowledge correction in CRAG. Source: [Corrective Retrieval Augmented Generation](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.15884.pdf>).

CRAG employs a lightweight [T5-large model](<https://huggingface.co/google-t5/t5-large>) as a retrieval evaluator and fine-tunes it. It is worth noting that in the era of large language models, T5-large is also considered lightweight.

For each query, typically ten documents are retrieved. The query is then concatenated with each document individually as input to predict their relevance. During the fine-tuning process, assign a label of 1 to positive samples and -1 to negative samples. During inference, the evaluator assigns a relevance score to each document, ranging from -1 to 1.

These scores will be categorized into three levels based on thresholds. Clearly, two thresholds are required for this categorization. In CRAG, the threshold settings may vary depending on the experimental data:

> The two confidence thresholds for triggering one of the three actions were set empirically. Specifically, they were set as (0.59, -0.99) in PopQA, (0.5, -0.91) in PubQA and ArcChallenge, as well as (0.95, -0.91) in Biography.

### Knowledge Refinement Algorithm

For the retrieved relevant documents, CRAG has designed a decompose-then-recompose method of knowledge extraction to further extract the most critical knowledge statements, as shown in Figure 4.

Firstly, heuristic rules are applied to break down each document into fine-grained knowledge strips, aiming to obtain fine-grained results. If the retrieved documents consists of only one or two sentences, it’s considered an independent unit. Otherwise, the document is divided into smaller units, typically composed of several sentences, depending on the total length. Each unit is expected to contain an independent piece of information.

Next, the retrieval evaluator is used to calculate the relevance score of each knowledge strip. Strips with low relevance scores are filtered out. The remaining relevant knowledge strips are then reassembled in order to form the internal knowledge.

### The constructor of class CorrectiveRAGPack

First, let’s look at the constructor function, [its source code](<https://github.com/run-llama/llama_index/blob/v0.10.29/llama-index-packs/llama-index-packs-corrective-rag/llama_index/packs/corrective_rag/base.py#L47>) is as follows:

```python class CorrectiveRAGPack(BaseLlamaPack): def __init__(self, documents: List[Document], tavily_ai_apikey: str) -> None: """Init params.""" llm = OpenAI(model="gpt-4") self.relevancy_pipeline = QueryPipeline( chain=[DEFAULT_RELEVANCY_PROMPT_TEMPLATE, llm] ) self.transform_query_pipeline = QueryPipeline( chain=[DEFAULT_TRANSFORM_QUERY_TEMPLATE, llm] ) self.llm = llm self.index = VectorStoreIndex.from_documents(documents) self.tavily_tool = TavilyToolSpec(api_key=tavily_ai_apikey) ```

Please note that the default setting is `**gpt-4**`. If you don’t have permission to use `**gpt-4**`, you can manually switch it to `**gpt-3.5-turbo**`.

### class CorrectiveRAGPack:: run()

[The source code of function run()](<https://github.com/run-llama/llama_index/blob/v0.10.29/llama-index-packs/llama-index-packs-corrective-rag/llama_index/packs/corrective_rag/base.py#L106>) is as follows:

```python class CorrectiveRAGPack(BaseLlamaPack): ... ... def run(self, query_str: str, **kwargs: Any) -> Any: """Run the pipeline.""" # Retrieve nodes based on the input query string. retrieved_nodes = self.retrieve_nodes(query_str, **kwargs) # Evaluate the relevancy of each retrieved document in relation to the query string. relevancy_results = self.evaluate_relevancy(retrieved_nodes, query_str) # Extract texts from documents that are deemed relevant based on the evaluation. relevant_text = self.extract_relevant_texts(retrieved_nodes, relevancy_results) # Initialize search_text variable to handle cases where it might not get defined. search_text = "" # If any document is found irrelevant, transform the query string for better search results. if "no" in relevancy_results: transformed_query_str = self.transform_query_pipeline.run( query_str=query_str ).message.content # Conduct a search with the transformed query string and collect the results. search_text = self.search_with_transformed_query(transformed_query_str) # Compile the final result. If there's additional search text from the transformed query, # it's included; otherwise, only the relevant text from the initial retrieval is returned. if search_text: return self.get_result(relevant_text, search_text, query_str) else: return self.get_result(relevant_text, "", query_str) ```

The code above and the standard CRAG process have three main differences:

* There’s no judgement or handling of `**ambiguous**` documents.
* Instead of using the trained T5-large model, it utilizes LLM to evaluate the retrieved information.
* The process of knowledge refinement is skipped.

Despite these differences, LlamaIndex offers an alternative way([langchain](<https://github.com/langchain-ai/langgraph/blob/main/examples/rag/langgraph_crag.ipynb>) also does this) of thinking.

### Use LLM to Evaluate Retrieved Information

[The code](<https://github.com/run-llama/llama_index/blob/v0.10.29/llama-index-packs/llama-index-packs-corrective-rag/llama_index/packs/corrective_rag/base.py#L71>) is as follows:

```python class CorrectiveRAGPack(BaseLlamaPack): ... ... def evaluate_relevancy( self, retrieved_nodes: List[Document], query_str: str ) -> List[str]: """Evaluate relevancy of retrieved documents with the query.""" relevancy_results = [] for node in retrieved_nodes: relevancy = self.relevancy_pipeline.run( context_str=node.text, query_str=query_str ) relevancy_results.append(relevancy.message.content.lower().strip()) return relevancy_results ```

[The prompt for calling LLM](<https://github.com/run-llama/llama_index/blob/v0.10.29/llama-index-packs/llama-index-packs-corrective-rag/llama_index/packs/corrective_rag/base.py#L13>) is shown below:

``` DEFAULT_RELEVANCY_PROMPT_TEMPLATE = PromptTemplate( template="""As a grader, your task is to evaluate the relevance of a document retrieved in response to a user's question. Retrieved Document: ------------------- {context_str} User Question: -------------- {query_str} Evaluation Criteria: - Consider whether the document contains keywords or topics related to the user's question. - The evaluation should not be overly stringent; the primary objective is to identify and filter out clearly irrelevant retrievals. Decision: - Assign a binary score to indicate the document's relevance. - Use 'yes' if the document is relevant to the question, or 'no' if it is not. Please provide your binary score ('yes' or 'no') below to indicate the document's relevance to the user question.""") ```

The CRAG paper shows that ChatGPT’s performance in identifying the relevance of retrieval is not as high as that of T5-Large.

## My Insights and Thoughts on CRAG

### The difference between CRAG and self-RAG

* From a process perspective, self-RAG can provide direct responses using LLM without retrieval, while CRAG must perform a retrieval before adding a evaluation layer.
* From a structural point of view, self-RAG is more complex than CRAG, it demands a more intricate training procedure and multiple label generation and evaluation during the generation phase, inevitably increasing the inference cost. As a result, CRAG is more lightweight than self-RAG.
* Performance-wise, as illustrated in Figure 5, CRAG generally outperforms self-RAG in most situations.

### Improvement of the Retrieval Evaluator

The retrieval evaluator can be seen as a scoring classification model. This model is used to determine the relevance of a query and document, similar to the [re-ranking model in RAG](<https://medium.com/towards-artificial-intelligence/advanced-rag-04-re-ranking-85f6ae8170b1>).

Such relevance judgement models could be improved by integrating more features that align with real-world scenarios. For instance, the scientific paper question-answering RAG contains many professional terms, while the RAG in the tourism field tends to have more colloquial user queries.

By adding scene features to the retrieval evaluator’s training data, it can better assess the relevance of the retrieved documents. Other features, such as user intentions and edit distance, can also be incorporated, as shown in Figure 6:

Furthermore, considering the results obtained by T5-Large, lightweight models also appear capable of achieving good results. This provides hope for the application of CRAG in small-scale teams or companies.

### Scores and Thresholds of the Retrieval Evaluator

As previously stated, the threshold differs for various types of data. Moreover, we can find that the thresholds for `**ambiguous**` and `**incorrect**` are basically around -0.9, suggesting that most of the retrieved knowledge is relevant to the query. It might not be advisable to entirely discard this retrieved knowledge and depend solely on web searches.

In practical applications, we need to adjust according to actual problems and needs.
