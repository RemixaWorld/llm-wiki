# RobustFT: framework for Robust Supervised Fine-tuning of LLMs under noisy response

Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) helps in adapting LLMs to specific domains or tasks, however do suffer from noise in data collection, thereby impacting the downstream model performance. In order to address it, this paper[1] introduce a robust SFT framework (ROBUSTFT) that performs noise detection and relabeling on downstream task data.

Key contributions:

* investigate the critical yet understudied challenge of noise-robust supervised fine-tuning for LLMs, which aligns more closely with real-world scenarios where noise is inevitable.
* design a self-contained framework to leverage the intrinsic interactions between models and data for effective noise detection and denoising, eliminating dependencies on external models or resources

## Methodology

### i) Overview

This approach addresses this challenge of detecting noisy data through a systematic framework comprising noise detection-and-denoising mechanisms to prevent performance degradation.

* For noise detection, we leverage the consensus among multiple expert LLMs and employ a Checker to identify noisy samples.
* For denoising, employed a two-pronged approach:

(1) first, we utilize context-enhanced reasoning with clean samples to relabel noisy instances through a Review Agent

(2) second, we implement a perplexity-based data selection mechanism to exclude samples with low confidence scores

* As shown in figure below, this dual-process framework effectively mitigates noise-induced performance deterioration

### ii) Noise Detection

* utilize the base LLM to generate predictions for all data samples

where qi represents the query,M denotes the LLM and ˆyi is the base prediction

* For internal noise detection, we introduce a reasoning-enhanced LLM that iteratively combines reasoning and reflection processes. This LLM first performs step-by-step reasoning, followed by self-reflection on its reasoning path, and iterates between these two stages to achieve superior reasoning capabilities.
* For each data sample, this iterative process can be formalized as:

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:388/1*e74V8xAJcP2EBlMX38C-tg.png)

where ˆyreasi represents the final prediction,MReas and MRefl denote the reasoning and reflection LLMs, respectively, with each reflection stage evaluating and refining the previous reasoning output.

* To ensure prediction reliability, we implement a consistency-based Checker mechanism that analyzes multiple prediction sources: the original label (yi), the base LLM prediction (ˆyi), and the reasoning-enhanced prediction (ˆyreasi ). This mechanism evaluates the agreement among these predictions through a consistency metric:

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:314/1*2BiM2uWh_t8bfl-1_pYrDg.png)

where ri = 1 indicates high prediction consistency (reliable sample) and ri = 0 indicates prediction inconsistency (potentially noisy sample).

* Based on this consistency evaluation, we partition the dataset into clean samples Dclean = {(qi, yi)|ri = 1} and potentially noisy samples Dnoise = {(qi, yi)|ri =0} for subsequent denoising treatment.

### iii) Data Denoising

* For the potentially noisy dataset Dnoise, we employ a context learning approach for data relabeling, leveraging external knowledge to reduce noise in the data
* Specifically, it involves projecting queries from both the reliable dataset Dclean and the potentially noisy dataset Dnoise into a shared latent space:

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:203/1*trMK1sXVVYPBhYmzdoGCfQ.png)

where hi represents the d-dimensional latent representation of query qi obtained through the encoder network

* During inference, for each noisy sample, we retrieve the k most similar samples from the reliable dataset as context for reasoning:

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:362/1*792TXtpzQTejxRLHnxNVVw.png)

where Nk (qi, Dclean) denotes the indices of the k most similar samples to qi in Dclean based on their latent representations.

* Combined with the previously obtained reasoning enhanced predictions ˆyreas i , we introduce a Review Agent to evaluate and relabel the data:

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:259/1*cF40qzRmkF52Fuuhq30-Aw.png)

### iv) Data Selection

* To maintain high data quality and prevent error propagation during subsequent training, introduced a confidence-based filtering mechanism leveraging entropy metrics. This approach enables us to quantitatively assess the uncertainty in context-enhanced predictions and retain only the most confident sample
* entropy score for each context-enhanced response is computed as:

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:337/1*7abb7ZJFoi6NO-hXCSWoCw.png)

where p(yij |qi, yi<j) represents the model’s prediction probability for the j-th token conditioned on the input query and previous tokens, and N denotes the sequence length

* Lower entropy scores indicate higher model confidence and more deterministic predictions.
* Based on these scores, we rank and filter the samples to form our final selected dataset:

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:445/1*yp1QDIbfB_aB2TNzYGGMsQ.png)

where β controls the selection ratio, which defaults to 50%

* Through this process, we obtain Dselect, which demonstrates reduced noise levels and higher confidence scores through the combined application of denoising relabeling and selective filtering

## Experiments

### i) Backbones and Baselines

**a) Base Models**

* employed diverse model architectures, including Gemma2–9B and Llama3.1–8B , along with models of varying parameter sizes such as Llama3.2–3B

**b) Baselines**

(1) Vanilla: direct model inference

(2) SFT-enhanced solutions utilizing supplementary data to improve LLM performance, including Hermes-3 and Tulu-3

(3) Standard SFT using potentially noisy training data

(4) Denoising approaches, including the state-of-the-art NoiseAL and LLM-based denoising methods such as SelfLabel and SelfSelect

(5) Self-enhancement methods like SelfRAG, which augments inference context using training data.

### ii) Implementation Details

* partitioned each dataset into training and test sets, introducing varying degrees of noise perturbation in the training data.
* For model fine-tuning, we employed Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) implemented through Llama-factory across all open-source models
* fine-tuning process was conducted for 2 epochs. We set the n = 4 and θ = 50%, with further parameter analysis planned for subsequent experiments

### iii) Comparison with Baselines

* Table below shows Performance comparison under different noise rates with Llama-3.1 8B

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:700/1*HCufaPaxmaBSF6RAwWIYHA.png)

experimental results yielded the following key insights:

* **Noise management is critical in LLM fine-tuning:** SFT results clearly demonstrate that direct fine-tuning with noisy data substantially degrades model performance, emphasizing the necessity for robust noise detection and removal
* **LLMs exhibit limited inherent noise detection capabilities:** SelfSelect’s inferior performance compared to SFT indicates that LLMs cannot effectively identify noise, necessitating specialized noise detection and removal mechanisms.
* **Enhanced SFT approaches lack consistent improvement:** Methods like Tulu-3 and Hermes-3 failed to show uniform performance improvements across downstream tasks, suggesting the need for task-specific LLM adaptation strategies
* **Inference enhancement methods show modest gains:** these approaches achieved performance improvements despite potential noise in context data, though the improvements were not comparable to our method’s results
* **Denoising approaches demonstrate mixed results:** methods such as NoiseAL and SelfLabel show noise resistance and improvements on some datasets, they exhibit degradation on others

### iv) Comparison across Architectures and Parameter Sizes

* Table below shows Performance comparison across different model architectures and noise rates.

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:700/1*UIt5gDX0OZdeUcWwf0TPrQ.png)
* **Larger models are not inherently more robust:** general-purpose large models may be more susceptible to noise during domain-specific finetuning due to their lack of domain priors
* **Transformation mechanism from general models to domain experts:** Gemma2–9B showed strong general capabilities, it initially performed worse on domain-specific tasks.
* **Critical importance of denoising for smaller models:** Smaller models benefit more significantly from denoising strategies during domain-specific training

### v) Analysis

**a) Sensitivity Analysis**

* Figure below shows Sensitivity analysis on MMLU under different β and k with varying noise levels

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:523/1*Devb1e7Es7ZIsKV_QF8PiA.png)
* results show that model performance peaking at β = 40−50%, with performance degrading significantly beyond this range due to the inclusion of excessive noisy samples.
* For context length, performance improves with increasing k but plateaus, particularly in the range of k = 3−5, suggesting that moderate k provide sufficient reasoning support.
* These findings validate our default parameter choices (β = 50%, k = 3) without requiring extensive hyperparameter search

**b) Perplexity Analysis**

* Figure below shows Perplexity analysis of ROBUSTFT on MMLU and ARC with varying noise levels

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:700/1*ovuUdVC7_3uzfEcvl0yTYQ.png)
* Noise significantly increases perplexity, as evidenced in both SFT and vanilla models. In contrast, ROBUSTFT maintains relatively low perplexity level even with increased noise, demonstrating its robustness.
* The vanilla model exhibits flatter and more dispersed perplexity distributions, indicating frequent uncertainty in predictions. ROBUSTFT effectively concentrates perplexity in lower ranges, suggesting more confident and reliable predictions
* method shows consistency across datasets, with similar perplexity reduction patterns observed on both MMLU and ARC, validating its generalizability across different domains

**c) Category-wise Performance Analysis**

* Figure below shows Category-wise performance of ROBUSTFT

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:419/1*wRgcnlo49KdHFg0Jyw5Zlw.png)
* impact of noise varies significantly across different knowledge domains, with knowledge-intensive categories such as History, Healthcare, and Law experiencing more severe performance degradation under noisy conditions.
* ROBUSTFT demonstrates balanced and expanded performance across all categories, achieving comprehensive noise resistance rather than isolated improvements, as evidenced by its smooth and expanded radar plot.

**d) Stability Analysis**

* Figure below shows Stability analysis on MMLU and ARC

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:522/1*ChbZ6IedCqpxeGGG3-QqKg.png)

* Results show that ROBUSTFT maintains consistent performance, with only minimal variance increase at higher noise rates

## Conclusion

* propose a novel noise detection and denoising framework ROBUSTFT, which is specifically designed for LLMs
* proposed approach leverages a multi-expert collaborative mechanism for noise detection, enhanced by a reasoning-enhanced process
* implemented context-enhanced reasoning for data relabeling and utilize response entropy for data selection
