So, some of you may remember that around four weeks ago, there was a significant hype bubble around a new AI tool called Manus. At the time, you had to sign up for a waiting list to get access.

I duly did that, and the other day, I received notification that I had been approved to use Manus.

### What is Manus?

Manus is an autonomous artificial intelligence (AI) agent developed by the Chinese startup company Monica. It was launched in early March 2025 and quickly gained significant attention for its ability to perform complex tasks independently without continuous human guidance.

### Key Features of Manus:

* **Autonomy.** Manus operates autonomously, handling tasks such as website creation, stock analysis, travel planning, and schedule management.
* **Multi-Agent Architecture.** It uses a multi-agent system where specialized sub-agents manage complex workflows.
* **Cloud-Based Operation.** Manus runs asynchronously in the cloud, allowing tasks to proceed without constant supervision.
* **Real-Time Observability.** It provides a side panel called “Manus’s Computer,” which shows the steps to complete tasks, offering transparency in its operations.
* **Task Handling.** Manus can analyze data, compile reports, and create interactive websites without direct human intervention.

### Manus in action

Naturally, I was excited to try it out. When you click on the big `**Try Manus**` button, you’ll be asked to sign in to their service using an existing Gmail or Apple email, or you can enter your name and other email credentials.

Once connected, you’ll be presented with a very ChatGPT-like interface.

Now, it just so happens that someone I know plans to go to Cusco in Peru (from the UK) in a few weeks for a temporary work placement. Planning how to get there seems like an ideal task for Manus to work on. So that’s what I asked it to do. Here is the prompt I used.

> I’m travelling to Cusco in Peru from Edinburgh UK at the end of April (around the 24th — 26th). Please get me a travel itinerary for the cheapest one-way airfare including baggage charges and any overnight hotel stays required.

Manus started to work on the problem straight away and replied with,

> I’ll help you find the cheapest one-way airfare from Edinburgh, UK to Cusco, Peru for travel around April 24–26, 2025, including baggage charges and any necessary overnight stays. I’ll research options and prepare a comprehensive travel itinerary focusing on cost-effectiveness. This may take a few minutes while I gather the most current information.

After a minute or two, Manus came back with this,

> I’m encountering CAPTCHA challenges on some travel websites which is preventing me from accessing flight information directly. Would you like me to:
>
> Try alternative travel search methods and websites to gather flight information
>
> Suggest that you take over the browser temporarily to complete the CAPTCHA and help me access the flight search results
>
> I can continue researching using other methods, but your input would help me provide the most accurate information for your Edinburgh to Cusco trip.

I replied with,

> Try alternative search methods

After about another two minutes, I interjected with an additional request.

> Any overnight hotel stays must be located close to or within the boundaries of the nearest airport

Manus picked up on this added information right away.

> I understand your requirement that any overnight hotel stays must be located close to or within the boundaries of the nearest airport. I’ll make sure to focus on airport hotels or accommodations very close to the airports when researching potential overnight stays needed for your Edinburgh to Cusco journey. This will help minimize additional transportation costs and ensure convenient access for early departures or late arrivals

Overall, I was a bit disappointed with the response, although to be fair, it can give you a good starting point for your own research and the money-saving tips at the end could prove helpful. What I disliked most in the response was the vagueness around some of the information. I wasn’t looking for estimates. I had hoped that Manus could provide actual flight numbers and, crucially, flight prices.

Also, some of the pricing data it **_did_** provide just seemed wrong. For example, the quoted $100–$130 for an overnight stay at the Wyndham Costa del Sol Lima Airport hotel didn’t match reality. My research—using booking.com—showed a price double that amount.

### Summary

I don’t want to be too hard on Manus. It did a reasonable job of extracting data, but it seems a long way from automating your life. However, I only used it for one task, and it may well cope much better with different use cases.

I do think it’s useful for providing a starting point for your own research and investigation into whichever topic or tasks you’re considering.
