[Large Language Models (LLMs)](<https://github.com/SalvatoreRa/tutorial/blob/main/artificial%20intelligence/FAQ.md#:~:text=What%20is%20a%20Large%20Language%20Model%20\(LLM\)%3F>) have shown remarkable capabilities, but suffer from problems such as hallucination, lack of domain adaptation, and lack of continual learning. These problems are more pronounced when they have to deal with knowledge-intensive tasks. One of the solutions to these problems (or at least partial) is to provide relevant information in the context of the LLM (insert in the prompt). This system is generally [retrieval augmented generation (RAG)](<https://github.com/SalvatoreRa/tutorial/blob/main/artificial%20intelligence/FAQ.md#:~:text=What%20is%20Retrieval%20Augmented%20Generation%20\(RAG\)%3F>). This system has been shown to be capable of reducing hallucinations and improving responses.

## [RAG is Dead, Long Live RAGIs it really true that long-context LLMs are killing the RAG?levelup.gitconnected.com](</rag-is-dead-long-live-rag-c607e1799199?source=post_page-----7ec213516a56--------------------------------------->)

Nevertheless, there are still limitations associated with reliability and traceability. In fact, contextual hallucinations can still emerge, and sometimes the right context is not found. If the context is noisy the LLM generation can be impacted. In fact, the presence of irrelevant information can confound LLM and its ability to extract relevant information for generation. Second, LLMs do not incorporate which sources are used during generation, making it more complex to verify which sources are used.

## [Chat Quijote and the Windmills: Navigating AI Hallucinations on the Path to AccuracyStrategies and Tools for Enhancing Reliability in Large Language Modelslevelup.gitconnected.com](</chat-quijote-and-the-windmills-navigating-ai-hallucinations-on-the-path-to-accuracy-0aaecf46354c?source=post_page-----7ec213516a56--------------------------------------->)

During its pretraining phase, an LLM focuses on acquiring knowledge. After all, a model is trained in an autoregressive manner with the aim of predicting the next word in a sequence. LLM is thus not trained specifically for reasoning but this emerges during training (as if we could call it a side effect of training and [in-context learning](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.00234>)). Although we have not trained it for reasoning when we use the RAG we are interested in its ability to interpret context and use this information for response (so we are less interested in the knowledge gained during pre-training).

Or will be replaced?towardsdatascience.com](<https://towardsdatascience.com/a-requiem-for-the-transformer-297e6f14e189?source=post_page-----7ec213516a56--------------------------------------->)

We could then think about how to adapt the model’s capabilities in conducting reasoning about the found context and learning how to extract information. In a sense conducting an additional post-training step to focus on the capabilities we are interested in in our system.

> How can we adapt the model to reason about context?

This question is tried to be answered by a recently published article:

## [Improving Retrieval Augmented Language Model with Self-ReasoningThe Retrieval-Augmented Language Model (RALM) has shown remarkable performance on knowledge-intensive tasks by…arxiv.org](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19813?source=post_page-----7ec213516a56--------------------------------------->)

Briefly, the process consists of three steps:

* **a Relevance-Aware Process (RAP)** , where the model is instructed to assess the relevance of documents to the application.
* **Evidence-Aware Selective Process (EAP)** , where the model selects and cites relevant documents.
* **Trajectory Analysis Process (TAP)** where the model generates a concise analysis based on what happened in the previous two steps and subsequently produces the answer.

image source: [here](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.19813>)

The authors start from a classic RAG model with an LLM and a retriever. The initial intuition is that a person is capable of judging whether or not a document is relevant to a question. So they decide to instruct an LLM to judge the relevance of found documents to the user’s query. The model is instructed to specify why it believes those documents are relevant to the query. The output at this step should include whether it is relevant and why it is relevant. At the same time, if the retriever does not find relevant documents, LLM should use its parametric memory (i.e., knowledge acquired during training) to answer the query

To answer a question from documents, humans identify crucial sentences (perhaps mark them with a marker) and then use them to answer. it is good practice to cite which documents are used (especially if you are writing an essay or producing a paper). LLM struggles with this process. so authors force LLM to identify sentences that are supportive of the answer to the question (these sentences are called evidences by the authors). The authors then instruct the model to select the sentences that are important to the answer and also provide why these sentences are important. The expected output at this stage is a list containing the sentences to be cited and why they are important to cite.

In the last step, LLM benefits from the previous steps and can finally generate a response. The model analyzes the reasoning conducted during this process and then produces both the response and a concise analysis. The final output is then both the analysis and the response.

image source: [here](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.19813>)

Obviously, at this point, we need data, and the authors have generated 10,000 quality examples. This is especially important for reasoning training. They then built a corpus that is augmented with self-reasoning trajectories (which are the various analyses produced at each step). Given a question q and an answer y, the model is trained with y, q, and the trajectories.

To verify that their approach is functional, they then selected two datasets of short question-answering (QA) and one of long QA. They used [LLaMA2–7B](<https://huggingface.co/meta-llama/Llama-2-7b>), LLaMA2–13B, and their instruction-tuned versions as models. After that, they also used other prompt engineering approaches as baselines to see if their method gave better results.

In the table we can see:

* The results confirm that using RAG improves performance significantly toward systems composed only of the LLM. Prompt engineering improves this baseline, and their approach gives a better result.
* For long-QA, the method produces very good results, superior even by recall to GPT-4 (which is no small thing because these are very small models in comparison).

Conducting an ablation study, the authors note:

* removing the first step produces an overall performance drop in short-form QA. This suggests that checking the relevance of documents to the query helps performance. Especially in the fact verification dataset, it is important to filter out irrelevant documents.
* Removing the second step brings a significant performance drop by showing that identifying key judgments matters for accuracy.
* The third step is instrumental for all datasets tested showing a marked impact on performance.

image source: [here](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.19813>)

Because the presence of irrelevant information hurts retrieval, the authors test the robustness of their noise approach. They test their approach in two settings:

* by conducting random shuffling of documents post-retrieval.
* by replacing half of the retrieved documents with documents that are relevant to a different question in the dataset.

The model is more robust than other noise approaches. Especially the first setting has minimal impact on their approach. The second setting is more challenging and there is a decline in performance, but still not too impactful.

Finally, the authors thoroughly investigated whether the model really supports citations or not. They chose human evaluators to check that the model was citing documents. They asked human judges whether the submitted documents supported the sentences in the answer. The result shows that the system is functional in presenting citations and also correlates well with an automatic evaluation

image source: [here](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.19813>)

> RAGs can effectively enhance the performance of LLMs in handling knowledge-intensive tasks. Despite their effectiveness, notable concerns about their reliability and traceability persist. To address these limitations, we propose a novel SELFREASONING framework to improve the performance of RALMs by using reasoning trajectories generated by the LLM itself. — [source](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.19813>)

The approach presented is interesting because it improves both the capabilities of the system’s response and the tracking of why LLM responded the way it did. The idea behind it is to improve the model’s ability to exploit context and extract relevant information. So the authors use specific training to enable the model to improve its capabilities.

This approach is interesting because it is based on the effective use of reasoning. It seems promising but has not been tested with more complex questions that require multi-hop reasoning, code generation, or arithmetic reasoning.
