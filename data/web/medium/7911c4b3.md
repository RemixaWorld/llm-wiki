It is definitely more than an OCR and more like a pair of eyes:

* **Math equations** — Turns printed math into neat, readable code you can copy and reuse
* **Images** — Doesn’t ignore them; it _describes_ them
* **Signatures** — Finds them and pulls them out separately
* **Watermarks** — Same thing, it can spot them and label them
* **Checkboxes** — Tells you which ones are ticked and which aren’t
* **Tables** — Doesn’t mess them up; it gives you full, proper tables you can paste into a doc or webpage

## Understanding the training

Press enter or click to view image in full size

Press enter or click to view image in full size

Can handle signatures as well

They used over 250,000 documents — everything from tax forms to research papers.

* Some of it was fake (but realistic), some of it was real and carefully labeled.
* They trained it first on the fake stuff, then fine-tuned it using the real ones.
* No handwriting support yet, and sometimes the model guesses wrong — but for typed documents, it’s really solid.

## It’s of great use

trust me, this is a big release and one of its kinda model.

* **Students & Researchers**: You can scan papers and keep the math and tables intact.
* **Lawyers & Finance Folks**: Finally, an OCR that knows what a signature is.
* **Doctors & Clinics**: Forms with checkboxes? It gets them.
* **Office Teams** : If you’re stuck with scanned reports, this turns them into real text you can search or use.

## Why is it important

We’re living in a world where everyone wants to feed documents into AI tools.

> But if the document is a mess, even the smartest AI can’t help.

This model cleans up the mess first. It can be of great use to prepare data from unstructured images. It gives you a clean, structured version of your document — something an AI tool can actually make sense of.
