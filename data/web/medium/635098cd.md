## **1\. Data collection and preprocessing**

As we embarked on the process of batch processing to source and ingest unstructured text datasets, we explored a multitude of techniques to handle and transform the raw data into a structured format suitable for analysis. This was crucial for ensuring that the data was clean, coherent, and ready for further processing.

Our batch processing setup involved the use of Small Language Models (SLMs) like [Phi 3.5](<https://huggingface.co/microsoft/Phi-3.5-mini-instruct>) to handle challenges such as ambiguous sentences, emoji conversion, and slang.

### **Ambiguous sentences**

**Purpose** : We leveraged SLMs to refine the text, remove ambiguity, and enhance coherence and overall quality through rewriting, ensuring the text was well structured and easier to understand for further analysis. This approach was beneficial for text containing reviews and feedback, where the original content might have been cluttered with extraneous information.

**Example** :

* **Before** : “Hi! The product I bought from [https://example.com](<https://example.com/>) last week is amazing! But the delivery was late.”
* **After** : “Product bought last week was amazing but delivery late.”

### **Emoji conversion**

**Purpose** : Feedback and review texts often contained emojis that conveyed nuanced sentiments but were inconsistently interpreted by analysis tools. Converting emojis to textual descriptions helped sentiment analysis capture the intended sentiment more effectively, providing clearer context. This ensured accurate sentiment capture and analysis, which traditional text analysis tools might have missed.

**Example:**

* **Before:** “The product is 😍”
* **After:** “The product is amazing.”

### **Handling slang**

**Purpose:** Reviews often contained slang, abbreviations, or incomplete sentences, which could hinder comprehension. Adding context to prompts during rewriting helped translate slang into standard language and completed fragmented thoughts, improving text quality and coherence. Slang and incomplete sentences could obscure meaning, so translating and clarifying them ensured the text was clear and easy to understand.

**Example:**

* **Before:** “The product is lit. But… delivery was late.”
* **After:** “The product is excellent, but the delivery was delayed.”

## **2\. Clustering**

Faced with vast unstructured datasets, we turned to the realm of unsupervised Machine Learning, specifically clustering, to transform our chaotic text into coherent, manageable clusters. This strategic move allowed us to unearth common themes and topics, sharpening our analysis and making it more comprehensible.

In document summarization, clustering grouped similar sections to streamline key information extraction. For topic modeling, it revealed intricate hierarchical structures within extensive text corpora. In customer feedback analysis, clustering improved efficiency by grouping reviews to identify common themes and sentiments. Additionally, it supported spam filtering, social network analysis, content-based profiling, and data reduction, making it essential for extracting insights and enhancing decision-making.

Despite our confidence in [clustering techniques](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hierarchical_clustering>), we meticulously explored the best-suited method for our needs among various options — DBSCAN, hierarchical, and agglomerative, among others.

After thorough analysis, we embraced agglomerative clustering, which aligned perfectly with our need to examine the nested structure of data. This method offered several advantages over other techniques, namely _k_ -means and DBSCAN:

* **Pre-determination of number of clusters:** Unlike _k_ -means, agglomerative clustering’s flexible approach allowed us to avoid defining the number of clusters in advance.
* **Handled different cluster shapes and sizes:** Agglomerative clustering managed clusters of various shapes and sizes better than _k_ -means, which assumed spherical clusters of similar sizes.
* **Robust to noise and outliers:** While DBSCAN was also effective at handling noise and clusters, agglomerative clustering’s additional benefits around handling noise and outliers made it our preferred choice.
* **Scalability:** Although agglomerative clustering was computationally intensive compared to DBSCAN, we optimized it with single and complete linkage to handle our large datasets.
* **Parameter sensitivity:** DBSCAN required careful selection of parameters — such as eps and minPts — which was challenging for us. Agglomerative clustering did not need such parameters and was easier to implement.
* **Handled varying densities:** DBSCAN struggled with clusters when their densities varied. Agglomerative clustering merged clusters based on similarity rather than density, enabling us to form clusters based on word or topic similarity.
* **Deterministic approach:** Unlike DBSCAN, agglomerative clustering was deterministic, providing a stable model for a given set of data.

## **3\. MapReduce summarization**

After clustering the unstructured text into coherent groups, we utilized the [MapReduce](<https://python.langchain.com/docs/tutorials/summarization/#map-reduce>) framework to process these clusters in parallel, creating summaries that were both meaningful and representative of the text.

* **Map step:** The text was segmented into smaller chunks or sub-documents and processed in parallel, accelerating the summarization process. Each chunk was then individually summarized using a language model.
* **Reduce step:** The individual summaries produced in the map step was then merged into a single, cohesive summary. This final summary encapsulated the key points from the entire document.

We found the MapReduce summarization technique highly effective for summarizing large corpora of unstructured text by summarizing chunks individually (map) and then combining them (reduce), especially when sub-documents didn’t rely heavily on preceding context. This method was both scalable and efficient for large volumes of text. While we also explored the Refine Method and Stuff Chain, each presented unique advantages and limitations.

* **Refine method:** The Refine method simplified summarization by iteratively updating the summary with each new document, making it suitable for continuous data streams.
* **Stuff Chain method:** The Stuff Chain method processed large documents by splitting them into chunks, summarizing each separately, and then combining them. While efficient for handling large files, it was constrained by the language model’s context window.

We share here our findings when we compared these alternative techniques to MapReduce:

### **MapReduce versus Refine method**

* **Scalability:** MapReduce was more scalable as it processed data in parallel, whereas the Refine method was more sequential.
* **Complexity:** The Refine method was simpler and easier to implement but did not handle large datasets as efficiently as MapReduce.
* **Functionality:** MapReduce offered more robust functionality for large-scale summarization tasks, while the Refine method was better suited for continuous data streams.

### **MapReduce versus Stuff Chain**

* **Scalability:** MapReduce was more scalable and could handle larger volumes of text, while Stuff Chain was limited by the language model’s context window.
* **Efficiency:** MapReduce processed data in parallel, making it more efficient for large datasets, whereas Stuff Chain struggled with very large corpora.
* **Flexibility:** MapReduce was more flexible in handling various data structures and sizes, while Stuff Chain was more straightforward but less adaptable to complex data.

## **4\. Attributes identification**

After sourcing and pre-processing the unstructured text data, we employed agglomerative clustering and MapReduce summarization techniques to organize the data into meaningful clusters. The next crucial step was the identification of key attributes within these clusters. This involved extracting prominent topics from each cluster, which provided a high-level overview of the subject matter in each group. This process facilitated easy labeling and a deeper understanding of the data.

To achieve this, we experimented with various topic modeling techniques, including the [BERTopic](<https://maartengr.github.io/BERTopic/index.html>) library. While BERTopic was a powerful technique for topic modeling, we also explored the following other techniques: [Latent Dirichlet Allocation](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latent_Dirichlet_allocation>) (LDA), [Latent Semantic Analysis](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latent_semantic_analysis>) (LSA), [Non-Negative Matrix Factorization](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-negative_matrix_factorization>) (NMF), and [Top2vec](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word2vec>). Both LLMs and SLMs were effective for topic modeling, with LLMs offering a broader and more contextually rich understanding, while SLMs provided efficiency and speed for specific use cases.

## **5\. Generating prime synopsis**

To maximize the value derived from unstructured text data, we generated a “golden summary” or prime synopsis. This was essentially a summary of summaries, providing a concise yet comprehensive overview of all the clusters combined. This ensured that no crucial information was overlooked.

To achieve this, we employed advanced techniques such as the [Chain of Density](<https://github.com/richawo/chain-of-density>) (CoD) and [Chain of Verification](<https://www.reddit.com/r/PromptEngineering/comments/1bh610y/chainofverification_cove_explained/?rdt=61851>) (CoVe) prompt engineering methods. The CoD technique enhanced the quality of AI-generated summaries by gradually increasing the density of information without significantly expanding the summary’s length. By systematically adding critical details while maintaining readability, CoD significantly improved the quality and relevance of the summaries. On the other hand, the CoVe technique enhanced the reliability of responses generated by Large Language Models (LLMs) by systematically verifying their outputs. By breaking down complex queries into simpler verification tasks, CoVe enhanced the overall quality of generated content across various applications and built trust in AI-generated content.

## **6\. Sentiment tagging**

In addition to summarization, [sentiment tagging](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentiment_analysis>) was performed to extract the sentiments (positive, negative, neutral) associated with each text entry. This allowed us to identify not only the topics being discussed but also how people felt about them. For instance, sentiment analysis revealed which topics elicited positive feedback and which were sources of dissatisfaction.

To achieve accurate sentiment tagging, we used various approaches to identify positive, negative, and neutral texts. One of the key challenges in sentiment analysis was dealing with paradoxical sentiments and emojis. Paradoxical sentiments were difficult to classify as positive, negative, or neutral.

To address this, we used the [Chain-of-Thought](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prompt_engineering#Chain-of-thought>) (COT) reasoning technique. This technique enhanced LLMs’ performance in scenarios with conflicting sentiments by encouraging the model to articulate its reasoning process step by step. This allowed the model to weigh different emotional cues before arriving at a conclusion. For example, when analyzing feedback with mixed feelings about a service, COT helped clarify whether the overall sentiment leaned positive or negative based on the arguments presented.

## **Custom evaluation metrics**

After developing the text summarization and sentiment analysis process, it was crucial to thoroughly evaluate its effectiveness to ensure that the generated summaries and insights were both precise and actionable without losing their essence. We adopted the approach we describe as follows for this critical evaluation.

### **Creating the prime synopsis and score card**

To comprehensively measure the performance of the summarization process, we leveraged traditional metrics like [ROUGE](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ROUGE_\(metric\)>) and [BLEU](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BLEU>) scores, creating two distinct and insightful views:

* **Prime synopsis** : This high-level summary was meticulously derived from the summaries of individual text clusters, distilling the entire text corpus into one cohesive and comprehensive overview. It served as a benchmark for assessing the quality and completeness of the generated summaries.
* **Score card** : The score card captured key topics from the text data and mapped each one to its associated sentiment (positive, negative, or neutral). This structured view of sentiment across different topics provided a clear and nuanced picture of the data’s emotional landscape. By visualizing the sentiment distribution, we could swiftly identify areas of concern and satisfaction, enabling more informed and strategic decision-making.

![Figure 2: Score card Illustration.](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:700/1*tpIxEv2spc3-jzSd-3tk2w.png)

To evaluate the consistency and completeness of generated summaries and topics, we used the prime synopsis and score card. We ensured that the golden summary aligned with key topics and sentiments in the score card, leveraging LLMs for consistency checks. Topic coverage was another crucial metric, assessing how well key topics were represented in the golden summary. By using these metrics, we refined our text summarization tool, enhancing the quality of outputs. These evaluation methods were vital for business stakeholders, ensuring reliable and comprehensive insights from text datasets, which were essential for informed decision-making.

For visualization, we used an intuitive and interactive framework that organized insights, making it easy to explore and navigate through the key findings and texts. In this article we refer to it as Distilled Insight Navigation Tree (DINT).

## **Learnings and challenges**

Throughout our journey to create an effective text summarization tool, we faced several challenges and learned important lessons. Handling noisy data, such as typos, slang, and irrelevant information, required robust preprocessing techniques to ensure the algorithms focused on the core meaning. Balancing summary quality and completeness was another key challenge, which we addressed by leveraging the golden summary approach and the score card to systematically evaluate and refine the summaries. Additionally, we encountered consistency issues with LLMs, where relevant topics sometimes failed to appear in the final summaries or sentiments were misclassified. To improve consistency and reliability, we employed custom evaluation metrics like topic coverage and consistency checks, iteratively fine-tuning the prompts and evaluation techniques to enhance the accuracy and relevance of the summaries.
