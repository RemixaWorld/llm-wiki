## Introduction

Transfusion is a new model developed by Meta’s team for generating both text and images using a unified model. It is pretrained on an equal mix of text and image data, applying different objectives: next token prediction for text and diffusion for images. The model processes both modalities together, with text converted into vectors and images represented as patch sequences. Causal attention is used for text, and bidirectional attention for images. For inference, the model combines techniques from text and image generation.

Compared to Chameleon’s approach[[link](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09818#>)], Transfusion scales more effectively across all modalities. In text-to-image tasks, Transfusion achieves better results at a fraction of the computational cost, with improved FID and CLIP scores. In image-to-text tasks, it matches Chameleon’s performance with just 21.8% of the compute. Even in text-only tasks, Transfusion reaches similar perplexity with only 50% to 60% of Chameleon’s computational load.

Ablation studies show that intra-image bidirectional attention is vital for performance, as replacing it with causal attention reduces effectiveness in text-to-image tasks. Adding U-Net blocks for image processing allows the model to compress images efficiently, cutting serving costs by up to 64 times with minimal impact on performance.

Transfusion’s capabilities are further demonstrated by training a 7B parameter transformer on a large-scale dataset with 1T text tokens and 1T image patches. The model generates images on par with top diffusion models like DALL-E 2 and SDXL, while also achieving text generation performance similar to Llama 1. This highlights Transfusion’s potential as a powerful multi-modal model for generating both text and images.

## Diffusion

Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs) are a type of generative model that focus on learning to reverse a noise-adding process applied to data. While language models often handle discrete tokens, DDPMs are designed for continuous data, making them ideal for image processing. The framework comprises two main processes:

1. **Forward Process** : A method for systematically adding Gaussian noise to data, eventually transforming it into pure noise.
2. **Reverse Process** : A denoising mechanism where the model learns to reconstruct the original data from its noisy version.

## Transfusion

Transfusion is an innovative training method that enables a unified model to handle both discrete (like text) and continuous (like images) data types. The key breakthrough of this approach is the use of distinct loss functions tailored to each modality — employing language modeling for text and diffusion processes for images — while sharing the same underlying data and model parameters. This allows the model to effectively understand and generate across different modalities within a single framework. Above figure gives a complete idea of Transfusion.

**Data Prep**

Data representation in this approach spans both discrete text and continuous images. Text is tokenized into integer sequences based on a fixed vocabulary. Images are encoded into continuous vector patches using a Variational Autoencoder (VAE), arranged sequentially. For mixed-modal data, image sequences are enclosed with special BOI (Beginning of Image) and EOI (End of Image) tokens before being merged into the text sequence, resulting in a combined sequence that includes both discrete and continuous elements.

**Model Architecture**

The model primarily relies on a single transformer to process sequences across different modalities. It operates on high-dimensional vectors and uses modality-specific components to convert data into this vector space. For text, embedding matrices map integers to vectors and vectors back to discrete distributions. For images, two methods compress k x k patch vectors into a transformer vector: a linear layer and U-Net up and down blocks.

Source: Meta’s [paper](<https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2408.11039>)

**Transfusion Attention**

Transfusion Attention integrates two attention mechanisms to effectively manage both text and images. Language models use causal masking to process sequences without future token information, ideal for text’s sequential structure. Images, on the other hand, typically use bidirectional attention for full interaction within the image. Transfusion applies causal attention across the sequence and bidirectional attention within each image. This allows each image patch to fully interact within its own image while only attending to preceding text or images in the sequence, leading to significant performance improvements.

**Training Objective**

The training objective for the model integrates two loss functions: language modeling loss LLM​ for text tokens and diffusion loss LDDPM​ for image patches. The language modeling loss is computed per token, while the diffusion loss is calculated per image, which may include multiple patches. Noise is added to the latent image according to the diffusion process before it is divided into patches, and then the image-level diffusion loss is computed. This approach represents a specific instance of a broader strategy that combines discrete and continuous distribution losses within a single model. Future work might explore alternatives, such as replacing diffusion with flow matching.

**Optimization**

The optimization uses AdamW with parameters β1=0.9, β2=0.95, =0.9 and ϵ= 1×10^–8. The learning rate starts at 3×10^–4, warmed up over 4000 steps, and decays to 1.5×10^–5 with a cosine scheduler. Training involves 4096-token sequences in 2M-token batches for 250k steps, totaling 0.5T tokens. Larger experiments use 4M-token batches over 500k steps, reaching 2T tokens. Regularization includes a 0.1 weight decay and gradient clipping at norm 1.0. The coefficient λ is set to 5, with future tuning planned.

**Inference**

The inference process alternates between language modeling (LM) and diffusion modes. In **LM mode** , tokens are sampled sequentially from predicted distribution. When a Beginning of Image (BOI) token appears, the model switches to **diffusion mode** , appending noise xT​ as image patches and denoising over T steps. The model predicts and updates the sequence at each step, conditioning on the last timestep. After generating the image, an End of Image (EOI) token is added, and the model returns to LM mode. This method enables seamless generation of mixed text and image sequences.

## Conclusion

This review paper has explored key aspects of a model designed to integrate both discrete and continuous modalities, with a focus on text and image processing. By leveraging innovations like Transfusion attention, modality-specific components, and a unified training objective, the model demonstrates the potential for effective cross-modal learning. The optimization strategies, including the use of AdamW and careful scheduling of learning rates, further contribute to the model’s robustness and scalability. As this field continues to evolve, there remain opportunities for enhancing these models, such as refining loss functions and exploring alternative methods like flow matching. The work reviewed here lays a strong foundation for future advancements in multimodal learning and offers valuable insights into the complexities of integrating diverse data types within a single model architecture.
