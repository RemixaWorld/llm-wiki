## How GPT Rose to Power in Modern AI Revolution

Before we do a deep dive into Adversarial training on GPT, let us first understand “ _What is GPT?_ ” or “ _What is ChatGPT?_ ”. OpenAI introduced a teaser for the well known chat language model back in 2018 that is, in this modern AI era, more than capable of just “ _chatting_ ” with its chat interface known as ChatGPT. The underlying models that are actually generating the content are referred to as GPT models, where GPT stands for **G** enerative **P** re-trained **T** ransformer.

### The Origins of GPT

The first version of GPT which was released in 2018 and contained 117 million parameters marked the beginning of GPT series, was developed as a proof-of-concept for pre-training transformer model. It followed the approach of unsupervised pre-training it on a large corpus of textual data that is used for learning the vocabulary and grammar, basically like _a kid learning basics in kindergarten_. Then, it can be supervised fine-tuned for downstream tasks like question answering, translation, summarization and many more.

This initial iteration had several limitations and it mainly laid the foundation for its successors. The following year, in 2019, OpenAI scaled up the architecture for GPT-1 and introduced GPT-2 with more parameters and a dataset way larger than its predecessor. With 1.5 billion parameters and 48 transformer layers, it demonstrated remarkable capabilities in generating coherent, contextual and human-like responses in conversations. _The second blog from this series covers the fundamentals of transformers and GPT-2 in great detail. Make sure to_[ _check it out_](</dsc-vit-bhopal/understanding-gpt-2-the-evolution-of-transformers-in-ai-464d94164baa>) _if you’re interested._

### Breaking the Internet with GPT-3

GPT-2 was a great success and signification milestone for OpenAI but came with several limitations. Its shortcomings can be noted briefly, especially when the text generation is longer than a couple paragraphs and starts lacking coherence. It lacked factual knowledge and often had a tendency to generate biased or offensive text. It couldn’t answer questions well enough as compared to other platforms and systems that relied on algorithms to extract and review information. It was vulnerable to adversarial prompts and often struggled with a phenomenon known as [hallucinations](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.05232>).

To address these limitations from GPT-2 and enhance the capabilities of GPT models, GPT-3 was launched in May 2020. It was introduced progressively to fix underlying issues and prevent possible language model misuse. It was **THE** innovation that made the world aware of Artificial Intelligence and was accessible worldwide for use **FOR FREE**! The model architecture was bumped up to 175 billion parameters and 96 transformer layers. It was trained on a huge and diverse dataset including web pages, books, articles and programming codes. It handled tasks like translation, question answering and creative writing fluently and with coherence that made life of people easier and even threatened some existing jobs. _We, students, would be in a lot of misery if not for ChatGPT. ### How GPT-3 became a global hit

There are hundreds, even thousands, of articles and blogs on the internet stating the general information on GPT-3. This blog, however, aims to dig a bit deeper on how this iteration made _Large Language Models_ a worldwide blockbuster!

**Larger models make increasingly efficient use of in-context information.** Source: [OpenAI](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.14165>).

> During unsupervised pre-training, a language model develops a broad set of skills and pattern recognition abilities. It then uses these abilities at inference time to rapidly adapt to or recognize the desired task. We use the term “in-context learning” to describe the inner loop of this process, which occurs within the forward-pass upon each sequence.
>
> _Source:_[_OpenAI_](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.14165>)

In-context learning (ICL) is a technique for Large Language Models that enables them to perform and learn new tasks from the examples provided in the prompt. It leverages the ability of the model to understand patterns from the given query. _It is equivalent of asking programmers to write a technical blog about the work they‘ve done._ ICL consists of three approaches for guiding the model’s output without changing its internal parameters.

* **Few-Shot (FS)** is a technique where the model is prompted with instructions and a few sets of examples, typically ranging from 10 to 100 examples. However, modifying the internal model parameters is not allowed. The instructions are structured as natural language tasks, enabling the model to interpret it with ease.
* **One-Shot (1S)** is similar to Few-Shot with the key distinction being that it relies on a single example, just as the name suggests. It was introduced to mimic the way tasks are passed to humans.
* **Zero-Shot (0S)** is same as One-Shot but the prompt does not include any examples or demonstrations. It is the most challenging setting with the model pre-trained on such a large corpus of data which often leads to tasks being misinterpreted easily.

**Zero-shot, one-shot and few-shot, contrasted with traditional fine-tuning.** Source: [OpenAI](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.14165>).

Fine-tuning has been widely used in recent years to enable any machine learning model to incline towards a specific task. This technique involves progressive modification of internal model parameters with supervised dataset. For this, thousands of labelled examples are used to improve the model parameters. In-context learning has a slight advantage from this technique with the models having the ability of understanding instructions with few to no examples and can adapt to perform several different tasks flawlessly. Otherwise, fine-tuning can be computationally and resource expensive. _GPT models take advantage of in-context learning to adapt to different roles such as personal assistants for different organizations for its customers, providing customized support, content creation, programming help and a lot more variety of tasks. Just like plug and play. One model for all tasks._ The main disadvantage of ICL is that it gives much worse results than the state-of-the-art fine tuned models.

## Understanding Adversarial Training

Let’s talk about adversarial training, the main objective of this blog! For the beginners in the world of Artificial Intelligence, _Adversarial training is a technique which leverages adversarial examples to improve the robustness of the model. Such examples may look completely normal to our eyes but can trick a machine learning model to make incorrect and even dangerous predictions._ In layman’s terms, think of it like “ _Don’t take anything from strangers._ ” — _Parents._ But, in adversarial training, we go a step further and teach the model how to recognize and deal with those strangers safely.

### **History**

_This one’s an honorable mention._ One of the first models that made adversarial training famous was **Generative Adversarial Network** (GANs). It comprises of a Generator and a Discriminator. The Generator takes random noise as input and tries to generate data. The Discriminator takes in the generated and real data, and tries to tell whether the generated data is counterfeit or real. Both, the Generator and Discriminator follows a min-max approach where the former tries to minimize the probability of generated data to be detected as counterfeit and the latter maximizes its chances to distinguish real and fake.

It‘s the part of adversarial training that focuses on generating realistic samples, and not a method for making model resistant to adversarial inputs._

To improve model robustness, techniques like Fast Gradient Sign Method (FGSM) and Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) were introduced to manipulate the inputs relying on loss gradient that can _fool_ the model to predict incorrect outputs.

### Adversarial Attacks

The adversarial attacks can roughly be categorized in three parts: White-box, Grey-box and Black-box.

* **White-box:** In this system, the model architecture and every internal working of the model is known to the attacker. Techniques like FGSM falls under this category.
* **Grey-box:** Only a part information about the model is known. The attacker can’t fully interpret the model fully. RLHF (Reinforcement Learning with Human Feedback) refines the model training with human feedback which is used from GPT-4 onwards.
* **Black-box:** No internal knowledge of the model is disclosed. The inputs and the outputs are the only things that can be interacted with. For text-based models, text attack techniques manipulate the inputs for adversarial training.

White-box attacks have the most efficient examples that can lead to better robustness in models, whereas the black-box attacks provide more generalization to the model.

In the later sections, the blog includes adversarial training setup for White-box and Black-box techniques. Before that, let’s understand their theory behind the attacks.

### Text Attack in NLP

Text Attack techniques employ the use of text manipulation to serve as inputs. These manipulation are performed directly on the input text, therefore, they are Black-box attack. For this blog, Deep Word Bug and Text Fooler are two different techniques used to generate adversarial samples.

**Deep Word Bug** , proposed by [Ji Gao](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.04354>) in 2018, follows a simple character-level manipulation technique. It randomly performs character swap, insertion, deletion and substitution. Another minor variation is the case flipping. These changes are often small and often imperceptible.

Press enter or click to view image in full size

Deep Bug Word example. Source: [Black-box Generation of Adversarial Text Sequences to Evade Deep Learning Classifiers](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.04354>)

Another technique used is **Text Fooler** by [Di Jin](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.11932>), introduced in 2019. It is a word-level attack that leverages semantic similarity. It replaces certain words with similar ones that have the same meaning.

Text Fooler example. Source: [Is BERT Really Robust? A Strong Baseline for Natural Language Attack on Text Classification and Entailment](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.11932>)

FGSM training data distribution

The FGSM attack function generates the perturbed embeddings, and the model parameters are initialized as usual. Alpha is set to `0.5` to favor clean accuracy to prevent model from forgetting its original results. Most importantly, the model is switched to training mode.

```python from torch.optim import AdamW# FGSM formuladef fgsm_attack(embeddings, epsilon, data_grad): sign_data_grad = data_grad.sign() perturbed_embeddings = embeddings + epsilon * sign_data_grad return perturbed_embeddingsoptimizer = AdamW(model.parameters(), lr=2e-5) # AdamW optimizer with 2e-5 learning ratemodel.train() # Set mode to trainingepsilon = 0.1 # Epsilon for FGSMalpha = 0.5 # total loss = clean loss + alpha * perturbed loss ```

The last step is to train the model for one or two epochs as fine-tuning. Just like before, we create perturbed embeddings using the same method. We first call `loss_clean.backward(retain_graph=True)` to get the gradients from the clean loss and keep the computation graph around for the next step. Then, we do another backward pass with `total_loss.backward()`, which includes both the clean and perturbed losses. This way, the model updates its weights based on the combined loss, helping it improve on both clean and perturbed inputs.

``` for epoch in range(1): # 1 epoch loop = tqdm(dataloader, desc=f"Epoch {epoch + 1}") # Training step visualization # Accuracy correct_clean = 0 correct_adv = 0 total = 0 for batch in loop: # Batched training # Input IDs, Attention mask and labels input_ids = batch["input_ids"].to(device) attention_mask = batch["attention_mask"].to(device) labels = batch["label"].to(device) optimizer.zero_grad() # Reset gradient embeddings = model.get_input_embeddings()(input_ids) # Clean embeddings embeddings.requires_grad_() # Compute gradient embeddings.retain_grad() # Keep gradient outputs_clean = model(inputs_embeds=embeddings, attention_mask=attention_mask, labels=labels) # Clean output loss_clean = outputs_clean.loss # Clean loss loss_clean.backward(retain_graph=True) # Backprop and calculate gradient data_grad = embeddings.grad.detach() # No gradient calculation perturbed_embeddings = fgsm_attack(embeddings, epsilon, data_grad) # Perturbed embeddings outputs_perturbed = model(inputs_embeds=perturbed_embeddings, attention_mask=attention_mask, labels=labels) # Adversarial outputs loss_perturbed = outputs_perturbed.loss # Adversarial loss total_loss = loss_clean + alpha * loss_perturbed # Total loss = Clean loss + Alpha * Adversarial loss optimizer.zero_grad() # Reset gradients total_loss.backward() # Calculate gradients for total loss optimizer.step() # Update gradients # Logits pred_clean = outputs_clean.logits.argmax(dim=1) pred_adv = outputs_perturbed.logits.argmax(dim=1) # Accuracy calculation correct_clean += (pred_clean == labels).sum().item() correct_adv += (pred_adv == labels).sum().item() total += labels.size(0) loop.set_postfix(loss=total_loss.item(), acc_clean=100 * correct_clean / total, acc_adv=100 * correct_adv / total) # Update epoch visualization ```

## The Next Steps…

FGSM is considered a simple white-box attack, and one drawback is that the model can easily overfit to its specific perturbed examples. To better balance clean and adversarial accuracy, here are a few improvements that could help:

* **Adjusting Clean and Adversarial Loss Weightage**
* **Reduce epsilon** : A value of 0.1 may be too aggressive. Reduce it to a lower value, start from 0.1 and reduce gradually, or take random values between 0 and 0.1 every training step.
* **Curriculum Adversarial Training** : Start with low epsilon and gradually increasing it.
* **Use Harder White-box attacks** : Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) and its variations, CW (Carlini & Wagner), DeepFool and many more.

The Text attack techniques only aims to provide robustness in the type and level of attack, and that is to be expected. To improve this:

* **Generate multiple adversaries from a single text**
* **Use multiple text attack strategies in combination** : Bert Attack, Text Bugger, Probability Weighted Word Saliency (PWWS) and many more.

To further strengthen the robustness of the model, try combining both the white-box and black-box attacks.
