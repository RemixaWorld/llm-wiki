# How to prepare an instruction dataset to fine-tune LLM?

Fine-tuning large language models (LLMs) on custom datasets is a popular technique to adapt these powerful models for specific downstream tasks (see [this blog](</p/3d2dff670de3>) to learn what tasks benefit from fine-tuning). However, research has shown that simply providing more training data does not necessarily lead to better fine-tuning performance [1]. In fact, some studies have demonstrated strong fine-tuning results with very small datasets, given high-quality and diverse examples [2]. The key to successfully fine-tuning seems to lie more in carefully curating the instruction dataset rather than maximizing raw size [3]. However, precisely defining data quality and optimal variation for the target domain remains an open question. In this post, I will summarize insights from recent studies on how certain data properties and task similarities impact fine-tuning success.

**Defining High Quality Training Data**

A critical aspect of preparing datasets for LLM fine-tuning is the careful selection and curation of high-quality training data. The definition of “high-quality” can vary depending on the specific goals and requirements of the project. However, a common thread is the need for data that is **relevant** , **accurate** , and **diverse** , contributing to a model’s ability to understand and generate meaningful, informative responses.

An illuminating example of such meticulous dataset preparation can be found in the process used for the LIMA paper [2]. The researchers curated a set of 1000 examples, drawing from top questions and answers across renowned community forums like Stack Exchange and WikiHow, as well as crafting manual examples themselves. This approach was not just about gathering large amounts of data but selecting content that was inherently valuable and representative of real user interactions.

It’s worth to notice that the traditional notion of data augmentation through slight variations — such as paraphrasing or minor alterations in the dataset — often falls short in contributing to true diversity. This limitation stems from the exceptional capability of LLMs as few-shot learners, which can rapidly assimilate and generalize from minimal examples. For LLMs, diversity must encapsulate variations in the context, complexity, and formulation of tasks, ensuring that the model encounters a comprehensive spectrum of scenarios within the target domain.

**Evaluating and Improving Data Quality**

A rigorous data quality evaluation process helps in pinpointing gaps in the dataset, such as underrepresented instructions or scenarios, and provides insights into areas where the model may require additional tuning to improve its understanding and response generation capabilities. A recent paper MoDS [3] provides a structured framework for not only assessing but also enhancing the quality of instructional datasets through a model-oriented data selection methodology. This selection is grounded in three key criteria: **quality** , **coverage** , and **necessity**. The architecture involves a multi-step process to curate a subset of instruction data that is both high-quality and diverse, and which addresses the specific needs of the LLM being fine-tuned. More specifically, it involves four steps as below:

1. **Quality Evaluation** : Initially, the architecture employs a quality evaluation model to assess and filter out high-quality instruction data from a larger dataset. This ensures that only the most relevant and well-constructed instruction-response pairs are retained for further processing.
2. **Diverse Data Selection for Seed Instructions** : Following the quality evaluation, a k-center greedy algorithm is applied to the high-quality subset to select a seed dataset. This algorithm aims to maximize the diversity and coverage of the dataset by choosing data points that are maximally distant from each other. The selected seed dataset provides a broad representation of instructions, which is crucial for effectively training the LLM.
3. **Augmented Data Selection** : After fine-tuning the LLM with the seed dataset, the initial model’s performance on the high-quality dataset is assessed. This evaluation identifies instructions that the model struggles with, deemed necessary for further improving the model’s capabilities. An augmented dataset is then compiled, consisting of these necessary instructions, to address the identified gaps in the LLM’s instruction-following abilities.
4. **Fine-tuning with Selected Data** : Finally, the LLM is fine-tuned again using a combination of the seed and augmented datasets. This process ensures that the model not only has a broad understanding of various instructions but also excels in areas where it initially lacked proficiency.

![MoDS architecture](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:700/1*sAsIxfnxWBwGKN_pDgIPZQ.png)

The MoDS approach presents a sophisticated method for selecting instructional data, assuming the availability of a large initial dataset. However, this assumption can be a significant limitation, especially for projects or models where extensive, high-quality instructional datasets are not readily available. In many practical scenarios, the volume of data required by MoDS to effectively filter, evaluate, and enhance through its methodology may not be present, posing challenges in applying this approach universally.

**Best Practices for Dataset Creation**

Facing the hurdles of gathering substantial, high-quality instructional datasets necessitates the deployment of strategic best practices in dataset creation. Here are essential tips to guide you:

1. Start with What You Have: Begin by aggregating all available instructional data, regardless of size. Even small datasets can be invaluable if they are of high quality and relevance.
2. Focus on Quality Over Quantity: Prioritize the curation of high-quality examples. This involves meticulous review and potential refinement of each data point to ensure it meets the desired standards of clarity, relevance, and informativeness.
3. Diversify Your Sources: Expand your dataset by incorporating instructional data from a variety of sources. This can include public forums, educational materials, and domain-specific literature. The diversity of sources can help mitigate the volume limitations by enriching the dataset’s coverage and variety.
4. Synthetic Data Generation: Consider using smaller, high-quality datasets as seeds for generating synthetic instructional data. Advanced language models can be fine-tuned to produce additional instructional content that mirrors the quality and style of the original data.
5. Iterative Refinement: Adopt an iterative approach to dataset curation, where initial model performances on smaller datasets inform subsequent rounds of data collection and refinement. This iterative cycle can help in progressively building a more comprehensive and effective dataset.
