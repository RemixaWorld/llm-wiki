Existing RAG benchmarks evaluates if LLMs can correctly answer the general knowledge, however they are unable to evaluate the effectiveness of the RAG system in dealing with the data from different vertical domains

To address it, this paper[1] introduces RAGEval, a framework for automatically generating evaluation datasets to evaluate the knowledge usage ability of different LLMs in different scenarios.

Key contributions:

* introduces RAGEval, a novel framework for automatically generating and scenario-specific datasets to assess the capabilities of RAG systems
* introduces three keypoint-based evaluation metrics and two adapted retrieval metrics, aiming to provide a more comprehensive assessment of the RAG pipeline

## Method

* RAGEval employs a “schema-configuration-document-QAR-keypoint” pipeline, focused on utilization of factual information and enhancing the robustness of answer estimation to improve the accuracy and reliability of the evaluation process
* components of this pipeline in detail in the figure below and following sub-sections

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:700/1*UTdWEAXJqEYVlor6pX-7WA.png)

### i) Stage 1: Schema Summary

* employ LLMs to perform inductive reasoning based on a small set of seed texts
* seed texts may share the same type, they can vary greatly in style and specific factual content, like, For instance, financial reports might cover diverse industries from internet technology to food processing
* This approach ensures the validity and comprehensiveness of the derived schema, enhancing the controllability of text generation and enabling the production of coherent, domain-appropriate content that aligns with the structural patterns of specific document types

### ii) Stage 2: Document Generation

* first generate configurations C derived from the schema S established in Stage 1, rather than immediately producing the text
* These configurations directly correspond to the types and content of factual information, serving as a reference and constraint for text generation
* To generate these configurations, we employ a hybrid approach combining rule-based and LLM-based methods to assign values to the schema elements
* Rule-based value generation employs programmatic algorithms to create or select values, ensuring high accuracy, factual consistency, and efficiency, particularly for structured data like dates or categorical information
* leverage LLMs to generate more complex or diverse content as LLMs excel at creating varied and nuanced information, particularly for elements that require natural language understanding or creativity, such as detailed descriptions or complex relational information
* integrate configuration details into a structured narrative format appropriate to the domain, incorporating domain-specific prior knowledge to ensure accuracy and contextual relevance

### iii) Stage 3: QRA Generation

* Question-Reference-Answer (QRA) triples are generated using the given documents D and configurations C
* process is outlined as follows:

**a) Utilizing Configurations for Questions and Initial Answers Generation**

* leverage the configurations C to guide the generation of questions and initial answers
* configurations are embedded within the prompts to ensure that the generated questions are specific and the answers are precise
* As shown in table below, it address seven types of questions: factual questions, multi-hop reasoning questions, summarization questions, multidocument questions, and others

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:700/1*GDZRfGWJ_kSpH6MiqQiL7w.png)
* input to the GPT-4o model includes detailed instructions and a few examples for each question type, which are tailored according to the configurations
* This results in questions Q and initial answers A that are more targeted and accurate

**b) Extracting References**

* Using the constructed questions Q and initial answers A, we extract relevant information fragments (references) R from the articles by utilizing an extracting prompt
* prompt used emphasize the importance of grounding answers in the source material to ensure reliability and traceability.

**c) Optimizing Answers and References**

refinement process follows these principles:

* If the references R contain content not present in the initial answers A, we supplement the answers accordingly
* Conversely, if the initial answers A contain content not found in the references R, we first check the article for any overlooked references. If such references are found, we add them to the reference set and keep the answer unchanged
* If no corresponding references are found, we remove the irrelevant content from the answer.

**d) Generating Keypoints**

* generate keypoints from the standard answers A for each question Q.
* for generating keypoints, employ a predefined prompt for the GPT-4o model, which supports both Chinese and English
* prompt used is designed using in-context learning, providing examples of keypoint extraction across different domains and question types, including cases where the answer is unanswerable, and the keypoints reflect that appropriately.
* responses are distilled into 3–5 keypoints, encompassing indispensable factual information, relevant inferences, and final conclusions necessary to answer the question.

## DRAGONBall Dataset

* Leveraging the aforementioned generation method, authors construct the DRAGONBall dataset, which stands for Diverse RAG Omni-Benchmark for All domains
* This dataset encompasses a wide array of texts and related RAG questions across three critical domains: finance, law, and medical. Moreover, the dataset includes both Chinese and English texts, providing a comprehensive resource for multilingual and domain-specific research.
* For document generation, the dataset includes texts from 20 different corporate domains in finance, with one randomly selected text per domain; 10 different legal domains, with two randomly selected texts per domain; and 19 major medical categories, each with two subcategories and one randomly selected text per major category.

## Evaluation Metrics for RAG Systems

### i) Retrieval Metrics

**a) Recall**

* RAG Retrieval Recall metric is used to evaluate the effectiveness of the retrieval process in matching ground truth references
* Recall is formally defined as

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:263/1*cJilvF3Cbowq7NTmuAu9QQ.png)

where n is the total number of ground truth references, Gi denotes the i-th ground truth reference,R = {R1,R2, . . . ,Rk} represents the set of retrieved references,M(Gi,R) is a boolean function that returns true if all sentences in Gi are found in at least one reference in R, and false otherwise, and I(·) is the indicator function, returning 1 if the condition is true and 0 otherwise

* This metric assesses the alignment between retrieved and ground truth references at the sentence level

**b) Effective Information Rate (EIR)**

* This metric quantifies the proportion of relevant information within the retrieved passages, ensuring that the retrieval process is both accurate and efficient in terms of information content
* It is calculated as

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:208/1*-nc_tYRVjDPn17I6QCGFyg.png)

where Gi is the i-th ground truth reference, Rt is the set of total retrieved passages, m is the number of ground truth references successfully matched. |Gi ∩ Rt| represents the number of words in the intersection of the i-th ground truth reference and the concatenated retrieved passages Rt

### ii) Generation Metrics

**a) Completeness**

* measures how well the generated answer captures the key information from the ground truth
* Completeness score is then calculated as the proportion of key points(generated by a LLM) semantically covered by the generated answer A:

## Quality Assessment

* introduce the human verification process used to assess the quality of the generated dataset and the evaluation, with assessment divided into following three main tasks:

### i) QAR Quality Assessment

* ask annotators to assess the quality of the QARs by scoring th correctness of the QARs generated under different configurations, according to the standards listed in figure below

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:507/1*v6ffZ9k67qqsGdFTF0aP1w.png)
* randomly select 10 samples per question type for every language and domain, resulting in 420 samples in total for annotation
* When scoring, annotators are provided with the document, question, question type, generated response, and references
* Results in table below indicate that the QAR quality scores are consistently high across different domains, with slight variations between languages

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:356/1*4y-v_X1E55xtL51PtRmlgw.png)

### ii) Generated Document Quality Assessment

* authors evaluated the quality of the documents generated using RAGEval by comparing them with documents generated using baseline methods, which include zero-shot prompting
* randomly select 20, 20, and 19 generated documents for finance, legal, and medical domain for both language, respectively, and pack each document with 2 baseline documents generated by zero- and one-shot prompting into one group for comparison
* Annotators are asked to rank the documents in each group in terms of clarity, safety, richness, and conformity, as outlined in figure below with ties allowed

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:509/1*ttovgQyhzDPUxzQpnuC5Bg.png)
* Results in figure below demonstrate that our method consistently outperforms zero-shot and one-shot methods across all criteria, particularly in safety, clarity, conformity, and richness

* To validate the consistency between LLM and human evaluations, we compare the completeness, hallucination, and irrelevance metrics reported by the LLM with those reported by humans
* use the same 420 examples from the QAR quality assessment and ask human annotators to judge the answers from Baichuan-2–7B-chat

## Experimental Evaluations

### i) Generation Performance Comparison

* experimental results of the different generation models are outlined in table below

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:700/1*qDB4m7E9BCz3hMiiLrNbOQ.png)
* based on the Rouge-L metric, Baichuan-2–7B-chat achieved the best performance with scores of 0.3262 (CN) and 0.3039 (EN), while GPT-4o performed almost the worst with scores of 0.1527 (CN) and 0.2190 (EN)
* GPT-4o performed better on the Completeness, Hallucination, and Irrelevance metrics, proving that our proposed keypoints based evaluation metrics can indeed better and more comprehensively reflect model performance in RAG scenarios
* Among models in 7B-8B range and also in open source models, Llama3–8B-Instruct led other models with the highest score of 0.6524 for Completeness in English evals
* Although GPT-4o currently shows the best performance overall, the gap with the top-performing open-source models is not substantial.

### ii) Retrieval Performance Comparison

* Table below presents the performance results of various retrieval models on both Chinese (CN) and English (EN) datasets

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:700/1*yeyFpElDNnKFNxNJNLmHmQ.png)
* In English setting, GTE-Large model demonstrated superior performance across several key metrics, achieving a Recall of 0.7542 and EIR of 0.1372, indicating its robustness in retrieving relevant information with minimal noise.

### iii) Hyperparameter Comparison

**a) TopK Retrieval Observations**

* Recall Improvement with Higher TopK: Recall improved with higher TopK values. Specifically, Recall increased from 0.5685 at TopK=2 to 0.7542 at TopK=6 in English
* Generation Metrics Improve with Increased Recall: improvements in Recall due to increased TopK values are reflected in the generation metrics, demonstrating a positive correlation between retrieval performance and generation quality

**b) Chunk Size Impact**

* Optimal Chunk Size Varies by Language: as observed in table below, observe that the optimal chunk size varies varies between Chinese and English. For Chinese, the 128–8 setting (128 tokens, 8 chunks) performed best in Recall (0.5031) and EIR (0.0884), while for English, the 256–4 setting achieved the highest Recall (0.6161) and EIR (0.2628)

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:700/1*VdxmsFeENXKcQaWxU3pbAg.png)
* Trade-offs in Generation Metrics:best retrieval performance doesn’t always
* translate to the best generation metrics: For Chinese, the 512–2 setting achieved the highest Completeness (0.4932) despite lower Recall, while for English, the 128–8 setting led in Completeness (0.6683).
* Balancing Retrieval and Generation Performance:While smaller chunks (128-8) generally led to better retrieval metrics and lower hallucination, larger chunks sometimes improved completeness, This suggests that the optimal chunk size should be chosen based on the specific requirements of the task.

## Conclusion

* introduces RAGEval, a novel framework for automatically generating and scenario-specific datasets to assess the capabilities of RAG systems
* approach addresses the limitations of existing benchmarks by focusing on factual accuracy and scenario-specific knowledge, which are crucial in industries like finance, healthcare, and legal sectors
* experimental results demonstrate that the new metrics we designed provide a more comprehensive and accurate assessment of model performance in RAG scenarios compared to conventional metrics
