A few years back, I thought AI music platforms might make music creators irrelevant — but nope, not at all. The real issue isn’t AI itself, but how newer musicians understand the industry, especially when it comes to rights. If a song is fully AI-generated, ownership might be yours, but credit as a composer or writer won’t be given unless some tweaks or personal touches are added. After all, how can a person be called a producer if nothing was actually produced?

It doesn’t really matter which tools, AI or not, are used to make songs — if some workarounds have been applied, a contributor credit will still be assigned. This might not be a big deal for folks making tracks for background use, but it’s definitely something to think about if you’re planning to release music publicly.

**A few things to keep in mind:**

* Most record labels may want some sort of proof of your music-making process. This could be as simple as a video capture of the song making process.
* Now, imagine a song goes viral and the goal is to generate buzz with a remix contest. How can the stems be provided — both dry and clean wet versions?
* And for anyone trying to release their music on Apple Music, there’s a new rule, starting from January 2025: you need to include at least three credits assigned to the song during submission.

**The big question is:** _how to become a writer, performer, producer, and engineer all in one?_

With AI tools, it’s totally possible. The trick isn’t to generate AI music and call it a day. Nope, that’s actually just the starting point. Once the AI-generated track is ready, the focus shifts to jumping in, making adjustments, and adding personal touches to truly take ownership of the song.

## This is gonna be the process cycle:

### Making the lyric

With AI tools like ChatGPT, creating lyrics is now easier than ever. To make them sound even more human, some creativity is key. For example, when developing pop song lyrics, try using a prompt like this:

**_“Generate a pop lyric about a 22-year-old girl breaking up with her boyfriend. Include shots, hooks, informal language, 2 verses, a pre-chorus, and a chorus”._**

Once the result is ready, refine any words or phrases that don’t quite vibe with the vision. This can easily be done through tools such as ChatGPT, but for something a bit more tailored to songwriting, platforms like LyricStudio, also AI-driven, may prove more useful over time.

**This is a very important step** because it is not easy to change lyrics afterwards. Take all the time and tweak and adjust as much as possible until the lyrics feel just right.

### Time to generate the music itself…

Now, the moment of click-click: **music generation**. There’s a lot of AI music generators, but let’s now talk about **Suno**. There, it’s possible to create music, cover existing songs, upload tracks, separate vocals from the song, crop sections, and even replace parts — essentially functioning as a full editor, powered by AI.

While Suno delivers good-quality music and vocals, stems — particularly vocals — are often needed for things like remix contests, as mentioned earlier.

**_Suno can create stems, but here’s the thing:_**

1. **they are extracted from the audio output** , not pre-generation, due to how the system is trained.
2. **Suno only provides two stems:** vocals and instrumental. Again, that’s not ideal for more flexibility.

So, download that Suno-generated track and move to the other platform for better stem generation. Several options like BandLab, VocalRemover, Splitter, and Fadr are available.

Fadr, for instance, is particularly interesting since it doesn’t just give multiple stems but also provides the corresponding MIDI files.

The goal here? Get three things: **the vocals, the instrumentals, and the MIDI files for the next steps.**

1. Take that isolated vocal stem and upload it onto **Audimee** to choose the best singer for the track. That is where Audimee allows you to choose between original singers, which means singers who have been exclusively trained by Audimee, not cloned voices. This gives you a sound that is more authentic and unique.
2. If there is a particular melody that you like in the instrumental, [**replace it**](<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/Sb7Lf3qdN1k>) with an instrument, like guitar, sax, something that goes. And voila- almost there!
3. For added variation, working on the MIDI files in an editor program is more than sufficient.

### **Last step is arranging, mixing, and mastering.**

* The arrangement can be done online with **BandLab** or offline using a digital workstation like **GarageBand** or **FL Studio**. This is where the intro, drop, outro, and other sections are fine-tuned to perfection.
* No need to stress over mixing — AI plugins like **Izotope Neutron** handle most of the heavy lifting. Neutron uses AI to auto-mix all your tracks, listening to your music, analyzing it, and applying the best settings for each track based on its individual sound and role in the mix. It’s like having a virtual mixing engineer, helping to achieve a polished sound without having to tweak every minute detail.
* Once that’s done, export the track and have it mastered using online services like **Waves** or **Landr** — just adjust a few preferences, and for only a few bucks, the track is ready.

Now, credits as a producer and engineer are duly earned. These credits are applicable wherever needed, and earnings will flow from the publishing administration. Moreover, the copyright may be registered as owned, since the song has been created from scratch.
