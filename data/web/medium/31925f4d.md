As a developer and solo entrepreneur, part of what I do is create pitch decks for my own products and reports for my clients. Over the years, this used to take a lot of time. From planning the structure to designing every single slide manually. But ever since AI tools became powerful enough to automate the entire process, I never went back to doing it the old way.

When it comes to which tool I use, it’s never the same one for long. New platforms keep appearing almost every week, and I like trying out what’s fresh and more capable.

Today, I came across a new tool called [SketchBubble AI](<https://www.sketchbubble.com/>), which can create high-quality presentation slides in seconds from just an idea or a short description.

It is packed with AI-powered features like instant content creation, AI-driven recommendations, and AI-enhanced designs.

Sketchbubble AI Presentation Maker key features

The platform follows a very simple three-step process:

1. Enter your presentation topic or idea. The more details you provide, the better your slides will be.
2. Review the presentation outline and select from a wide range of AI-curated designs.
3. Download the presentation with a single click and present using MS PowerPoint and Google Slides.

In this article, I will show you examples of how Sketchbubble works and why it’s worth checking out. This is especially useful if you regularly prepare slides for work, school, or business.

Let’s get started.

### Creating Presentation Slides with AI

To get started, head over to Sketchbubble and create an account for free.

On sign-up, you’ll get 500 AI credits, which is enough to generate around 100 slides (roughly 10 complete presentations with 10 slides each).

You can learn more about the [AI Presentation Maker](<https://www.sketchbubble.com/en/ai-presentation-maker>) on this page. Take the time to check the features and explore the designs. Once you’re ready to create the slides, click on the “Generate a Presentation Now” button.

This will redirect you to the main AI presentation maker dashboard.

Now it’s time to fill in the required parameters:

* **Presentation topic:** You can either manually describe the topic or upload a document. Again, the more information you provide, the better the result will be.
* **Number of slides:** You can generate slides between 3–15. Let’s try to create 7 slides for now.
* **Language:** Sketchbubble supports all major languages. In my example, I will stick to English.

Here’s the exact prompt I used for this example:

> **Prompt:** Create a clean, modern restaurant presentation for The Urban Spoon, highlighting its story, food, and experience. The deck should include slides for the restaurant overview, philosophy, signature dishes with photos, dining experience, chef and team, sample menu, customer reviews, upcoming plans, and contact information. Keep the tone inviting and focus on high-quality food images, minimal text, and elegant design for a professional look.

### Sketchbubble Pricing

Sketchbubble offers two main subscription options designed for different kinds of users. You can pay monthly or go with a yearly plan that gives you up to 65% in savings.

Press enter or click to view image in full size

Sketchbubble pricing

The **Pro plan** costs $50 a month and includes full access to all templates, with a limit of 20 downloads each month. It also comes with 3,000 AI credits when you sign up and supports exporting to PDF, PowerPoint, or Google Slides. It’s a practical choice if you often use ready-made templates or need full creative control over your slides.

The **AI Only plan** is priced at $15 per month. It focuses on the AI Presentation Maker and is meant for users who prefer generating slides through automation. This plan also includes 3,000 AI credits on signup, but limits access to only free templates.

### Final Thoughts

I’ve been using SketchBubble’s AI presentation maker for the past few days, and I enjoyed it. The web app is fast, responsive, and well-integrated into the SketchBubble ecosystem. I also appreciate that it’s entirely web-based — no need for local installation.

The slides it generates look decent and professional enough for most uses. Just don’t expect complicated layouts or advanced animations. I see SketchBubble as a great way to create the base version of your presentation. You can export the slides to PowerPoint or Google Slides if you want to edit or add more details later.

The biggest advantage I’ve noticed so far is how massive the template collection is. SketchBubble has been around for more than 11 years, and there are over 200,000 available designs and more than a million users on the platform.

They’re currently offering 500 AI credits when you sign up, which is enough to generate around 100 slides (roughly 10 complete presentations with 10 slides each). That’s a lot of free usage, and no other platform I’ve tried gives this much right out of the gate.

If you often create pitch decks, reports, or product slides, I’d say give SketchBubble AI a try.
