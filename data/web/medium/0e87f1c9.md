Let’s focus on her career and products!

## 👩‍💼Building a career as a data scientist in IBM

After graduating from college, she first joined a joint venture between Accenture and Microsoft and worked as a data analyst.

A year or so later, she moved to IBM, where she successfully built a career as a data scientist.

She was promoted to a manager position at a young age, an elite position.

After five years as a manager, she has just recently officially quit her corporate job in June 2024.

This means that she was doing a side project while continuing to work for the company for a while.

I myself have been working on the side project since my first year in the workforce, so I can imagine how crazy busy she’s been.

In my case, I woke up at 6:00 a.m., arrived at a cafe near the office at 7:30 a.m., worked on the side project from 7:30 to 9:00 a.m., started a full-time job at 9:00 a.m., worked on the side project for an hour during lunch, returned home after work at 6:00 p.m. and worked on the side project until about 11:00 p.m. Every day.

It’s pretty crazy when I think about it now.

## 🛳Her indie hacking Journey

Now let’s take a look at her indie hacking journey! Her indie hacking journey began after Covid-19.

She was forced to work from home due to Covid-19, and in her spare time, she decided to try to do automated trading.

Indeed, since her day job is as a data scientist, it makes sense that she would consider an approach that predicts the price of stocks and crypt currencies and automatically trades them, rather than creating a product.

Well, she did automated trading of crypt currency and made 5 times her original capital in a few months with it.

And with this success, they decided to explore the possibility of creating a software product that would take advantage of their technology.

What she built was a product that would send customized buy/sell timing alerts to investors.

She was quite confident in this product, but in the end, no one bought it.
She says the lesson here is as follows.

* A product that is great for us is not necessarily the same for others.
* You should think about how to reach your target customers before you build your product
* It’s a waste of time to spend a lot of time on a product that no one sees or no one buys

It is a common story that a product is created with the thought, “This is a good idea!”

Before creating a product, you need to think about how to spread it.

After the failure of the first product, she consults Thibault, a very successful entrepreneur she knows.

## 🛥Sold the second product and tried the third product

Now, after selling the second product, she tries the third product.

It was to gather Twitter data, compile it into a Google Sheet, analyze it, and draw various graphs.

At first glance, it seemed like an interesting and promising idea.

However, this was the timing of the acquisition of Twitter by Elon Musk.

One of the various actions taken was to raise the minimum API fee.

It was announced that even the cheapest plan would cost $42k/month.

It is a severe decision to eliminate the free plan from Twitter’s API, which is incorporated into various products, and to have to pay at least $42k per month.

This change in API usage fees caused a stir in many areas.

In the end, she decided to abandon the development of this product and move on to another product development.

## ⭐Succeeded with her fourth product

After developing several products up to this point, she was in agony because she was unable to achieve significant results.

However, those who continuously challenge themselves find success.

She achieved great success with her fourth product.

It is called [iacrea](<https://www.iacrea.com/en-US>).

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:700/1*6ChswCvPCbLHj9rIEch_cg.jpeg)

This product generates an image of a specific house with furniture placed on it.

At the time, her relatives were having trouble imagining a room with furniture in it when they bought a new house.

This is why she created this product.

The functions are simple and very easy to understand. This product was accepted by the market and reached 13k€ /month.

So, why was this fourth product so successful?

First of all, she focused on real estate agents.

If this had been a product for individuals, no one would use it. The idea came from her relatives’ problems, but it is not her relatives who use the product. It is the agency.

Real estate agencies find buyers by making as many properties as possible more attractive.

In this case, iacrea can generate an image of the house when it is furnished, instead of the current modest state of the vacant house.

This will allow buyers to imagine themselves buying a house and living in it, which should increase the conversion rate.

In addition, while individuals would not use iacrea on a regular basis, real estate agents would use it on a regular basis as new properties come in one after another.

In this way, the number of clients increased, especially real estate agents.

In addition, to attract clients, she used SEO and affiliate contracts with other media to reach out to them.

She also increased their client base by attending events where people in the real estate industry gathered and talking directly with potential clients.

Conversely, advertising and direct mail did not work.

## 🚀How she reduced the churn rate from 41% to 16%.

Churn rate is very important in growing a SaaS product. If the churn rate is high, no matter how many new customers you acquire, you will fail.

The key to success is how to reduce the churn rate.

She succeeded in reducing the churn rate from 41% to 16%.

How did she reduce the churn rate so dramatically?

There are many products out there that make it difficult to cancel a subscription by intentionally placing the cancellation button in a difficult-to-understand location or by making the steps to cancel a subscription long.

Pauline says she did not want to make such a bad product.

She first designed the product so that credits could be carried over to the next month.

iacrea is a product that generates images by consuming monthly credits.

The credits were previously designed to be reset the following month, but now the credits that are not used can be carried over to the next month and used.

It is believed that this has prevented customers from forcibly canceling their subscriptions, as they feel they will not lose money even if they do not use the credits much during the month.

In addition, an annual plan was implemented.

Of course, with a monthly plan, there is a possibility of cancellation every month, but with an annual plan, customers who sign up for an annual plan are automatically expected to use the service for more than one year. The churn rate per month would drop dramatically.

In her case, she has set the pricing so that users can use the service for one year for the price of 10 months.

She also says that she has succeeded in reducing the churn rate by improving the product based on feedback from existing customers.

## 🧨Failure that lost 25% of revenue

She also shares her story of failure.

She says that one day a competitor came along offering a similar product.

And at a lower price than hers.

She felt threatened by this and chose to lower the price of her product by 25%.

However, she says that this action was meaningless. She only lost 25% of her revenue.

She says, “Our product has a value that only we can provide, and until now, our customers have used our product after agreeing to the initial price. Customers would not switch so easily just because a similar product came out.”

If the price is low from the beginning, it is difficult to make it even lower when signing a large contract.

Moreover the margin back to the affiliate is low, so there is less motivation to promote the product.

And if the profit margin is low, the quality of money that can be spent on improving the product will inevitably decrease.

Thus, it is dangerous to cheapen the price, because once the price is lowered, it is difficult to bring it back up again.
