# Mixture-of-Head Attention (MoH) : alternative to multi-head attention that treats attention heads as experts in Mixture-of-Experts

Drawing on the insight that not all attention heads hold equal significance, authors of this paper[1] propose Mixture-of-Head attention (MoH), a new architecture that treats attention heads as experts in the Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) mechanism.

MoH has two significant advantages:

* First, MoH enables each token to select the appropriate attention heads, enhancing inference efficiency without compromising accuracy or increasing the number of parameters.
* Second, MoH replaces the standard summation in multi-head attention with a weighted summation, introducing flexibility to the attention mechanism and unlocking extra performance potential.

Key contributions:

* propose a dynamic attention-head routing mechanism that allows each token to adaptively select the appropriate attention heads, enhancing model performance and inference efficiency without increasing the number of parameters
* In addition to training from scratch, demonstrate that pre-trained multi-head attention models, such as LLaMA3–8B, can be further continue-tuned into our MoH models, greatly enhancing the applicability of the proposed MoH method.
* A wide range of experiments across various popular model frameworks, including ViT, DiT, and LLMs, confirm that MoH is a promising alternative to vanilla multi-head attention, laying a solid foundation for developing advanced and efficient attention-based models

## Mixture-of-Head Attention

### i) Heads as Experts

* treats attention heads as experts
* MoH consists of h heads H = {H1,H2, …,Hh} and a router that activates the Top-K heads. Formally, given input tokens X and X′, the output of MoH is the weighted sum of outputs from the K selected heads:

where gi represents the routing score. gi is non-zero only when the ith attention head is activated.

### ii) Shared Heads

* designate a subset of heads as shared heads that remain always activated
* consolidating common knowledge within shared heads reduces redundancy among the other dynamically routed heads

## Experiments

### i) VIT for image classification

* Table below shows Comparisons to current state-of-the-art methods on ImageNet-1K classification

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:700/1*RHdXkbZz3pXwEaj4QJ_qCQ.png)
* despite activating only a subset of attention heads, MoH-ViT achieves highly competitive performance compared to current state-of-the-art methods
* MoH-ViT-B achieves 84.9% Top-1 accuracy on the ImageNet-1K classification benchmark with just 75% of the attention head.
* In contrast, the well-established ViT baseline, TransNeXt, attains a slightly lower accuracy of 84.8% while requiring 100% of the heads to be activated

### ii) DIT for class-conditional image generation

* Table below shows Comparisons to DiT on the benchmarking of class-conditional image generation on ImageNet-1K at 256×256 resolution

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:700/1*CJKyX3QMMSbfi97AD5dKzg.png)
* MoH-DiT models consistently outperform vanilla DiT models with 90% of attention heads activated
* However, when only 75% of the attention heads are activated, MoH-DiT models perform worse than DiT models with 100% of attention heads activated, because image generation tasks are dense prediction tasks that require attention mechanisms to capture pixel-level fine-grained relationships, leaving less redundancy in the attention heads compared to image classification tasks.

### iii) Training LLMs from Scratch

* despite activating only a subset of attention heads, MoH-LLMs achieve highly competitive performance compared to our baseline models
* MoH-LLMS achieves an average accuracy of 45.4% with just 50% of the attention heads activated. In contrast, the baseline model reaches a slightly lower accuracy of 43.9% with 100% of the attention heads activated

## Conclusion

* introduce MoH, a promising alternative to multi-head attention.
* MoH enables each token to adaptively select the appropriate attention heads, improving both model performance and inference efficiency without increasing the number of parameters
* experiments across various popular model frameworks, including ViT, DiT, and LLMs, demonstrate that MoH outperforms multi-head attention, even when using only 50%∼90% of the attention heads
