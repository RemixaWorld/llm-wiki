## Kosmos-2

KosMos-2[24] is designed to enable new capabilities of perceiving object descriptions and grounding text to the visual world.

Press enter or click to view image in full size

Figure 18 KosMos-2

KosMos-2 is a Transformer-based causal language model that is trained using the next-word prediction task. It builds upon the previous model, KOSMOS-1, and integrates grounding and referring capabilities into the model. This allows KosMos-2 to understand multimodal input, follow instructions, perceive object descriptions (such as bounding boxes), and ground language to the visual world.

To train KosMos-2 with grounding capability, the authors construct a large-scale dataset called GRIT (Grounded Image-Text pairs). This dataset consists of image-text pairs where text spans (such as noun phrases and referring expressions) are linked to their corresponding image regions using location tokens. The authors describe a pipeline for extracting and linking these text spans to the image regions.

KosMos-2 adopts the same model architecture and training objective as KOSMOS-1 but incorporates the grounded image-text pairs into the training data. The model learns to encode location tokens and associate them with text tokens, allowing it to ground the text output to the visual world. The authors provide details on the input representation for KosMos-2, which includes image embeddings, grounding tokens, and location tokens.

The paper evaluates KosMos-2 on various tasks, including multimodal grounding (such as referring expression comprehension and phrase grounding), multimodal referring (such as referring expression generation), perception-language tasks (such as image captioning and visual question answering), and language understanding and generation tasks. The results demonstrate the effectiveness of KosMos-2 in these tasks and highlight its new capabilities in grounding and referring.

Overall, KosMos-2 represents a significant advancement in multimodal large language models by integrating grounding and referring capabilities. It paves the way for the development of Embodiment AI and contributes to the convergence of language, multimodal perception, action, and world modeling, which is a crucial step towards artificial general intelligence.

## Shikra

Press enter or click to view image in full size

Figure 19 Shikra

Shikra[25] is a Multi-modal Large Language Model (MLLM) that aims to address the gap in current MLLMs’ ability to handle referential dialogue, which is an essential aspect of human communication. Unlike existing models, Shikra can comprehend and generate spatial coordinate inputs and outputs in natural language form.

The architecture of Shikra consists of three main components: a vision encoder, an alignment layer, and a Large Language Model (LLM). It is designed to be straightforward and simple, without the need for extra vocabularies, position encoders, pre-/post-detection modules, or external plug-in models. All inputs and outputs are represented in natural language numerical form.

Shikra is capable of handling various vision-language tasks, including location-related tasks like REC and PointQA, as well as conventional tasks such as Image Captioning and Visual Question Answering (VQA). Experimental results have shown promising performance of Shikra in these tasks.

One of the key features of Shikra is its ability to engage in referential dialogue with users. It can understand and respond to specific reference positions within a scene, providing explanations not only verbally but also spatially. This opens up numerous exciting applications, such as providing coordinates of mentioned objects in chains of thoughts and comparing user-pointed regions’ similarities.

To train Shikra, a combination of reorganized public datasets and high-quality referential dialogue data from Flickr30K Entities was used. The model was trained without the need for additional pre-/post-detectors or special encoders for position information.

In conclusion, Shikra is a unified and straightforward MLLM that can handle spatial coordinate inputs and outputs in natural language form. It fills the gap in current MLLMs’ ability to understand and engage in referential dialogue, offering promising performance in various vision-language tasks.

## GPT4V

GPT-4 with vision (GPT-4V) represents an advanced stage in artificial intelligence, where users can provide image inputs alongside text for analysis. This integration of multiple modalities, like images, into large language models (LLMs) is considered a significant development in AI research. Multimodal LLMs, like GPT-4V, extend the capabilities of text-only systems, offering new interfaces and functionalities. They can tackle diverse tasks and provide unique user experiences.

Initially, GPT-4V’s pre-training involved predicting the next word in a text using extensive datasets of text and images sourced from the internet and licensed data. Subsequently, it underwent fine-tuning through reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), aligning its outputs with human preferences.

A key feature of GPT-4V is its adeptness at handling interleaved image-text inputs. These inputs can vary — from being visually focused, like multiple images accompanied by a brief question, to being text-heavy, such as a lengthy webpage with a few images, or a balanced combination of both. This versatility allows for a range of applications. For instance, GPT-4V can calculate total taxes from several receipt images or extract specific information from multiple images. It can also correlate data across these mixed inputs (Figure 20), like identifying beer prices on a menu, counting items, and calculating total costs. Moreover, this capability to process combined image-text inputs is crucial for advanced applications like in-context few-shot learning and test-time prompting techniques, further enhancing GPT-4V’s utility.

Figure 20 GPT-4V can input interleaved images and texts

In addition, GPT-4V’s ability to comprehend and generate content based on visual information enables it to assist in creative endeavors such as graphic design, art generation, and visual storytelling. It can analyze and interpret visual art styles, suggest improvements in design layouts, and even create images based on detailed textual descriptions. This feature is particularly beneficial for professionals in creative fields who require a blend of textual and visual insights.

## Gemini

Recently, Google unveiled Gemini, a multi-modal large language model akin to GPT-4(V), but with a unique twist. Trained across a diverse range of data types including images, audio, video, and text, Gemini aims to be a generalist across these modalities while also pushing the boundaries of domain-specific understanding and reasoning.

Gemini distinguishes itself with its three tailored size modes: Ultra, Pro, and Nano, as shown in Figure 21. The Ultra mode is designed for highly complex tasks, offering the most robust capabilities of the three. The Pro mode balances advanced performance with scalability, suitable for widespread deployment. Lastly, the Nano mode is optimized for on-device applications, addressing the need for compact yet effective AI solutions.

Gemini 1.0 offers these three distinct sizes, each designed to cater to a wide array of applications. This flexibility and range make Gemini a formidable player in the realm of multi-modal large language models, advancing the state-of-the-art and broadening the horizon for AI applications.
