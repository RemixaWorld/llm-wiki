## I Don’t Need Hyperparameter Optimization

> Said no one ever

Ever try to cook something new, perhaps a dish from a different country that you liked in a restaurant, and blindly get all the ingredients and recipes right without looking at the recipe? **Likely not.**

You will either need someone to give you the exact recipe, or you will need a ton of trial and error. This is because there are **just too many variables**.

> I like to think of each machine-learning project as cooking a meal. Even if the model is the same, the data and requirements can be different, causing different behaviors to emerge from your model.

This means that in each project, you will need to tune the variables that control the “cooking” of your model, the hyperparameters, to get optimal performance. It’s not just reaching a final good performance; **good hyperparameters can actually improve your training speed by 10X.**

> But how do you find them?

> This is why Meta created **Nevergrad**

## What Makes Nevergrad Different?

> Isn’t `[Optuna](<https://optuna.org/>)` better?

There are actually several hyperparameter optimization tools out there, such as [Ray-Tune](<https://docs.ray.io/en/latest/tune/index.html>), [Optuna](<https://optuna.org/>), [Hyperopt](<https://github.com/hyperopt/hyperopt>). However, each of them has its own advantages and disadvantages. Nevergrad has two key differentiators that make it truly special:

1. **The incredible variety** of gradient-free optimization algorithms! It has a selection of** _over 70 optimizers_**! Including classics like [Differential Evolution](<https://medium.com/@reshma_shaji/differential-evolution-what-it-is-and-how-does-it-work-81d3415c2367>), [Particle Swarm Optimization](<https://thisisrishi.medium.com/understanding-particle-swarm-optimization-pso-from-basics-to-brilliance-d0373ad059b6>), and [CMA-ES](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.00772>).
Sound overwhelming? No worries, they have great “default optimizers,” such as `[NGOpt](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.04412>)`, which abstract this away and make a **smart selection of which optimizers will be best for your problem setting.**
2. **The variety of benchmarks** — from noisy to multimodal to multi-objective optimization benchmarks. Nevergrad is much more versatile than “just a hyperparameter optimization tool”.
**It can also be used as a research platform for optimization algorithm development!**
3. **It is wider in scope** than “just a hyperparameter optimization platform.” It can be used across many different research and engineering applications. Since it is designed for gradient-free optimization, any non-differentiable and black box function can be optimized — even a complex simulation!

## So, How Does It Work?

> As a practitioner of gradient free optimization, I like to think of it like this.

You have several knobs to turn and buttons to push, and you are tasked with trying to get the highest score in a game. But you can’t see the game; you just set the knobs and bottoms, hit “run,” and see your final score.

**How can you get good at this game if you can’t even see it?**

> Welcome to the world of black-box optimization.

In this game, you try a few things, get the result, and then try a few more things. The only feedback is the score you get. This is kinda abstract, **which is why this framework is so powerful!**

> The black-box function to be optimized can be ANYTHING! From the accuracy of a neural network to the power generated by a turbine to the maximal temperature a heat shield can withstand or the drag of a racecar.

Many gradient-free optimization algorithms have been developed over the years that thrive in these settings, including [CMA-ES](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.00772>), [Particle Swarm Optimization](<https://thisisrishi.medium.com/understanding-particle-swarm-optimization-pso-from-basics-to-brilliance-d0373ad059b6>), and more.

## Conclusion

> Should you jump on the Nevergrad train?

I think you should ask yourself the following questions:

1. **Do you hate wasting time** manually tuning hyperparameters?
2. Do you work with **non-differentiable or complex functions or have multiple objectives?**

If the answer to these questions is yes, then you should definitely go with it! This library offers a wealth of tools and will teach you about best practices in the world of gradient-free optimization. From my experience, this knowledge is very valuable _and highly transferable between projects._

That said, if you don’t need the huge variety of optimizers and benchmarks, it is possible that other libraries, such as `[Optuna](<https://optuna.org/>)` will also work for you.

> The main thing is that you don’t neglect your hyperparameters.
>
> Optimizing them is always worth your time.
