So, it’s important to develop more effective methods for aligning LLMs with a specific standard of behaviour.

Steering is one such method.

In this blog, we’ll first go over some of the issues with the current approaches to making models behave the way we want them to. Next we’ll go deeper into what steering is and how it works. Finally, we’ll go over the benefits of using steering and why it could prevent a global catastrophe.

## The Current Approaches to Behaviour Tuning

To change an LLM’s behaviour, we usually carry out model fine-tuning or prompt engineering.

For example, if we want our LLM to tell the truth, we might do the following:

* **We might engineer the prompt** and add the words: “Double check your answer and make sure it is accurate, and only answer if you’re sure of the answer”.
* **We might fine-tune the LLM** , which means teaching it how to answer specific kinds of questions to align with the truthful behaviour.

But there are issues with both:

* **Engineering the prompt** may push the LLM to behave a certain way, but it still isn’t always going to behave in a predictable way.
* **Fine-tuning the LLM** has the same issues, plus there’s an extra GPU cost of training the LLM on several examples which could be avoided through a training-free approach.

What happens when we scale the AI to do more than just answer questions? How do we make sure it doesn’t behave unpredictably?

The answer is: **to dig deeper**.

We look at the activations, understand why an LLM behaves in a specific way and what the activations are telling us. And possibly, how can we change the activations to make them behave the way we want them to?

## What is Activation Steering?

The idea behind activation steering is that we can add a vector at a specific layer of the LLM during the generation of outputs, and expect it to follow that behaviour more closely. We’ll start off by explaining how exactly this works in a more simple scenario and how that scales to LLMs.

### “Steering” word embeddings

Most introductory NLP blogs probably talk about this, but what’s especially interesting is how similar it is to activation steering.

> **Word embeddings** are vector representations of words. A vector is just a sequence of N numbers that represents a point in N-dimensional space. For example, a 3D vector consists of three numbers (X, Y, and Z-coordinates), and together, they represent a point in 3-dimensional space. The point of word embeddings is every word is placed somewhere in the N-dimensional space.

Take the following example. Let’s take the word embedding vector of the word “King” and the word “Queen”.

So how exactly does this relate with what we are trying to achieve with “steering”?

### “Steering” LLM behaviours

Let’s consider an LLM that always hallucinates. We want them to hallucinate less and tell the truth more.

We could then “steer” these LLMs along the truthful direction. That’s what activation steering is all about. But how exactly do we do this?

> This is just an illustrative example and is one way of steering LLMs towards the truth. There are more complex strategies, but when engaging in this kind of research, this is usually a good place to start.

I’ll go over a few possible ways to generate this “steering vector” and what needs to be done afterwards to steer our model in the right direction.

**Version 1:** Pass one truthful and one untruthful prompts into the LLM individually. The final token’s hidden state should contain information about the concept of “truth” and “untruth”. We extract these hidden states and take the difference to generate the steering vector.

Press enter or click to view image in full size

Generation of a truth steering vector with a single pair of prompts. (Image by Author)

**Version 2:** When we use only one prompt like in the example above, the vector might actually be the “roundness” vector (flat -> round) rather than the truth vector. So instead, we take several such prompts, average the corresponding vectors in each class, and then take the difference to generate the truth steering vector.

Press enter or click to view image in full size

Generation of a truth steering vector by averaging several prompts. (Image by Author)

Finally, we add this vector to the specific layer it was extracted from every time the model generates a new token, hoping that the model is now more truthful in its generations.

## Why is steering so important?

What this approach fundamentally does is it helps us find a “direction” in an LLM representing some behaviour, and adding a vector along that direction helps us steer the LLM towards that behaviour.

The reason this idea is so important compared to other methods is that it helps us interpret LLMs, and find “directions” representing various behaviours that LLMs naturally have.

* **By finding these directions, it allows for more fine-grained control of the outputs.** We can control the behaviour of an LLM to make it precisely as “sycophantic” as we want it to be, precisely as “truthful” as we want it to be, and precisely as “enthusiastic” as we want it to be just by controlling how much we steer it along those directions.
* **By figuring out interpretability, we don’t need to spend excessive GPU resources to train LLMs.** The process of training fundamentally changes the weights to get the behaviour to match the examples given to it. Instead, we can just change the weights or activations directly if we know how to change them to effect some behaviour.
* **Their behaviour just becomes way more predictable this way.** Training could improve an LLM’s performance in one criterion, but ruin the other ones. But if we know how these directions are related to each other, we’d be able to predict which behaviours get negatively affected when we steer one behaviour.

Ultimately, the idea of activation steering and finding directions corresponding to different behaviours might be the secret to ensuring we can align AI in exactly the way we want. The fact that this helps make AI much more _controllable_ and _predictable_ means we have some power over the AI systems. That way, once they become more powerful and autonomous, we can control them according to our values, and prevent a potential AI-related catastrophe.
