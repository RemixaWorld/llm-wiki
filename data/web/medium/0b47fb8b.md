This is the second part on the exploration of the capabilities of the Phi3 LLM developed by Microsoft as OCR on images of personal document as the identity card, the driving license or the health insurance card.

In the first story, I applied the Phi3 model on well made and flawless images of those documents. In that case the documents were colored, well disposed, not blurry, with ideal brightness and contrast. I showed that, with these conditions, the Phi3 model is able to extracts the data of the documents with a zero shot prompt, without finetuning.

In real life applications, OCR methods are often used on photocopied or scanned documents, which may be rotated, blurred, too dark or too bright.
In these cases the quality of the documents is poorer respect to the ideal one, therefore the model capabilities to extract the right data may also degrade. In what follow I will explore the ability of the model to extract data from low quality images of the identity card document. Furthermore, I will apply some common computer vision technique to restore the quality of the images, then I will check once again if the model improves in its extraction capabilities.

The code and the images used in this post are available at my GitHub repo at this [link](<https://github.com/enrico310786/phi3_vision_language>).

## 4) Quality recovery of the test images

In the following I consider three non-deep learning computer vision techniques that we can apply sequentially on the modified images to restore their quality and align them to the standard one. All these techniques can be applied using the OpenCV package.

### a) SIFT

SIFT stands for Scale-Invariant Feature Transform. Its heart lies in the ability to detect key points invariant to scale and orientation. Owing to this algorithm, it is possible to find the relevant keypoints of the reference and transformed images. Thus, once identified the same keypoints, is possible to rotate and scale the second image in order to align and resize it with the reference one.

On the left of the images below, are shown the common keypoints between the standard and test image found by the SIFT algorithm. On the right, the rotated and scaled test image. We can note that in certain cases, as that in Figure 6 for the cie_fronte_mod7.jpg, the number of matching keypoints is lower owing to the low quality of the test image. As a consequence, also the rotated and scaled image is not perfect aligned and readable.

### b) blur removal

Once rotated and scaled, the result image may be blurry. Blurring smooths an image, thus it loses edge details. In this cases we can measure the amount of blur and, if it is over a certain threshold, we can remove it and unblur the image.

To measure the amount of blur of an image we can apply the Laplacian method. An interesting post where you can find deeper details about the Laplacian method is here.
