# Multi-token prediction : Improves over next-token prediction for faster and better LLMs

Large language models such as GPT and Llama are trained with a next-token prediction loss. In this paper[1], authors propose multi-token prediction as an improvement over next-token prediction in training language models for generative or reasoning tasks.

More specifically, at each position in the training corpus, authors asked the model to predict the following n tokens using n independent output heads, operating on top of a shared model trunk. The method is increasingly useful for larger model sizes, with big gains on generative benchmarks like coding, where models with multi-token prediction will consistently outperform strong baselines by several percentage points.

With multi-token prediction, 13B parameter models solves 12 % more problems on HumanEval and 17 % more on MBPP than comparable next-token models. As an additional benefit, models trained with 4-token prediction are up to 3 times faster at inference, even with large batch sizes.

**Key contributions:**

* proposed a simple multi-token prediction architecture with no train time or memory overhead.
* provide experimental evidence that this training paradigm is beneficial at scale, with models up to 13B parameters solving around 15% more code problems.
* Multi-token prediction enables self-speculative decoding,making models up to 3 times faster at inference time across a wide range of batch-sizes

## Overview of multi-token prediction

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:536/1*BlLJHiI0jHRA2Ifi0zyufA.png)

* During training, the model predicts 4 future tokens at once, by means of a shared trunk and 4 dedicated output heads.
* During inference, we employ only the next-token output head. Optionally, the other three heads may be used to speed-up inference time.

### ii) Memory-efficient implementation

* For current LLMs the vocabulary size V is much larger than the dimension d of the latent representation, causing logit vectors to become the GPU memory usage bottleneck.
* Naive implementations of multi-token predictors that materialize all logits and their gradients, both of shape (n, V ), severely limit the allowable batch-size and average GPU memory utilization.
* In this approach[1], authors propose to carefully adapt the sequence of forward and backward operations as outlined in figure below

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:549/1*K_HMvQbGa_ZtLJGlqcKo2Q.png)
* As outlined in figure above, author proposed specifying order of the forward/backward in an n-token prediction model with n = 2 heads. By performing the forward/backward on the heads in sequential order, they avoid materializing all unembedding layer gradients in memory simultaneously and reduce peak GPU memory usage

### iii) Inference

* During inference time, the most basic use of the proposed architecture is vanilla next-token autoregressive prediction using the next-token prediction head Pθ(xt+1 |xt:1), while discarding all others
* additional output heads can be leveraged to speed up decoding from the next-token prediction head with self-speculative decoding methods such as blockwise parallel decoding, a variant of speculative decoding, without the need for an additional draft model — and speculative decoding with Medusa-like tree attention

## Experiments on real data

### i) Benefits scale with model size

* For studying this, models were trained in six sizes in the range 300M to 13B parameters from scratch on at least 91B tokens of code.
* Evaluation results shown below on MBPP [5] and HumanEval [6]

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:531/1*d2uvuBRAfJmTDq6aa1bCjg.png)
* As observed in figure above, Multi-token prediction models are worse than the baseline for small model sizes, but outperform the baseline at scale.

### ii) Faster inference

* greedy self-speculative decoding [2] was implemented with heterogeneous batch sizes using xFormers[7]
* decoding speeds measured on author’s best 4-token prediction model with 7B parameters on completing prompts taken from a test dataset of code and natural language not seen during training.
* observed speedup of 3.0× on code with an average of 2.5 accepted tokens out of 3 suggestions on code and of 2.7× on text.
* On an 8-byte prediction model, the inference speedup is 6.4×.

### iii) Learning global patterns with multi-byte prediction

* For showing that next-token prediction task latches to local patterns, a a 7B parameter byte-level transformer trained on 314B bytes, equivalent to around 116B tokens.
* 8-byte prediction model achieved huge improvements compared to next-byte prediction, solving 67% more problems on MBPP pass@1 and 20% more problems on HumanEval pass@1, despite having been trained on 1.7× less data compared to token-level models.
* Self speculative decoding can achieve speedups of 6 times for the 8-byte prediction model, which would allow to fully compensate the cost of longer byte-level sequences at inference time and even be faster than a next-token prediction model by nearly two times.

### iv) Searching for the optimal n

* For understanding the effect of the number of predicted tokens, comprehensive ablations performed on models of scale7B trained on 200B tokens of code.
* Following table shows that training with 4-future tokens outperforms all the other models consistently throughout HumanEval and MBPP for pass at 1, 10 and 100 metrics: +3.8%, +2.1% and +3.2% for MBPP and +1.2%, +3.7% and +4.1% for HumanEval.

### v) Training for multiple epochs

* Multi-token training still maintains an edge on next-token prediction when trained on multiple epochs of the same data.
* improvements diminish but still presents a +2.4% increase on pass@1 on MBPP and +3.2% increase on pass@100 on HumanEval, while having similar performance for the rest.

### vi) Finetuning multi-token predictors

* Pretrained models with multi-token prediction loss also outperform next-token models for use in finetunings
* 4-token prediction model compared with the next-token prediction baseline, and include a setting where the 4-token prediction model is stripped off its additional prediction heads and finetuned using the classical next-token prediction target.
* As shown in figure below, both ways of finetuning on CodeContests dataset [8], the 4-token prediction model outperform the next-token prediction model on pass@k across k.

## Reasons for superior performance of multi-token prediction

### i) Lookahead reinforces choice points

* Not all token decisions are equally important for generating useful texts from language models.
* Multi-token prediction implicitly assigns weights to training tokens depending on how closely they are correlated with their successors.
* Figure below shows that Multi-token prediction loss assigns higher implicit weights to consequential tokens, with sequence in which all transitions except “5 → A” are easy to predict, alongside the corresponding prediction targets in 3-token prediction.

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:454/1*fC1_EtsT62Ur11aEGZm-UQ.png)
* Since the consequences of the difficult transition “5 → A” are likewise hard to predict, this transition receives a higher implicit weight in the overall loss via its correlates “3→ A”, …, “5→ C”.

### ii) Information-theoretic argument

* Language models are typically trained by teacher-forcing, where the model receives the ground truth for each future token during training. However, during test time generation is unguided and autoregressive, whereby errors accumulate.
* Teacher-forcing, we argue, encourages models to focus on predicting well in the very short term, at the potential expense of ignoring longer-term dependencies in the overall structure of the generated sequence.
* multi-token predictors are more accurate at predicting tokens X that are of relevance for the remainder of the text to come. That can be explained with following scenario
* Token X denoting the next future token, and Y denoting the second-next future token conditioned on some observed, input context C. When placed before token X, vanilla next-token prediction concerns the quantity H(X), while multi-token prediction with n = 2 aims at H(X) + H(Y )

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:520/1*jMXSSAPQyqB1WVexNSXheA.png)
* By discarding the term H(Y | X) — which appears again when predicting at the following position , it was observed that 2-token prediction increases the importance of I(X; Y ) by a factor of 2.

## Future work

* better understand how to automatically choose n in multi-token prediction losses, using loss scales and loss balancing.
* optimal vocabulary sizes for multi-token prediction are likely different from those for next-token prediction, and tuning them could lead to better results, as well as improved trade-offs between compressed sequence length and compute-per-byte expenses.
* developing an improved auxiliary prediction losses that operate in embedding spaces.
