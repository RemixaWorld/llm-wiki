> You don’t make a statement unless you make noise. — Adam Cole

[Large language models (LLMs)](<https://github.com/SalvatoreRa/tutorial/blob/main/artificial%20intelligence/FAQ.md#:~:text=What%20is%20a%20Large%20Language%20Model%20\(LLM\)%3F>) showed great capabilities, and people were impressed with their performance in a wide range of tasks. On the other hand, some challenges remain, such as outdated knowledge or the presence of [hallucinations](<https://github.com/SalvatoreRa/tutorial/blob/main/artificial%20intelligence/FAQ.md#:~:text=What%20does%20it%20mean%20LLM%27s%20hallucination%3F>). In fact, LLMs can also produce outputs that are either factually incorrect or utterly nonsensical.

A new paradigm named [Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)](<https://github.com/SalvatoreRa/tutorial/blob/main/artificial%20intelligence/FAQ.md#:~:text=What%20is%20Retrieval%20Augmented%20Generation%20\(RAG\)%3F>) is designed to solve this problem. RAG is designed with the idea that it can solve limitations such as outdated knowledge or the production of hallucinations. To do this, RAG embeds different documents and then finds the appropriate context to answer a user question. The problem is that these documents in the database can have several problems: outdated content, errors, AI-generated data, and more.

## [RAG is Dead, Long Live RAGIs it really true that long-context LLMs are killing the RAG?levelup.gitconnected.com](</rag-is-dead-long-live-rag-c607e1799199?source=post_page-----b9d67253500f--------------------------------------->)

[LLMs are notoriously sensitive to noise](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04004>), and have trouble extracting the correct information when there is noise:

> However, Retrieval-Augmented Generation brings not only positive effects to LLMs. On one hand, there is a significant amount of noise information even fake news in the content available on the Internet, which poses challenges for search engines in accurately retrieving desirable knowledge. — [source](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.01431>)

Previously, they were referred to as noise, [but in this study](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.01431>), they began to differentiate. An LLM, when presented with the context, should be resistant to irrelevant information (noise robustness), able to not use the context if it does not present the answer (negative rejection), integrate information from multiple chunks (information integration), and avoid if there are errors in the context (counterfactual robustness).

image source: [here](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.01431>)

Despite this, there is still a lack of in-depth analysis of the impact of noise, its effect, and how to categorize it. This study attempted to do this by differentiating the various types of noise and their effect on RAG:

## [Pandora's Box or Aladdin's Lamp: A Comprehensive Analysis Revealing the Role of RAG Noise in Large…Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) has emerged as a crucial method for addressing hallucinations in large language…arxiv.org](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13533?source=post_page-----b9d67253500f--------------------------------------->)

The authors categorize noise into seven different types:

* **Semantic Noise (SeN)**. documents that contain low semantic relevance to the query, are off-topic or distort meaning.
* **Datatype Noise (DN)**. when there is the mixing of various types of data (e.g. conducting Wikipedia scraping mixes of text and links end up in the chunks).
* **Illegal Sentence Noise (ISN)**. The Web is heterogeneous and there are sentences that are not grammatically correct.
* **Counterfactual Noise (CN)**. this is false information (such as fake news or outdated knowledge) and is often found in data downloaded from the web or social networks. In short, they can be defined as texts that contain factual errors.
* **Supportive Noise (SuN)**. are documents that contain high semantic relevance but lack corresponding answer information (so despite being on the topic they do not provide information for the query answer).
* **Orthographic Noise (ON)**. documents that contain writing errors such as spelling mistakes and word lengthening.
* **Prior Noise (PN)**. per the authors: “ Our study defines prior noise as questions based on false assumptions or premises. For example, the question “ _Who was the CEO of Google when they were restructured into Alphabet in 2017?” contains prior noise because the restructuring occurred in 2015, not 2017._ ”

The authors then find a set of data and generate a set of questions and answers (along with the evidence that supports them). After that, they introduce noise and create the testbed for their system. To simplify the evaluation they decided to transform the questions from open-ended to multiple-choice (the model must choose the right answer from four options).

image source: [here](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.13533>)

The authors then used two models for the generation: [Llama3–8B-Instruct](<https://huggingface.co/meta-llama/Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct>) and [Qwen2–7B-Instruct](<https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2-7B-Instruct>). The intriguing result is that not all noise is deleterious; in fact, some types of noise are beneficial.

> Based on these trends, we can categorize retrieval noises into two types: harmful noise (counterfactual, supportive, and orthographic) and beneficial noise (semantic, datatype, and illegal sentence) — [source](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.13533>)

image source: [here](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.13533>)

So harmful noise is those noises that impact the performance of the model in a negative way. For example, counterfactual noise confuses the model and leads it to disregard correct information and respond erroneously. In contrast, beneficial noise helps the model to improve performance. According to the authors, illegal sentence noise exhibits the most notable improvement in model performance

Another interesting result is that the boost is also scale-dependent, with smaller models having a greater benefit

image source: [here](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.13533>)

Moreover, this beneficial noise also has a positive effect not only on naive RAG but also on more advanced forms of [RAG](<https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/what-is-retrieval-augmented-generation/>).

image source: [here](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.13533>)

Furthermore, although harmful noise reduces performance, the addition of beneficial noise still has a positive impact by improving performance compared to pure harmful noise.

Press enter or click to view image in full size

image source: [here](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.13533>)

When the model is exposed to harmful noise, the model ignores correct information and responds incorrectly.

> But why is beneficial noise beneficial to performance?

The authors propose three hypotheses on why beneficial noise contributes to performance:

* **A clearer and more explicit reasoning process.** The model seems to offer more attention to the right context and better distinguish correct and incorrect information. Thus, beneficial noise could improve the LLM’s ability to integrate its parametric knowledge with found information.
* **More standardized response formats.** Responses with beneficial noise are more standardized.
* **Increased confidence with golden context.** LLM has lower uncertainty and increased confidence in their answers, this seems to suggest that the model pays more attention to correct answers and is, therefore, more confident.

image source: [here](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.13533>)

[In this paper](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.13533>), they show that not all noise is harmful but rather sometimes helps the model. It seems that the noise that helps the model the most are semantic, datatype, and illegal sentences. In general, these errors are the easiest to identify (for example, an illegal sentence here is made up of random sample words). It could be that these errors flag the model that there is something wrong within the context and thus supplement its parametric knowledge. There is a subtle interplay between the model’s knowledge and what it finds in the context. More confident models reject when the answer in context is too different from their knowledge.

## [The AI Student Dilemma: Trust Yourself Or The Book?LLMs have to decide if trust their knowledge or additional context. What will they choose?levelup.gitconnected.com](</the-ai-student-dilemma-trust-yourself-or-the-book-4aee1250718a?source=post_page-----b9d67253500f--------------------------------------->)

So adding some errors might prompt the model to pay more attention and focus more on finding the correct information (perhaps in accordance with its knowledge or by conducting more reasoning). In any case, it is an interesting point and would need more study.

The results are interesting because they could perhaps be exploited in the future as an additional technique for advanced RAG. For example, adding noise to the context before sending it to the LLM to generate.
