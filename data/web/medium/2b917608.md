## Understanding LayoutLMv3 in Layman’s Terms:

> I went through the paper of LayoutLMv3 and several explanation articles from the internet, but I found that most of them **may still give you a headache in comprehending the structure** of this Language Model.

You can check the [**original paper of LayoutLMv3**](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.08387.pdf>)

So before delving into the application of the model, I will explain this in the simplest terms for you to grab how it works.

### Definition:

In simple terms, LayoutLMv3 is a powerful language model that excels in comprehending **(1) the structure (position)** and **(2) content of documents**. Think of it as a highly advanced tool that not only recognizes words but also understands their positions within that document and **(3) relationships** within a document. This feature is crucial when dealing with diverse document formats, including PDFs, images, and forms.

The LayoutLMv3 model is pre-trained on a massive dataset of document images and text. This allows it to learn the visual and semantic features of documents. Once pre-trained, the LayoutLMv3 model can be fine-tuned for a variety of document analysis tasks, such as document classification, NER, and question answering.

### Here are 2 simple analogies to help you understand LayoutLMv3:

Imagine that you are trying to understand a cooking recipe. You can (1) **read the text of the recipe to learn the ingredients and steps involved** , but you can also **(2) look at the images of the finished dish to get a better understanding of the overall look and feel**. LayoutLMv3 is like a chef who can both read the recipe and look at the images to understand how to cook the dish, by **(3) combining the relationship of (1) and (2) to have better understand of how to cook**. This allows it to learn more about the recipe than it could if it could only read or look at one or the other.

Imagine that you are trying to understand a foreign language. You can (1) **read the text in a foreign language** and try to translate it yourself, but you can also (2) **look at images of the objects or actions** that the text is describing. LayoutLMv3 is like a translator who can **(3) both read the text in the foreign language and look at images to help them translate the text**. This allows LayoutLMv3 to translate the text more accurately and efficiently than a traditional translator who can only read the text.

### Architecture:

LayoutMLv3 Architecture

As you can see, the LayoutLMv3 architecture is similar to the Transformer architecture, but with a few additional components:

* **Text embedding layer (The content of documents):** This layer converts the text of the document into a numerical representation. **The blue area above.**
* **Image embedding layer** **(The position of text / information)** : This layer converts the image of the document into a numerical representation. **The orange area above.**
* **Cross-modality attention layer (The relationship of text and its position)** : This layer allows the model to learn the relationship between the text and image embeddings. **The purple area above.**

### Note:

In architecture, before combining the relationship between text and its position, we may notice a new term “**Multimodal Transformer** ” (gray area). You can think of it as the ability of a model that can read and learn from multiple inputs: text, image, audio, and so on. That’s why it is called “**Multimodal** ”. In addition, you may encounter the word “**unified** ” many times when reading the original paper,

In the context above, the multimodal learns data from text and image to extract the relationship of the two. In our example, the model extracts the text for cooking steps and also looks at the image of a finished product, so it reads 2 different types of input data.

You can forget about Self-Attention, Multi-Head, Position-wise Fully Connected Feed-Forward Networks,… these heavily academic terms and still understand the algorithm, I hope.

### **Applied to a project and compared with the traditional approaches:**

Let's say we need to extract a candidate's address from a Resume.

For traditional approaches, if we use regular expression, rule-based approach or NER, our address list may **contain the candidate's address and those of the companies** , for which our candidate worked.

But for LayoutMLv3, since we combine the position of text in relation to the whole document, we know where is the region for Candidate BASIC INFORMATION and where is the region for EXPERIENCE. When we annotate the labels, the algorithm can understand that all addresses below the EXPERIENCE session are not the candidate’s address. In short, with the position, we can capture the document information much better.

### **Summary:**

In short, here is a simple example of how LayoutLMv3 can be used to classify documents:

1. The user provides LayoutLMv3 with a document image.
2. LayoutLMv3 extracts the text and image embeddings from the document.
3. LayoutLMv3 passes the text and image embeddings through the Transformer model.
4. The Transformer model learns the relationship between the text and image embeddings and produces a prediction for the document class.

## LayoutMLv3 Use Cases:

Up to this point, we could induce that LayoutLMv3 is best used for extracting structured data like Invoice, Resume, … but how about unstructured data or types of input with various formats?

LayoutLMv3 can also be used for unstructured data or data with various formats, but it may require some additional pre-processing and we need a huge amount of time to annotate the labels and train the model on various data formats.

It is important to note that **LayoutLMv3 is still under development**(I stated this today, November 9th, 2023), and its performance on unstructured data or data with various formats may vary depending on the specific task and dataset. However, LayoutLMv3 has the potential to be a powerful tool for document analysis on a wide variety of data formats.

### Here are some use cases of LayoutLMv3:

* **Document classification:** Classifying documents into different categories, such as invoices, receipts, contracts, and medical records.
* **Named entity recognition (NER)** : Identifying and extracting named entities from documents, such as people, places, organizations, and products.
* **Question answering:** Answering questions about the content of documents, such as “What is the total price of this invoice?” or “What is the patient’s diagnosis in this medical record?”
* **Document visual question answering:** Answering questions about the visual layout of documents, such as “Where is the customer’s signature on this contract?” or “What is the price of this product in this catalog?”
* **Form understanding:** Extracting information from forms, such as loan applications, insurance claims, and tax returns.
* **Receipt processing:** Extracting information from receipts, such as the date and time of purchase, the items purchased, and the total amount spent.
* **Invoice processing:** Extracting information from invoices, such as the vendor name, invoice number, due date, and line items.
* **Contract analysis:** Extracting key information from contracts, such as the parties involved, the terms of the contract, and the signatures of the parties.
* **Medical record analysis:** Extracting key information from medical records, such as the patient’s demographics, medical history, and treatment plan.
* **E-discovery:** Identifying and extracting relevant documents from large collections of electronic data.
* **Fraud detection:** Identifying fraudulent documents, such as fake invoices and forged checks.
* **Customer support:** Answering customer questions about their documents and accounts.
* **Research:** Conducting research on documents, such as news articles, scientific papers, and historical records.

LayoutLMv3 can be used in a variety of industries, including finance, healthcare, legal, insurance, and retail. It is a powerful tool that can help businesses to automate their document processing tasks, improve their customer service, and reduce their risk of fraud.

### Here are some specific examples of how LayoutLMv3 is being used today:

* A bank is using LayoutLMv3 to classify loan applications and extract key information, such as the borrower’s income and debt-to-income ratio. This helps the bank to make faster and more accurate lending decisions.
* A healthcare provider is using LayoutLMv3 to extract information from medical records, such as the patient’s demographics, medical history, and treatment plan. This helps the provider to provide better care to their patients.
* A law firm is using LayoutLMv3 to identify and extract relevant documents from large collections of electronic data during e-discovery. This helps the firm to save time and money on litigation costs. **This is a stunning example of extracting data from an unstructured format.**
* An insurance company is using LayoutLMv3 to identify fraudulent insurance claims. This helps the company to reduce its risk of fraud and protect its customers.
* A retail company is using LayoutLMv3 to answer customer questions about their receipts and accounts. This helps the company to improve its customer service and reduce the number of customer inquiries.

LayoutLMv3 is a powerful new tool for document analysis, and it is being used in a variety of ways to improve efficiency, reduce costs, and mitigate risk.

### Note:

LayoutLMv3 is a **pre-trained model** , which was trained on a massive dataset of **11 million** document images and their corresponding text embeddings. The dataset contains a variety of document types, including invoices, receipts, contracts, and medical records (Number of document types: 42). For that reason, when training the model, **we reserve all the weights of most layers** , and only need to fine-tune, unfreezing, a few last layers in the network. I can firmly project that the more we train, we wont get much of accuracy. But the **ONLY THING WE NEED IS TO HAVE A GOOD INPUT DATASET.**

As a company, you and your team need to prepare and annotate a lot of data as your own purpose and train the model with just 100 epoches, then I can assure that you are good.

In practice, we embrace a technique called Iterative training and Model refinement, and most of the time, we only work with input data. The model training accounts less than 10% of our work.
