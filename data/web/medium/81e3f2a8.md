## Method

This paper introduce Probing-RAG, an efficient RAG pipeline that incorporates a prober to determine whether the language model needs to retrieve additional documents. Similar to the conventional retrieval-augmented generation pipeline, this approach comprises a generating language model and a retriever. Different from the general pipeline, the generator of Probing-RAG leverages the output from the prober and adaptively calls the retriever based on the model’s internal hidden state.

### i) Prober

* Given the LLM’s hidden state during answer generation, the prober assesses whether an additional retrieval step is necessary
* designed prober as a feed-forward network with a single hidden layer and an output layer for binary classification
* Based on findings of [2], lower layers in language models capture low-level information while higher layers capture more abstract, high-level information, therefore authors position prober after the one-third point of the model to maximize the utility of these representations
* authors employ Gemma-2B an 18-layer model, as the generator in our RAG pipeline and position the prober on even-numbered layers starting from the 6th layer
* prober utilizes the hidden states corresponding to the model-generated rationale (r) and answer (ˆa).
* Let Hlk represent the hidden state at the kth layer, and let the combined length of the r and ˆa tokens be u. We define the hidden states corresponding to these tokens as:

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:258/1*u_wcECJOscCqcOy-sDb7vw.png)
* To reduce the dimensionality of T′lk and obtain a summary representation, we first compute the mean of the hidden states across the token dimension, producing a single vector.
* Next, we normalize this vector to maintain numerical stability and consistency, resulting in the final input to the prober:

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:324/1*9N9VGUgH3pi3ymje0Ge5IQ.png)
* This normalized representation T′′lk is then fed into the prober:

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:189/1*B-Y1YswDqkdYLOm17cRilQ.png)
* Using the value of logitlk , prober decides whether or not to perform an additional retrieval

### ii) Training Prober

* To train the prober, we require pairs of (T′lk, y)N1, where T′lk is the input derived from the hidden states, and y is the output denoting whether additional retrieval is needed
* Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting is used to generate these pairs, to let the LLM generate r and ˆa for the given question. The hidden state, T′lk,is recorded during this answer generation, and the label y is assigned based on the accuracy of ˆa compared to the ground truth
* If the answer is correct, y = 1 (indicating no retrieval is needed); if the answer is incorrect, y = 0 (indicating retrieval is necessary).
* For each question (q), two versions of r and ˆa are generated: one without retrieval and one with retrieval, which helps the prober learn to distinguish when retrieval improves performance and when it does not.
* As shown in figure below, the dataset includes four types of examples: (with retrieval, y = 0), (with retrieval, y = 1), (without retrieval, y = 0), and (without retrieval, y = 1).

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:507/1*1qZFwnJUoiUSZ6ACMYuABw.png)
* three open-domain QA datasets to build the training dataset: HotpotQA, NaturalQA and TriviaQA
* The final dataset consists of 26,060 training and 500 validation samples.
* trained the probers using the generated dataset with a cross-entropy loss as follows:

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:435/1*A2iXbzBGW02Z4BmwHxbyHQ.png)

### iii) Probing based Retrieval-Augmented Generation

* In the first iteration, using CoT prompting, the LLM generates r and ˆa based solely on the input question q:

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:443/1*wAPIEXml1jsi5J5Z4TivyQ.png)
* After generating the initial r and ˆa, the prober assesses whether retrieval is necessary. To do this, we extract hidden state representations T′′lk of the r and ˆa tokens from intermediate layers and feed them into the probers assigned to each layer to generate logit values.
* decision to perform additional retrieval is based on the sum of the probers’ logit values.
* If the difference between the logit for retrieval necessity and the logit indicating no need for retrieval is higher than the threshold θ, additional documents dkj1 are retrieved based on the following conditions:

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:440/1*3ifrmnuLBtZF3Rg3PnnPCQ.png)
* In subsequent iterations, the LLM uses these retrieved documents along with q to generate updated versions of r and ˆa.
* This iterative process continues until either no further retrieval is needed or the maximum number of iterations is reached.

## Limitations

* Probing-RAG method is limited to open-source LLMs, as it is incompatible with certain APIs where hidden state access is restricted
* due to resource constraints, didn’t tested the prober on a broader range of models, including those with hyper-scale models such as 70B parameters, or validate its effectiveness on domain-specific datasets, which may limit its generalizability

## Conclusion

* introduce Probing-RAG, an efficient adaptive retrieval pipeline that uses a pretrained prober to determine whether additional retrieval is necessary by utilizing the language model’s hidden states
* show that Probing-RAG outperforms previous methods for various open-domain QA datasets
* show that Probing-RAG reduces overhead through appropriate retrieval calls by effectively leveraging both external and internal knowledge via the prober.
