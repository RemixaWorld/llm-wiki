When required to generate responses of a specific length, large language models often struggle to meet users’ needs due to their inherent difficulty in accurately perceiving numerical constraints. In this paper[1], authors introduce a novel, model-agnostic approach called RULER, which employs Meta Length Tokens (MLTs) to enhance the instruction-following ability of large language models under length-constrained instructions. Existing LLMs has limitations in capability to follow instructions for generating texts of a specified length.

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:575/1*Nr0uOqaIzVbWCJLtq5K3Ew.png)

Key contributions:

* introduce the Target Length Generation Task (TLG), which designed to assess the instruction following capability of LLMs. It evaluates how well models generate responses of target lengths as directed by instructions.
* propose RULER, a novel and model-agnostic approach which employs the Meta Length Tokens (MLTs). Through end-to-end training, it enables models to generate response matching the target lengths indicated by MLTs

## Can LLMs Accurately Generate with Target Length?

This section involves examining the capability of LLMs to generate responses of a target length.

### i) Target Length Generation Task

* this task was developed to assess the models’ ability in producing responses that match target lengths as directed designed target lengths as detailed in Table below

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:700/1*wQNsfCz2ukD6a9stxjV8Eg.png)
* these nine target lengths are divided into three levels: Level:0, Level:1, and Level:2
* developed two metrics to evaluate the accuracy of response lengths :

**a) Precise Match (PM)**

* This metric requires that the length of the generated response be very close to the target length
* For different Level, a precise tolerance range is set (±10,±20, . . . ) necessitating that the response length stringently conforms to these defined limits

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:494/1*H1xKKrQblfZAXDw3kyF8cQ.png)

**b) Flexible Match (FM)**

* This metric requires a broader tolerance interval for target length.
* For longer texts, the range incrementally widens to meet response generation requirements.

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:495/1*YB1yA9z2vJ6XyaDt1-d4Aw.png)

where: ci denotes the i-th response generated by LLM. The function L(·) calculates the word count of the input string. lbPTLi and ubPTLi denote the lower and upper bounds of the precise match range associated with the target length of i-th response. lbFTLi and ubFTLi denote the lower and upper bounds of the flexible match range associated with the target length of i-th response.

* Table below displays the PM and FM scores of open-source models at different Levels.

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:700/1*rfC7NHgR8dRp503pjGjMBw.png)
* Generally, models with advanced capabilities achieve higher PM and FM scores, indicating stronger adherence to instructions. This observation aligns with human expectations
* For most models, scores are lowest at Level:2, while scores at Level:1 are the highest, which can be attributed to prevalence of shorter responses in the training datasets utilized for model fine-tuning,which influences their generative biases

poor performance in TLG can be attributed to a discrepancy between the token counts generated by LLMs and the lengths as understood by humans due to several factors like:

* Tokenization Schemes: LLMs employ subword tokenization schemes that decompose words into smaller units of varying lengths.
* Model Training: Most LLMs are not explicitly trained with objectives that prioritize output length, which often makes length control a secondary effect rather than a primary training goal

This can only be solved by explicitly training models to know the concept of length

## RULER: Meta Length Token Controlled Generation

### i) Method

RULER as an approach is introduced to effectively control the response length of LLMs using MLTs, which can explicitly communicate length requirements within instructions. Proposed end-to-end training enables the LLMs to automatically generate MLTs in various scenarios, regardless of target length requirements.

![RULER Overview](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:700/1*wx2rP1grHwbDIk-Wb2kpyw.png)

* MLTs as shown in figure below offer more precise control than traditional text prompt methods, which often prove insufficiently constraining.

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:493/1*Im2J5Pf6u79XBDFTcYCBoQ.png)

**a) Data collection for RULER**

* Based on the predefined MLTs as shown in Table above and their range of variation, we aim to match each y to a corresponding mlt based on its word count.
* If a match is found, the data is reformatted as (x, mlt, y). This method aids in the construction of the fine-tuning training dataset DMLT

**b) RULER learning**

* positioned the MLT immediately before the original response during the construction of fine-tuning data. This strategy maintains the model chat template.
* Consequently, the combination of mlt and the original response y forms a new complete response y′.
* training of the RULER M was conducted on the curated corpus DMLT , which is augmented with Meta Length Tokens DMLT , employing the standard next token objective

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:446/1*eLb1otqH7-mk7ShJGjgcpA.png)
* Then we concatenate the MLT directly to the beginning of y to compute the loss and use the MLTs to expand the original vocabulary V

### ii) RULER Inference

**a) Target Length Generation (TLG) scenario**

* user’s instruction specifies a target length, decomposed into a question and a target length
* The RULER converts this target length into the corresponding MLT and appends it to the model chat template.
* Subsequent to the MLT, RULER generates response that aligns with the target length, ensuring compliance with both the user’s question and the target length, as illustrated in Figure above of RULER Overview

**b) non-TLG scenario**

* users provide straightforward instructions consisting solely of a question.
* RULER integrates these instructions directly into the model’s chat template for generation
* RULER autonomously generates a MLT prior to producing the textual response. This MLT is designed to match the length of the content generated, thereby ensuring normal generation of the model in non-TLG scenarios.

## Conclusion

* This study initially investigate the instruction following abilities of LLMs and introduces Target Length Generation Task (TLG).
* propose RULER, a novel and model-agnostic method that controls generated length for LLMs.RULER utilizes the MLT and end-to-end training to enhance model performance.
* Experimental results demonstrate that substantial improvements in PM and FM scores across various models
