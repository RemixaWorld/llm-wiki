# SEALONG : approach for LLMs to self-improve in long-context reasoning tasks

Existing approaches typically involve fine-tuning LLMs with synthetic data, which depends on annotations from human experts or advanced models like GPT-4, thus restricting further advancements. To address it, this paper[1] propose SEALONG, an approach for LLMs to self-improve in long-context reasoning. It involves sampling multiple outputs for each question, score them with Minimum Bayes Risk, and then apply supervised fine-tuning or preference optimization based on these outputs.

## Potential of LLMs in Long-context Reasoning

### i) Prompting Strategies for long-context reasoning

examined following strategies:

**a) Default:** Prompting the LLM with the long context and a question.

**b) Direct Answer:** Asking the LLM to directly answer the question based on the long context.

**c) Think Step-by-step:** Providing the LLM with the context, question, and an instruction to think step-by-step.

**d) Fact-and-reflection:** Providing the LLM with the long context, question, and an instruction to first identify the relevant information from the long context, and then carry out step-by-step reasoning and provide the answer.

**e) Plan-and-solve:** Providing the LLM with the long context, question, and an instruction to first devise a plan and then follow it to solve the problem step-by-step.

### ii) Potential of LLMs for Correct Long-context Reasoning

* used temperature sampling to produce multiple outputs per question, evaluate each with SubEM, and designate the highest-scoring output as the oracle sample.
* Figure below shows Scaling up the number of sampled outputs improves the performance of both the oracle sample and MBR decoding, with results based on Llama-3.1–8B-Instruct.

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:700/1*VA5MO-kySbla4EOHobnStg.png)
* figure above shows that there is a notable gap between oracle performance and that of greedy search, even with just 8 outputs. Scaling up to 128 samples achieves over 90% correct answers
* results above underscore the potential of LLMs for long-context reasoning and motivate the development of methods that enable LLMs to self-improve in this area.

## SEALONG

Overview of SEALONG is presented in figure below:

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:700/1*NLxhYQ8MVYOSOm0fFhqqKQ.png)

consists of following two stages:

### i) Self-supervision

* begin by leveraging plan-and-solve prompting to sample multiple reasoning trajectories for each question and its corresponding long context
* Based on idea that correct reasoning trajectories typically exhibit higher semantic consistency, used Minimum Bayes Risk (MBR) to assign a score, which prioritizes outputs that exhibit higher consistency with others
* In MBR, quality of an output is assessed by its expected utility under the model distribution

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:348/1*gP0X6lJaU-orUlScFVmScA.png)

s(y) is the score assigned to output y, where x denotes the input, including the long context, question and instruction. term πθ(y | x) represents the policy distribution of the LLM and the utility metric u(y, y∗) assesses y based on y∗.

* approximate this expectation using the Monte Carlo method with N sampled outputs, to measures the consistency between two outputs:

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:294/1*wJsLZvOxvm4aPnmxzmCO0w.png)
* used a lightweight RoBERTa-based model to embed outputs and measure similarity with the inner product
* assigned each output y a score s(y), and selected the output with the highest score is referred to as MBR decoding.

### ii) Fine-tuning

**a) Supervised Fine-tuning**

* Fine-tuning is then conducted using highest-scoring output
* minimize the negative log-likelihood of the output as follows:

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:421/1*FprIVN5dcKBJKrR3FaazPg.png)

where y denotes the MBR decoding output.

**b) Preference Optimization**

* Alternatively, preference optimization can be done to reinforce the tendency toward high-scoring outputs and reduce the likelihood of low-scoring outputs
* both high-scoring and low-scoring outputs are used here
* adopted the monolithic odds ratio preference optimization (ORPO) algorithm due to its strong empirical performance
* ORPO introduces an odds ratio loss to minimize the negative log odds ratio between a preferred output yw and a less-preferred output yl:

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:421/1*sVsPNOCCkSL0GWVT4V90TQ.png)

where, σ represents the sigmoid function, and oddsθ(y|x) measures how much more likely y is to be generated than not

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:329/1*ueq1fjCnqMP_viqXzpshrA.png)

* final objective in ORPO combines SFT and OR losses, with a hyperparameter β controlling their relative importance

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:307/1*YmWn18-qGYVgTEr528yWPg.png)
* in this implementation, used the MBR decoding output as yw and randomly select a low-scoring output to serve as yl.

## Experiments

### i) Implementation

* SEALONG requires query and long-context pairs to synthesize training data
* leveraged the training dataset of MuSiQue[2], where each question is related to several Wikipedia documents
* To achieve a specified number of tokens in the context, we randomly sample some unrelated documents, shuffle them with the related ones and concatenate them into a single context.
* use the original questions in MuSiQue without the annotated answer, relying on the LLM to produce self-supervision
* conduct experiments using the Llama-3.1 models, Qwen-2.5 models, with jina-embeddings-v3 serving as the sentence embedding model.
* ORPO is used as the default fine-tuning method

## Limitations

* substantial performance gap remains between the highest MBR-scored output and the oracle sample
* reliance on MuSiQue for synthetic data, which consists of multi-hop questions spanning multiple paragraphs, and it dose not cover all challenging question types, such as those requiring full-context reasoning, which remains a key limitation of current long-context LLMs
* restricted the implementation of SEALONG to LLMs with up to 14B parameters, though its effectiveness at larger scales warrants further investigation.

## Conclusion

* investigate the potential of LLMs to self-improve in long-context reasoning and propose SEALONG for this purpose
* This method achieves substantial improvements across multiple long-context reasoning tasks
