**Tired of AI that only understands words? Discover how Multimodal Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) is revolutionizing how we interact with complex information, bringing images and tables into the conversation.**

Artificial Intelligence has made incredible strides, especially with Large Language Models (LLMs) that can write, summarize, and chat. A popular technique to make these LLMs more knowledgeable and factual is **Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG)**. Traditionally, RAG systems have been superheroes of the text world, diving into vast document libraries to find relevant snippets and help LLMs generate accurate, context-aware answers.

But what happens when the “documents” aren’t just text? Real-world information is a rich tapestry of text, images, charts, and tables. Think financial reports, product catalogs, scientific papers, or even social media feeds. Most current RAG systems hit a wall here. This article dives into the exciting world of **Multimodal RAG systems** , exploring how they bridge this gap and why they’re the next big leap in AI-powered information retrieval. We’ll look at cutting-edge API solutions and how you can build your own Multimodal RAG Systems.

## The Trouble with Text-Only: Why Traditional RAG Falls Short

Imagine a standard text-based RAG setup. You feed it documents, it breaks them into chunks, creates “embeddings” (numerical representations of the text’s meaning), and stores them in a vector database. When you ask a question, the system finds the most similar text chunks and feeds them to an LLM to generate an answer.

![image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:700/0*jYP9VKSKBc-wgKn6.jpeg)

This works brilliantly for text. But if your documents contain crucial images (like an infographic explaining company profits) or complex tables, the traditional approach stumbles:

1. **Ignoring Visuals:** Most systems simply ignore non-text elements.
2. **Captioning as a Crutch:** A common workaround is to use a Vision Language Model (VLM) to generate text descriptions (captions) for images and tables. These captions are then embedded alongside the text.

**The Problem:** This is like describing a Van Gogh painting over the phone. You lose a _massive_ amount of contextual information. The quality of the description heavily depends on the VLM used and the prompts given. Nuance, intricate details, and the direct visual relationships are often lost in translation.

What if we could directly “show” the AI the images and tables, much like we do with text?

## The New Cohere Solution

Enter **Cohere** , a frontier foundation model company. They recently introduced **Embed v4** , a multimodal embedding model designed specifically for multimodal search. Their benchmarks are impressive, showing state-of-the-art results on vision-based retrieval, even outperforming specialized models. The game-changer? Embed v4 generates **fixed-size embeddings** , making them readily compatible with your favorite vector stores.

## How Multimodal RAG Works: The Modern Workflow

The workflow for a modern multimodal RAG system is elegantly powerful:

1. **Image Embedding:** Instead of just text, you now directly embed the images (and potentially associated text/captions) from your documents. For PDFs, this means converting pages into images first. Cohere’s Embed v4 can handle this, creating a single, robust embedding for each item.
2. **Vector Storage:** These multimodal embeddings are stored in a vector database, just like text embeddings.
3. **Query Embedding:** When a user asks a question, the same Embed v4 model is used to create an embedding for the query.
4. **Retrieval:** The system searches the vector database for images whose embeddings are most similar to the query’s embedding.
5. **Generation:** The retrieved images (and any associated text) are then passed, along with the original user query, to a powerful multimodal LLM (like Google’s Gemini or OpenAI’s GPT-4V). This model can “see” the images and “read” the text to generate a comprehensive answer.

Further, because high data storage costs lead to reduced ROI on technology investments, they designed Embed 4 to output compressed embeddings. This helps organizations to save up to 83% on storage costs while maintaining search accuracy. Compression can occur on the format precision of the vectors (binary, int8, and fp32) and the dimension of the vectors.

## Why Multimodal RAG is a Game-Changer

The ability to directly process and reason over images and tables within a RAG framework opens up a world of possibilities:

* **Deeper Understanding:** AI can finally grasp the rich information embedded in visuals, leading to more accurate and comprehensive answers.
* **Reduced Pre-processing:** No more struggling with complex captioning pipelines that lose information.
* **New Applications:** Unlocking insights from visually rich documents in finance, healthcare, engineering, research, and more.
* **Enhanced Enterprise Search:** Imagine an internal search engine that can find _that specific chart_ in a 200-page report based on a natural language query. This is a massive unsolved problem in many organizations.

Multimodal RAG isn’t just an incremental improvement; it’s a fundamental shift in how AI interacts with and understands the diverse formats of human knowledge.

## The Future is Multimodal

As models like Cohere’s Embed v4 become more accessible we’re on the cusp of a new era for information retrieval. The line between text-based and vision-based AI is blurring, leading to systems that are far more capable and aligned with how humans naturally process information.

Whether you’re leveraging powerful APIs or building private, local systems, incorporating multimodal capabilities into your RAG pipelines is no longer a futuristic dream — it’s a rapidly evolving reality with immense potential.
