Jeff Bezos was known for keeping an empty chair in meetings to represent the customer. This chair served as a visual reminder of customer obsession, prompting someone to ask, “What would the customer say about this?” It forced employees to think from the customer’s perspective, acting as the customer’s hypothetical proxy.

With the rise of LLM-powered agents, we are witnessing a significant leap forward in this concept. Companies can now create ‘customer personas’ — internal agents capable of analyzing vast amounts of customer data, from reviews and interactions to business plans and competitive offerings. Over time, these personas will offer real-time insights with a depth and breadth that far surpasses what any human employee could match. Imagine having a permanent fixture (akin to Alexa) which participates in meetings as a customer. The key point? Customers can now speak for themselves, making the empty chair more than just the figurative (perhaps literally?) presence of the customer. It now has a “butt in seat” — a brain and a voice, really.

But the purpose of this article isn’t just to breathe life into the empty chair. It’s also to discuss an important question many companies face when adopting LLMs: **Which use case should we prioritize in our first wave of investments?** The Customer Persona emerges as a leading candidate, and here’s why:

## Value Proposition

Historically, customer data has been scattered across departments, locked in reports, or accessed only through time-consuming processes. The Customer Persona changes all of that.

* **Real-time Insights** : Imagine every strategic conversation, product brainstorm, or marketing campaign enriched with direct, relevant, and real-time customer insights. No need for days of analysis. Customer perspectives can now be injected immediately into decision-making processes.
* **Better-Informed Decisions** : For executives, this is more than just speeding up decisions — it’s about making smarter, data-driven decisions.
* **Precision & Personalization**: The Customer Persona leverages not only internal company data but also external factors such as competitor offerings and industry trends. Instead of guessing what your customers might want, you can know with precision.
* **Scalability** : This persona isn’t static. It can be rapidly updated and scaled as new data sources become available, ensuring you’re always acting on the most current customer sentiment.
* **Increased Confidence** : By drawing insights from vast datasets, executives gain confidence in high-stakes decisions. The comprehensive understanding of your customers significantly reduces risk, making it invaluable in navigating today’s competitive landscape.

## **The Permanence**

Whenever Jeff Bezos was asked, “What’s going to change in the future?”, he would respond with, “I look for what’s _never going to change_ and invest there, instead.” The need for a deep understanding of customers falls squarely into this category. No matter how much AI or market conditions evolve, companies will **always** need to know their customers intimately — their preferences, behaviors, available options, complaints, opinions, and shifting patterns.

It’s not a question of **if** companies will adopt such personas, but rather a question of **when** , **how** , and **how quickly**. The absence of timely investment in it could in fact pose significant business risk. Given the increasing complexity of customer data and the competitive advantages it unlocks, this is a “no-brainer” investment that aligns with the timeless business principle of customer obsession.

## Barrier to Entry

For many companies exploring LLM applications, the challenge lies in the level of accuracy required and the appetite for risk. LLMs still face well-documented accuracy challenges, including hallucinations. If your use case requires near-perfect precision, LLMs may not be suitable (not yet) without heavy investments. However, the Customer Persona is an excellent fit for LLMs because:

* **Internal-facing Tool** : Since the Customer Persona is an internal tool, the risk of backlash from occasional inaccuracies is minimal. It’s for internal decision-making, not direct customer interaction, which lowers the stakes.
* **Directional Accuracy** : Even insights that are directionally accurate mark a vast improvement over the status quo. Many companies have customer insights trapped in unstructured data — reviews, transcripts, customer interactions, and internal reports — that are rarely surfaced in real time. Unlocking just a fraction of this data is a huge leap forward, even if it’s not perfectly accurate. .

This lower accuracy threshold has two key advantages:

* **Get Started Immediately** : You don’t need a fully fleshed-out, ideal customer persona to start. Yes, there may be a day with an device with an actual voice replacing the empty chair. But in the meanwhile, with the data you already have — transcripts, reviews, internal reports, and data scraped from forums — you can begin building a useful customer persona today. Even incremental steps toward this vision offer significant value. Perfection shouldn’t stand in the way of progress.
* **Plug-and-Play Solutions** : In the past 20 months, RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) approaches have advanced significantly. There are now plug-and-play options that can ingest your existing data and deliver results quickly. These tools offer low-hanging fruit, allowing companies to get started quickly and easily.

## The Road Ahead

The Customer Persona is just the beginning. As AI continues to evolve, we will see this concept extend into other areas of business. As was evident through the Google event last week (Gemini At Work), we’re already seeing the proliferation of agents — customer facing, employee-facing, creative agents, data agents and more. The possibilities are endless.

Consider the landscape of agents today, much like the mobile app landscape in 2007 when the iPhone launched. Few could have predicted the number of apps we would have today, and the same applies to agents in 2024. As Microsoft AI’s CEO, Mustafa Suleyman said — “we’re building a digital species.” And, these don’t necessarily take 9 months to be born.

## **Key Takeaway for Executives**

There are use cases with permanent, long-term applicability, as well as quick, scrappy solutions that don’t require high accuracy to deliver value. Many of these use cases are ready for immediate investment, and the Customer Persona is a prime example — a no-brainer. It’s a powerful starting point with low risk and high reward, and it’s only the tip of the iceberg. The opportunities for leveraging AI in your business extend far beyond this, but the Customer Persona is a great place to begin.
