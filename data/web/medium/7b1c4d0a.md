PDF files can be challenging to convert into other formats, often locking substantial information in a format that is inaccessible to AI applications. If we could transform PDF files or their corresponding images into structured or semi-structured formats that are machine-readable, it would significantly alleviate this problem. This can also significantly enhance the knowledge base of artificial intelligence applications.

**This series of articles is dedicated to demystifying PDF Parsing.** In the [first article](<https://medium.com/generative-ai/demystifying-pdf-parsing-01-overview-130f9e4064c2>) of this series, we introduced the main task of PDF parsing, categorized existing methods, and provided brief introductions to each. And in the [second article](</demystifying-pdf-parsing-02-pipeline-based-method-82619dbcbddf>) of this series, we focused on the pipeline-based method.

**This article is the third in the series, introducing another approach to PDF parsing: OCR-free small model-based method**. We begin with an overview, then introduce the principles of various representative OCR-free small model-based PDF parsing solutions. Lastly, we share the insights and thoughts we’ve gained.

Please note that the “small model” referred to in this article is relatively small compared to large multimodal models, typically having fewer than 1.5 billion parameters.

## Overview

The previously introduced [pipeline-based PDF parsing method](</demystifying-pdf-parsing-02-pipeline-based-method-82619dbcbddf>) primarily uses OCR engines for text recognition. However, it results in high computational costs, inflexibility with regard to language and document type, and potential OCR errors that may impact subsequent tasks.

Therefore, OCR-free methods should be developed, as illustrated in Figure 1. They do not use OCR to recognize text explicitly. Instead, they use neural networks to implicitly complete the task. Essentially, these methods adopt an end-to-end approach, directly outputting the results of PDF parsing.

Press enter or click to view image in full size

Figure 1: OCR-free small model-based method. Image by author.

From a structural perspective, OCR-free methods are simpler compared to pipeline-based methods. The primary aspects of OCR-free methods that require attention are the design of model structure and the construction of training data.

Next, we will introduce a few representative OCR-free small model-based PDF parsing frameworks:

* [Donut](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.15664>): OCR-free document understanding transformer.
* [Nougat](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.13418>): Based on the Donut architecture, it’s particularly effective when used with PDF papers, formulas, and tables.
* [Pix2Struct](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.03347>): Screenshot Parsing as Pretraining for Visual Language Understanding.

## Donut

As illustrated in Figure 2, [Donut](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.15664>) is an end-to-end model designed for the comprehensive understanding of document images. Its architecture is straightforward, comprising a transformer-based visual encoder and a text decoder module.

Press enter or click to view image in full size

Figure 2: The architecture of Donut. Source: [Donut](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.15664>).

Donut does not depend on any modules related to OCR. Instead, it uses a visual encoder to extract features from document images and directly generates token sequences using a text decoder. The output sequence can then be converted into a structured format like JSON.

[The code](<https://github.com/clovaai/donut/blob/4cfcf972560e1a0f26eb3e294c8fc88a0d336626/donut/model.py#L373>) is as follows:

```python class DonutModel(PreTrainedModel): r""" Donut: an E2E OCR-free Document Understanding Transformer. The encoder maps an input document image into a set of embeddings, the decoder predicts a desired token sequence, that can be converted to a structured format, given a prompt and the encoder output embeddings """ config_class = DonutConfig base_model_prefix = "donut" def __init__(self, config: DonutConfig): super().__init__(config) self.config = config self.encoder = SwinEncoder( input_size=self.config.input_size, align_long_axis=self.config.align_long_axis, window_size=self.config.window_size, encoder_layer=self.config.encoder_layer, name_or_path=self.config.name_or_path, ) self.decoder = BARTDecoder( max_position_embeddings=self.config.max_position_embeddings, decoder_layer=self.config.decoder_layer, name_or_path=self.config.name_or_path, ) def forward(self, image_tensors: torch.Tensor, decoder_input_ids: torch.Tensor, decoder_labels: torch.Tensor): """ Calculate a loss given an input image and a desired token sequence, the model will be trained in a teacher-forcing manner Args: image_tensors: (batch_size, num_channels, height, width) decoder_input_ids: (batch_size, sequence_length, embedding_dim) decode_labels: (batch_size, sequence_length) """ encoder_outputs = self.encoder(image_tensors) decoder_outputs = self.decoder( input_ids=decoder_input_ids, encoder_hidden_states=encoder_outputs, labels=decoder_labels, ) return decoder_outputs ... ... ```

### Encoder

Donut utilizes [Swin-Transformer](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.14030>) as image encoder because it has exhibited superior performance in preliminary document parsing research. This image encoder transforms the input document image into a set of high-dimensional embeddings. These embeddings will be used as input for text decoder.

[The corresponding code](<https://github.com/clovaai/donut/blob/4cfcf972560e1a0f26eb3e294c8fc88a0d336626/donut/model.py#L27>) is as follows.

```python class SwinEncoder(nn.Module): r""" Donut encoder based on SwinTransformer Set the initial weights and configuration with a pretrained SwinTransformer and then modify the detailed configurations as a Donut Encoder Args: input_size: Input image size (width, height) align_long_axis: Whether to rotate image if height is greater than width window_size: Window size(=patch size) of SwinTransformer encoder_layer: Number of layers of SwinTransformer encoder name_or_path: Name of a pretrained model name either registered in huggingface.co. or saved in local. otherwise, `swin_base_patch4_window12_384` will be set (using `timm`). Donut initializes the decoder model weights using the weights from the publicly available, pre-trained, multilingual BART model.

Text Decoder’s output is the generated token sequence.

### Training

**Pre-training**

The goal of pre-training is to minimize the cross-entropy loss of next token prediction. This is achieved by jointly conditioning on the image and previous contexts. This task resembles a pseudo-OCR task. The model is essentially trained as a visual language model over visual corpora, such as document images.

The training data used is the [IIT-CDIP](<https://ir.cs.georgetown.edu/downloads/sigir06cdipcoll_v05-with-authors.pdf>), a collection of 11 million scanned English document images. Meanwhile, [Synthetic Document Generator (SynthDoG)](<https://github.com/clovaai/donut/blob/4cfcf972560e1a0f26eb3e294c8fc88a0d336626/synthdog/README.md>) was employed to produce multi-language data, including [English](<https://huggingface.co/datasets/naver-clova-ix/synthdog-en>), [Chinese](<https://huggingface.co/datasets/naver-clova-ix/synthdog-zh>), [Japanese](<https://huggingface.co/datasets/naver-clova-ix/synthdog-ja>), and [Korean](<https://huggingface.co/datasets/naver-clova-ix/synthdog-ko>). It generated 0.5 million images for each language.

### Fine-tuning

The primary purpose of fine-tuning is to adapt to downstream tasks.

For instance, in a document classification task, decoder is trained to generate a token sequence `[START class][memo][END class]`. This sequence can be directly converted into a JSON format like `{"class": "memo"}`.

## Nougat

[**Nougat**](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.13418>) is an end-to-end, OCR-free small model introduced in August 2023. It can directly parse the content of images. It accepts images scanned from literary works or those converted from PDFs as input, and produces markdown as output.

### Model Architecture

[Nougat](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.13418>) is developed on the Donut architecture. It recognizes text via neural network implicitly, eliminating the need for any OCR-related input or modules, as demonstrated in Figure 5.

Press enter or click to view image in full size

Figure 5: End-to-end architecture following Donut. The Swin Transformer encoder takes a document image and converts it into latent embeddings, which are subsequently converted to a sequence of tokens in a autoregressive manner. Source: [Nougat](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.13418>).

### Construction of Training Dataset

Nougat’s model is not particularly innovative; its primary emphasis is on constructing a large training dataset, which is a challenging task.

Nougat has implemented a cost-effective approach by creating large-scale training data consisting of pairs of images and markdown. **This is the most valuable aspect of Nougat to learn.**

**Data Source**

Due to the absence of a large-scale dataset containing PDF images and markdown pairs, Nougat has constructed datasets from three sources: [arXiv](<http://arxiv.org/>), [PMC](<http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/>) (PubMed Central), and [IDL](<http://www.industrydocuments.ucsf.edu/>) (Industry Documents Library), as shown in Figure 6.

**Get Images as Input**

The process of [obtaining the image of PDF page](<https://github.com/facebookresearch/nougat/blob/47c77d70727558b4a2025005491ecb26ee97f523/nougat/dataset/split_htmls_to_pages.py#L113>) is relatively simple; just directly [use the relevant APIs of PyPDFium2](<https://github.com/facebookresearch/nougat/blob/47c77d70727558b4a2025005491ecb26ee97f523/nougat/dataset/rasterize.py#L18>).

```python def rasterize_paper( pdf: Union[Path, bytes], outpath: Optional[Path] = None, dpi: int = 96, return_pil=False, pages=None,) -> Optional[List[io.BytesIO]]: """ Rasterize a PDF file to PNG images. **Get Markdown as Label**

As shown in Figure 7, to obtain markdown, we must first convert the TeX source code into HTML files. We can then parse and format these files into markdown.

This involves two challenges.

**The first challenge** is figuring out how to paginate the Markdown, because training data consists of images from each PDF page, with the corresponding markdown as labels.

As the LaTeX source files for each paper haven’t been recompiled, we can’t automatically determine the page breaks of the PDF file in the same way a LaTeX compiler would.

To achieve this goal, it is necessary to utilize the currently available resources. The strategy is to heuristically match the text from the original PDF page with the Markdown text.

Specifically, first, [use PDFMiner](<https://github.com/facebookresearch/nougat/blob/47c77d70727558b4a2025005491ecb26ee97f523/nougat/dataset/utils/pdf_text_extract.py#L68>) to [extract text lines from the PDF](<https://github.com/facebookresearch/nougat/blob/47c77d70727558b4a2025005491ecb26ee97f523/nougat/dataset/split_md_to_pages.py#L281>), and then preprocess the text to remove page numbers and potential headers or footers. Then, [train a tfidf_transformer model](<https://github.com/facebookresearch/nougat/blob/47c77d70727558b4a2025005491ecb26ee97f523/nougat/dataset/split_md_to_pages.py#L325>) using the PDF lines as input and the page numbers as labels. Next, [apply the trained model](<https://github.com/facebookresearch/nougat/blob/47c77d70727558b4a2025005491ecb26ee97f523/nougat/dataset/split_md_to_pages.py#L326>) to divide the Markdown into paragraphs and predict the page numbers for each paragraph.

## Pix2Struct

[Pix2Struct](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.03347>) is a pre-trained image-to-text model specifically designed for pure visual language understanding. Moreover, it can be fine-tuned for many downstream tasks.

### Model Architecture

[Pix2Struct](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.03347>) is an image-encoder-text-decoder based on [ViT](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.11929>).

Since Pix2Struct’s architecture is not illustrated in the paper and it cannot be found elsewhere online, I provide a reference diagram here, based on the ViT architecture, as depicted in Figure 8.

Press enter or click to view image in full size

Figure 8: The architecture of Pix2Struct. Inspired by [ViT](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.11929>).

Using the [standard ViT](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.11929>) method, which scales the input image to a predefined resolution before extracting fixed-size blocks, can have two negative impacts:

* It can distort the true aspect ratio, which could differ significantly for documents, mobile UIs, and graphics.
* Transferring the model to downstream tasks with higher resolution becomes challenging, as the model only observes a specific resolution during pre-training.

Therefore, Pix2Struct has introduced a minor enhancement, which allows for aspect-ratio preserving scaling of the input image, either up or down, as shown in Figure 9.

Press enter or click to view image in full size

Figure 9: Comparison of variable resolution inputs and the typical fixed resolution input. Source: [Pix2Struct](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.03347>).

### Pre-training **Tasks**

Pix2Struct propose a screenshot parsing objective that requires predicting an HTML-based parse from a masked screenshot of a web page.

* Masking inputs encourages joint reasoning about their co-occurrence.
* Using simplified HTML as output is advantageous as it provides clear signals about text, images, and layouts.

Figure 10: Toy illustration of input-output pairs (right) sampled from the original web page (left). Source: [Pix2Struct](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.03347>).

As shown in Figure 10, the screenshot parsing proposed by Pix2Struct effectively combines signals from several well-known pre-training strategies:

* Recover the unmasked part. This task is similar to OCR, a fundamental skill for understanding language. The use of synthetic rendering or OCR output for OCR pre-training has also been proposed in Donut. In Figure 10, predicting `<C++>` is an example of this learning signal.
* Recovering the masked part. This task is similar to the masked language modeling in BERT. However, a key difference is that visual context often provides additional powerful clues. For instance, predicting `<Python>` in Figure 10 is an example of this type of signal.
* Recovering alt-text from images. This is a commonly used method for pre-training image titling strategies. In this approach, the model is permitted to use web pages as extra context. For instance, predicting `img alt=C++`, as shown in Figure 10, exemplifies this learning signal.

Pix2Struct has pre-trained two model variants:

* A base model consisting of 282M parameters.
* A large model consisting of 1.3B parameters.

### Pre-training Dataset

The goal of pre-training is to equip Pix2Struct with the ability to represent the basic structure of an input image. To achieve this, Pix2Struct generates self-supervised pairs of input images and target texts based on the URLs in the [C4 corpus](<https://www.tensorflow.org/datasets/catalog/c4>).

Pix2Struct have collected 80 million screenshots, each paired with its HTML source file. This constitutes about one third of the total number of documents. Each screenshots is 1024 pixels wide, with the height adjusting to match the content’s height. And the obtained HTML source file will be converted to simplified HTML.

## Insights and Thoughts

The introduction to representative OCR-free solutions ends here, now let’s talk about insights and thoughts.

### About Pre-Training Task

In order to comprehensively understand the layout, text, and semantic information from images or PDFs, Donut, Nougat, and Pix2Struct have devised similar training tasks:

* Donut: Image → JSON-like format
* Nougat: Image → Markdown
* Pix2Struct: Masked Image → Simplified HTML

If we aim to develop our own OCR-free PDF parsing tool, our initial step should be to design the training tasks. It’s crucial to consider the desired output format and the challenges of acquiring corresponding training data.

### About Pre-Training Data

Training data is crucial for OCR-free methods.

Obtaining training data for Donut and Nougat is challenging as (Image, JSON) and (image, Markdown) pairs are not readily available.

Conversely, Pix2Struct is adapted directly from web pages sourced from public datasets, making data acquisition more convenient. However, as Pix2Struct’s training data comes from web pages, it could potentially introduce harmful content. Multimodal models are particularly sensitive to this. Pix2Struct has not yet implemented measures to address this harmful content.

If we aim to develop an OCR-free PDF parsing tool, one strategy is to use public data to gradually construct (input, output) pairs for training.

Furthermore, determining the appropriate resolution for the input image and the number of PDF pages to include in one image are also important considerations.

### Limitations of OCR-Free Small Model-Based Method

* Although the [pipeline-based method](</demystifying-pdf-parsing-02-pipeline-based-method-82619dbcbddf>) involves multiple models, each one is lightweight. The total parameters might even be significantly fewer than those of OCR-free model. This factor contributes to the slower parsing speed of OCR-free model, which can pose a challenge for large-scale deployment. For example, despite being a small model, Nougat’s parameter volumes are 250MB or 350MB. However, its generation speed is slow, as stated in Nougat’s paper:

Press enter or click to view image in full size

Figure 18: Generation speed of Nougat. Source: [Nougat](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.13418>).

* Constructing the training dataset for this approach is costly. This is attributed to the need for building large-scale image-text pairs. Furthermore, it requires more GPUs and a longer training duration, adding to the machine cost.
* Additionally, end-to-end method can’t optimize for specific bad cases, resulting in higher optimization costs. In [pipeline-based solution](</demystifying-pdf-parsing-02-pipeline-based-method-82619dbcbddf>), if the table processing module underperforms, only this module needs optimization. However, for an end-to-end solution, without altering the model architecture, new fine-tuning data must be created. This could potentially lead to new bad cases in other scenarios, such as formula recognition.

## Conclusion

This article provided an overview of OCR-free small model-based method in PDF parsing. It delves into this approach using three representative models as examples, offering a detailed introduction and sharing derived insights.

Generally, the benefit of using OCR-free small model-based PDF parsing method is its one-step process, which avoids any potential damage from intermediate steps. However, its effectiveness relies heavily on the structure of the multimodal model and the quality of the training data. Moreover, it has a slower training and inference speed, making it less practical than the pipeline-based method. Also, this method’s interpretability is not as strong as that of the pipeline-based method.

Despite its need for improvements, OCR-free approach performs well in areas like table and formula recognition. These strengths provide valuable insights for us to build our own PDF parsing tools.
