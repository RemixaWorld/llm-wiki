# An Innovative RAG Idea for Multi-hop Question Answering

# **An Innovative RAG Idea for Multi-hop Question Answering**

In open-domain question answering, multi-hop question answering is complex and challenging. It requires the system to integrate information from multiple documents for multi-step reasoning to provide accurate answers.

In this artice, we introduce a new study called [“Retrieve, Summarize, Plan(ReSP)](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.13101>)”, a new iterative RAG method to improve the accuracy and efficiency of multi-hop question answering.

The architecture of this article is shown in Figure 1.

![Figure 1: The architecture of this article. Image by author.](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1000/1*8nFiX3hgZ_mvoPqlsE-9Ug.png)

## Motivation

Multi-hop question answering faces two main challenges:

1. **Context Overload** : Due to the significant increase in information during multi-turn retrieval, traditional methods often overlook key details or introduce noise when generating answers, leading to decreased accuracy.
2. **Over-Planning and Repetitive Planning** : Current iterative RAG methods often lack mechanisms to record the retrieval path, resulting in unnecessary retrieval even when sufficient information is already available (over-planning). They also may repeatedly generate sub-questions that have already been addressed, wasting computational resources and reducing efficiency (repetitive planning).

## Solution

[Retrieve, Summarize, Plan(ReSP)](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.13101>) features a dual-function summarizer that compresses information for both the overarching question and the current sub-question during each retrieval round. This effectively addresses context overload while optimizing the retrieval path to avoid over-planning and repetitive planning.

### Overview

![Figure 2: The ReSP framework consists of four modules: Reasoner, Retriever, Summarizer, and Generator. Source: ReSP.](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:700/1*4vAkT1r1RouSjRf0LfJABA.png)

The ReSP framework consists of four components:

* **Reasoner** : Decides whether to exit the iteration and generate a response based on the current memory queues or generate a new sub-question for further iteration.
* **Retriever** : Retrieves relevant documents from the corpus based on the sub-question provided by the reasoner.
* **Summarizer** : Performs dual summarization on the retrieved documents, updating the global evidence memory and local pathway memory.
* **Generator** : Generates the final answer based on the information in the memory queues.

Summary-Enhanced Iterative RAG Process involves several key steps, which is illustrated in Figure 2.

1. **Initial Document Retrieval** : Given a query Q and a document corpus D, the retriever identifies the top K documents relevant to Q.
2. **Dual Summarization** : These documents are processed by the dual-function summarizer, which: **Global Evidence Memory** : Summarizes information relevant to the overarching question; **Local Pathway Memory** : Generates responses for the current sub-question.
3. **Memory Queue Update** : The contents of the global evidence memory and local pathway memory are concatenated and used as contextual inputs for the reasoner.
4. **Reasoner Decision** : The reasoner evaluates whether the current information is sufficient to answer the overarching question. If sufficient, the iterative process stops, and the generator produces the final answer using the memory queues. If not, a new sub-question Q* is generated, ensuring it is distinct from previously retrieved sub-questions, and the process iterates.

This enhanced process ensures that critical information is retained and utilized effectively, mitigating the risks of over-planning and repetitive planning.

### Dual-Function Summarizer

This summarizer performs two key functions concurrently: summarizing global evidence and addressing local pathways.

* **Global Evidence Summarization** : The summarizer creates a summary of corroborative information from the retrieved documents for the overarching question. This summary is stored in the global evidence memory, which helps the model determine whether sufficient information has been gathered to answer the main question, thereby avoiding over-planning.
* **Local Pathway Summarization** : Simultaneously, the summarizer generates a response for the current sub-question based on the retrieved documents, storing this information in the local pathway memory. This ensures that each sub-question is addressed in detail, preventing repetitive planning by keeping track of previously retrieved sub-questions.

### Comparison of ReSP and traditional RAG

Traditional RAG methods typically use retrieved documents to generate answers in a single or iterative retrieval process. However, when handling multiple rounds of retrieval, they often lead to overly long contexts, introducing noise or missing critical information.

Additionally, traditional methods lack a mechanism to record the retrieval path, which can result in **over-planning** (unnecessary further retrieval) and **repetitive planning** (generating previously retrieved sub-questions).

ReSP distinguishes itself from traditional RAG in the following ways:

1. **Dual-Function Summarizer** : After each retrieval round, ReSP performs both global evidence summarization and local pathway summarization. The global evidence summarization helps the model determine whether enough information has been gathered to answer the main question, avoiding over-planning. The local pathway summarization records answers to sub-questions, preventing repetitive planning.
2. **Memory Queue Mechanism** : By updating the global evidence memory and local pathway memory, ReSP is able to clearly track the retrieved information and previously generated sub-questions, optimizing subsequent retrieval decisions.

## Evaluation

Experiments has been conducted on two multi-hop question-answering benchmark datasets: HotpotQA and 2WikiMultihopQA.

![Figure 3: Performance comparison on HotpotQA and 2WikiMultihopQA. We report the token-level F1 score of answer strings. All methods utilize fine-tuning-free Llama3–8B-instruct for generation. Source: ReSP.](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:700/1*02ri4qtMoqbAhAYI_HVY8Q.png)

As shown in Figure 3, the results demonstrate that ReSP significantly outperforms existing methods on both datasets:

* **HotpotQA** : ReSP improved the F1 score by 4.1 points compared to the state-of-the-art methods.
* **2WikiMultihopQA** : ReSP improved the F1 score by 5.9 points compared to the state-of-the-art methods.

![Figure 4: Impact of base model size on different modules. Source: ReSP.](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:700/1*93_zSaeYHooIIVIG2AJz3A.png)

Additionally, ReSP conducted several comparative experiments to examine the impact of model size on the performance of different modules and to verify the robustness of ReSP in handling context length. The findings indicate that using a larger base model can significantly enhance the performance of the generator module, while the reasoner and summarizer modules do not necessarily benefit from a larger model.

## Conclusion and Insights

This article introduces a new iterative RAG method, [ReSP](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.13101>), which effectively addresses context overload and repetitive planning issues in multi-hop question answering by incorporating a dual-function summarizer.

However, challenges remain, and there are some areas for improvement:

1. **Scalability of the Approach** : While the paper performs well on HotpotQA and 2WikiMultihopQA, which focus on specific types of multi-hop reasoning tasks, future work could explore applying the ReSP method to other task types (such as open-domain dialogue systems or generative QA systems) to verify its performance in broader application scenarios.
2. **Analysis of Model Complexity** : Although the paper provides a thorough experimental analysis of ReSP’s performance, it offers limited discussion on whether the introduction of the dual-function summarizer adds computational complexity, as well as the impact on model training and inference time. Future studies could further quantify the computational cost of this innovation, particularly in large-scale, real-world applications.
3. **Comparison with Other Modern Frameworks** : While it compares ReSP with existing single-round and iterative RAG methods, it could be enhanced by including comparisons with more recent large language model frameworks, especially in multi-modal models and emerging knowledge-enhanced language models, to further increase the paper’s breadth and authority.
