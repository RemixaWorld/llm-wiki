# Can AI Write a Scientific Paper?

## A deep dive analysis of Data-to-Paper that Uses LLMs to Automate Research, Hypothesis Testing, and Report Generation

Every year, we see big improvements in language models like ChatGPT. These models are not only good at writing text and answering questions, but they can now also handle coding, debugging, and even solve complex math problems. When combined with external tools or advanced techniques, they can even write entire books or help with medical diagnoses.

However, despite these impressive leaps, there’s still a domain where human intelligence holds its ground like **_scientific research_**. The scientific process is deeply rooted in human creativity, reasoning, and problem-solving.

> Scientific research is a complex, multi-step process that involves much more than just crunching numbers. It requires careful planning, interpretation of results, and meticulous documentation.

While scientists often follow established paths such as refining research questions, designing experiments, interpreting findings, and writing papers, the process is far from linear. Researchers often have to go back to previous steps, carefully trace information, and rely on feedback from colleagues and mentors.

Inspired by how scientists approach research, there’s a platform called “data-to-paper” that guides language models and algorithmic agents through the typical steps of hypothesis driven research. It’s not just about running analyses. It mimics the human process by incorporating feedback, review, and iterative refinement. The system can autonomously generate hypotheses, write and debug code, analyze data, and produce well-structured scientific papers. Importantly, it ensures that the entire research process is transparent and traceable so that others can verify and replicate the results.

In this blog I would like to share my explorations with the “data-to-paper” and also share my understanding of how it works, how it performs and my thoughts on how we could use AI for research effectively.

## How does it works?

To autonomously analyse a dataset and produce a research paper, “data-to-paper” makes use of multiple LLMs and rule-based agents to move through a series of predefined research steps. Each step is designed to generate specific outputs, whether it’s quantitative data or written text.

The process involves 17 distinct steps, including:

* **Data exploration**
* **Literature search**
* **Formulation of a research goal and hypothesis**
* **Creating a hypothesis testing plan**
* **Writing data analysis code**
* **Generating scientific tables**
* **Writing the paper section by section**

These steps work together to produce what are called “ _research products_ ” which are pieces of content like free text, LaTeX code, structured text, binary decisions, citations, Python code, and numerical data. The end goal is to create a complete, transparent, and traceable scientific paper.

Here’s a more detailed breakdown of each element and the overall process:

**1\. Starting Point: Human-Provided Dataset**
When a researcher provides a dataset and a textual description explaining its context, structure, or purpose. The dataset and its description serve as the foundational material for the AI to start the research process.

**2\. LLM Research Steps and Programmatic Tools**
Once the dataset is send as input, the system uses a series of LLM research steps and programmatic tools (represented by ellipses with gear icons). These tools automate different aspects of research, such as data analysis, hypothesis generation, writing, and coding, creating a seamless process from raw data to a publishable paper.

Each research step (columns) creates one or more research products (rows; “output”, blue), while using a provided subset of previously created products as inputs (“provided as context”, light blue). Products could be of different textual, structural, or numeric types . Numeric products can be provided not only as conversation context, but also as data files for code (centered dot). The raw data files and their descriptions are provided as human input products.

**3\. Fixed-Goal Modality**
In some cases, the research goal might already be defined by the human (e.g., answering a specific question or testing a hypothesis). In this scenario, known as the ”fixed-goal” modality the system skips the steps required to determine the research goal, depicted by a dashed bypass arrow. This means that instead of the system trying to figure out the research direction, it immediately starts executing the tasks toward achieving the predefined goal.

**4\. Modular System: Coding, Writing, Literature Search**
The workflow is divided into three major modules, each representing a specific set of tasks that the AI automates:

\- _Coding (Yellow)_ : These modules automates the writing of code necessary for data processing, analysis, or modeling.
— _Writing (Green)_ : These modules handles the creation of the textual content of the paper. LLMs are used to generate sections of the research paper, including introduction, methodology, results, and discussion.
— _Literature Search (Red)_ : These module searches for and retrieves relevant academic papers or sources that provide background information or context for the research. The AI reviews the literature and incorporates references into the paper.

**5\. Rule-Based and LLM Reviews**
After each module completes its tasks, the research products undergo a _rule-based review_. For example, in the coding module, there are several decision points (represented by diamond shapes) where the system performs static checks (e.g., code correctness and structure), runtime checks (ensuring the code runs as expected), and output checks (validating the generated outputs). This ensures the code is accurate, functional, and aligned with the research goals.

In addition to rule-based reviews, some steps involve an _LLM-based review_ (depicted as dark ellipses), where the AI system itself evaluates the quality and correctness of the work, providing an additional layer of scrutiny.

**6\. Final Outcome**
Each research step, whether coding, writing, or literature search, creates a specific research product (e.g., code, text, references). These products are refined through rule-based and LLM-based reviews until they form a complete research paper, ready for submission.

Each step in the process is automated, but it also allows for human oversight and approval. For example, in the coding steps, the LLM generates Python code to analyze the dataset, and the platform executes this code to produce numerical outputs, such as tables for the paper. In the literature search steps, the LLM creates structured queries, retrieves citations from external databases, and incorporates them into the research.

Finally, all the intermediate outputs like citations, data tables, or sections of the manuscript are automatically compiled into a cohesive research paper. For transparency, an AI-created watermark is added to ensure that it’s clear which parts were generated autonomously.

In essence, the data-to-paper workflow uses a combination of human input, automated LLM-driven tasks, and programmatic tools to streamline the research process, helping researchers automate various stages from coding and writing to reviewing literature, culminating in the creation of a well-structured research paper.

## So, How good it is on Creating Research Papers?

In open-goal, autopilot mode, the “data-to-paper” platform was tested using two publicly available datasets:

1. The **Health Indicators dataset** , a subset of the CDC’s 2015 Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System (BRFSS), which includes 253,680 responses with 22 features related to diabetes and general health.
2. The **Social Network dataset** , a directed graph of Twitter interactions between members of the 117th U.S. Congress, with additional data on party, chamber, and state affiliations.

The “data-to-paper” platform successfully completed five research cycles for each dataset, resulting in the creation of 10 unique research papers. Each cycle took approximately an hour to complete. These cycles showcased the platform’s capability to independently generate and test hypotheses, write and debug code, conduct literature searches, and draft and revise sections of a research paper.

The manuscripts generated by the platform followed the standard structure of a scientific paper. They included a clear title and abstract, an introduction that highlighted the research questions within the context of relevant literature, a transparent methods section outlining the analysis and methodologies used, supplementary sections containing custom-written code, properly formatted scientific tables, a results section linking the findings to the analysis tables, and a discussion summarizing the results, acknowledging limitations, and connecting the findings to the broader literature.

While the overall structure of the papers remained consistent, each paper addressed different hypotheses. These hypotheses were tested using statistical methods such as logistic regression, linear regression, ANOVA, or Chi-square tests.

> Research papers created AI, while not highly creative, effectively proposed and tested reasonable hypotheses using straightforward statistical approaches. As a result, they generated new insights from the datasets.

> Upon manual review, 8 out of the 10 generated papers were found to contain accurate analyses with minor wording issues.

However, two papers displayed significant errors:

* One paper from the **Health Indicators** dataset misinterpreted the analysis due to goal-setting errors, leading to conclusions that extended beyond the scope of the data.
* One paper from the **Social Network** dataset performed an incorrect analysis, falsely reporting statistical associations between social interactions and party affiliations.

Despite these issues, the generated tables in all papers accurately represented the analytical results. The platform demonstrated an ability to correctly interpret results, cite key figures from the analysis, and frame the research question and findings within the context of existing literature. However, some limitations were observed, including generic phrasing, overstating novelty, and inadequate citation choices.

## Closing thoughts:

Although “data-to-paper” autonomously completed the research cycles, the review emphasized the importance of human oversight for more intricate tasks. This ensures that the research insights maintain accuracy and depth, taking into account the complexities and nuances that may arise in the research process. Human involvement guarantees a thorough examination of the results and helps to enhance the overall quality of the research.

AI can be a valuable asset for researchers, providing them with new insights and a vast amount of information in an instant. These AI models are trained on human-generated text, allowing them to mimic human writing style and produce coherent paragraphs or even entire sections of a research paper within seconds. This saves researchers and students a significant amount of time and streamlines the peer review process.

> It’s both captivating and concerning how closely AI can imitate human work.

However, it’s important to remember that AI is still a machine and lacks the ability to fully grasp the intricacies and complexities of a research topic. AI-based models may miss important contexts, leading to misrepresentations or even the creation of entirely fabricated research. This could result in a surge of falsified studies, causing a nightmare for editors. While there are AI detection tools available to identify whether text is human-generated or AI-generated, there are also paraphrasing tools online that can bypass this detection.

> When using AI-based tools for writing research papers, there are three key considerations: ethical and moral concerns, critical thinking and rational analysis, and the question of originality.

The ethical concerns arise from potential bias and a lack of transparency in the use of these tools. One possible solution is for authors to acknowledge their use of AI tools. It’s important to remember that these tools are meant to assist, not replace, critical thinking and rational analysis necessary for research papers. Lastly, there’s the question of originality.

**_If most parts of a research paper are generated with AI assistance, what can be considered truly original?_**
