Enterprises are quickly adopting large language models (LLMs) like GPT-4, Claude 3, and Gemini Ultra to improve their applications. These models are flexible and unpredictable, making it hard to evaluate their performance.

LLM benchmarks are standardised tools that help compare the abilities of different LLMs in key language tasks. Understanding these benchmarks, and how they assess skills like answering questions, logical reasoning, and code generation, is essential for choosing the right LLM.

## What Are LLM Benchmarks?

LLM benchmarks are designed to test the capabilities of language models in a consistent and objective way. They usually consist of:

* **A Dataset of Test Examples:** This is a collection of text samples that the models will be tested on.
* **Specific Tasks or Questions:** These are the challenges or prompts that the models need to respond to.
* **Metrics for Scoring Model Outputs:** These are the criteria used to evaluate how well the models perform.
* **Leaderboards:** These rank the models based on their performance on the benchmark tasks.

## Types of LLM Benchmarks

## Common Sense reasoning Benchmarks:

### **HellaSwag** :

**HellaSwag** is a benchmark created in 2019 by researchers from the University of Washington and the Allen Institute for AI. It tests how well AI systems can understand commonsense language, and is more challenging than earlier benchmarks like SWAG. The name HellaSwag stands for “Harder Endings, Longer contexts, and Low-shot Activities for Situations With Adversarial Generations,” emphasizing complex scenarios and challenging incorrect answers.

**How It Works** :

* Contains around 70,000 multiple-choice questions based on video captions.
* Each question provides a context and four possible next events, with only one correct answer.
* Humans score over 95% on these questions, while initial AI models scored around 48%.

**Dataset Structure** :

* 70,000 multiple-choice questions.
* Each question includes a context and four possible next events, with only one being correct based on commonsense reasoning.

**References** : [[Paper](<https://paperswithcode.com/dataset/hellaswag>)] | [[Dataset](<https://huggingface.co/datasets/Rowan/hellaswag>)]

### ARC Challenge:

**ARC Challenge** is a benchmark created in 2019 by François Chollet from Google. It is designed to test AI’s abstract reasoning and ability to generalize, focusing on how well AI can apply learned rules to new situations.

**How It Works** :

* Tasks involve grids of colored squares.
* The AI must figure out the pattern or rule that maps input grids to output grids based on a few examples.
* These tasks are easy for humans using basic knowledge and intelligence but very difficult for AI, with top models scoring around 20%.

**Dataset Structure** :

* 400 tasks for testing and another 400 for training and development.
* Tasks involve figuring out patterns or rules based on a few example pairs of input and output grids.

**Purpose** : The ARC Challenge aims to measure AI’s general intelligence and ability to generalize from limited data, providing insights into the limitations of current AI models and highlighting areas for improvement in abstract reasoning and learning.

**References** : [[Paper](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.05457>)] | [[Dataset](<https://huggingface.co/datasets/allenai/ai2_arc>)]

### CommonsenseQA:

**CommonsenseQA** is a dataset with 12,247 multiple-choice questions designed to test AI’s ability to understand and reason about everyday situations using general knowledge. Instead of relying on specific contexts, these questions require AI to apply commonsense reasoning.

**How It’s Made** :

* Concepts are extracted from ConceptNet, a knowledge graph of commonsense facts.
* Each question involves a source concept and several target concepts with similar meanings.
* Crowdworkers write questions using the source concept, requiring commonsense reasoning to choose the correct answer from the target concepts.

**Challenge** :

* Questions involve complex reasoning and cover various topics like object properties, causes and effects, human behaviors, and social conventions.
* Humans score around 89% accuracy, while top AI models initially scored about 56%.

**Dataset Structure:**

* 12,247 questions: 9,741 for training, 1,221 for validation, and 1,140 for testing.
* Each question has one correct answer and four incorrect options.

**References** : [[Paper](<https://paperswithcode.com/dataset/commonsenseqa>)] | [[Dataset](<https://huggingface.co/datasets/tau/commonsense_qa>)]

### WinoGrande:

**WinoGrande** is a large dataset with 44,000 problems to test AI’s commonsense reasoning, particularly in resolving pronouns in sentences. Created in 2019 by researchers from the Allen Institute for AI and the University of Washington, it builds on the Winograd Schema Challenge (WSC) but is larger and more difficult.

**How It Works** :

* Problems are presented as sentences with pronouns needing resolution.
* Example: “The trophy doesn’t fit in the suitcase because it is too large. What is too large? Answer 0: the trophy, Answer 1: the suitcase.” Correct answer requires commonsense knowledge.

**Dataset Structure** :

* 44,000 problems, with training sets of various sizes (160 to 40,398 examples), a validation set (1,267 examples), and a test set (1,767 examples).
* Tasks range across social contexts, physical properties, and abstract ideas, requiring deep language understanding.

**References** : [[Paper](<https://paperswithcode.com/dataset/winogrande>)] | [[Dataset](<https://huggingface.co/datasets/allenai/winogrande>)]

## Reasoning over text:

### DROP: A Reading Comprehension and Discrete Reasoning Benchmark

**DROP** is a benchmark introduced in 2019 that evaluates AI models on their ability to extract key details from English-language paragraphs and perform reasoning tasks such as adding, sorting, or counting to find the correct answer.

**How It Works:**
\- AI models must read and comprehend paragraphs.
\- They then perform discrete reasoning actions like addition, sorting, or counting to answer questions based on the text.

**Dataset Structure:**
\- Includes a variety of questions that require different types of reasoning.
\- More details and access to the dataset can be found [[here](<https://huggingface.co/datasets/drop>)]

**References** : [[Paper](<https://paperswithcode.com/dataset/drop>)] | [[Dataset](<https://huggingface.co/datasets/ucinlp/drop>)]

### BIG-Bench Hard (BBH): A Subset of BIG-Bench for Multi-step Reasoning

BIG-Bench Hard (BBH) is a subset of the BIG-Bench evaluation suite, focusing on tasks that require multi-step reasoning. This benchmark is designed to evaluate the deeper capabilities of language models, particularly in logical deduction, arithmetic, and commonsense reasoning.

**How It Works:**
\- Consists of 23 tasks that require multi-step reasoning.
\- Evaluates performance through few-shot prompting without Chain-of-Thought (CoT).

**Dataset Structure:**
\- Contains 23 diverse tasks.
\- Tasks span logical deduction, arithmetic, and commonsense reasoning.
\- More information can be found [[here](<https://paperswithcode.com/dataset/bbh>)]

**References** : [[Paper](<https://paperswithcode.com/dataset/bbh>)] | [[Dataset](<https://github.com/suzgunmirac/big-bench-hard>)]

## Math Benchmarks:

### MATH: Arithmetic Reasoning Benchmark

**MATH** is a benchmark introduced in 2021, consisting of 12,500 challenging competition mathematics problems. Each problem includes a full step-by-step solution to teach models how to derive answers and provide explanations.

**How It Works:**
\- AI models solve complex mathematical problems.
\- Problems come with detailed step-by-step solutions to guide answer derivation and explanation generation.

**Dataset Structure:**
\- 12,500 competition-level mathematics problems.
\- Problems include full solutions for teaching and evaluation purposes.
\- More details and access to the dataset can be found [[here](<https://github.com/hendrycks/math/>)]

**References** : [[Paper](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.03874v2.pdf>)]| [[Dataset](<https://github.com/hendrycks/math/>)]

### GSM8K: Grade School Math

**GSM8K** is a dataset released in 2021, containing 8,500 high-quality, linguistically diverse grade school math word problems. These problems require 2 to 8 steps to solve and primarily involve elementary arithmetic operations.

**How It Works:**
\- AI models solve grade school-level math word problems.
\- Problems require performing a sequence of basic arithmetic operations (+, -, *, /) to arrive at the final answer.

**Dataset Structure:**
\- 8,500 grade school math word problems.
\- Problems require 2 to 8 steps involving basic arithmetic to solve.
\- More details and access to the dataset can be found [[here](<https://github.com/openai/grade-school-math?tab=readme-ov-file>)]

**References** : [[Paper](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.14168.pdf>)] | [[Dataset](<https://github.com/openai/grade-school-math?tab=readme-ov-file>)]

## Graduate and Under Graduate level reasoning Benchmarks

### GPQA: A Graduate-Level Google-Proof Q&A Benchmark

**GPQA** is a challenging benchmark introduced to evaluate the capabilities of large language models (LLMs) and scalable oversight mechanisms. It comprises 448 multiple-choice questions across biology, physics, and chemistry, crafted by domain experts to ensure high quality and difficulty.

**How It Works:**
\- AI models answer multiple-choice questions at a graduate level.
\- Questions are designed to be “Google-proof,” meaning they cannot be easily answered by a simple web search.

**Dataset Structure:**
\- 448 multiple-choice questions.
\- Covers three main domains: biology, physics, and chemistry.
\- Designed to be challenging for advanced AI models.

### MMLU: Measuring Massive Multitask Language Understanding (Under graduate level knowledge)

**MMLU** is a benchmark designed to measure the multitask accuracy of AI models across 57 tasks, including elementary mathematics, US history, computer science, and law, ranging from elementary to advanced professional levels.

**How It Works:**
\- AI models are evaluated on a wide range of tasks without task-specific fine-tuning.
\- Focuses on zero-shot and few-shot evaluation approaches.

**Dataset Structure:**
\- 57 tasks covering various domains and levels of difficulty.
\- Tasks range from elementary mathematics to advanced professional knowledge.
\- More details and access to the dataset can be found [[here](<https://github.com/hendrycks/test>)]

**References** : [[Paper](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.03300.pdf>)]| [[Dataset](<https://github.com/hendrycks/test>)]
